A Moroccan investor researching capital gains tax will usually find one headline figure: 15%. What that number typically does not mention is that it applies to shares listed on the Casablanca Stock Exchange specifically - gains on securities held abroad, US stocks included, are taxed at a higher **20%**. Foreign dividends carry their own quirk: no withholding at source at all, with the reporting obligation shifted entirely onto you.

This guide covers what you can buy, the treaty's dividend rate, the real capital gains rate for US holdings, and the self-declaration requirement for dividends.

Everything here is verified from official sources for 2026.

## Step 1: What You Can Actually Buy

Morocco is not in the EU or EEA, so the PRIIPs restriction that blocks European residents from buying US-domiciled ETFs does not apply here. **Yassine can buy VOO, VTI, and QQQ directly** through a broker offering US market access.

## Step 2: The Treaty - 15% on Dividends

The US-Morocco tax treaty (1977) caps US withholding on dividends paid to Moroccan resident individuals at **15%** for portfolio investors, claimed with Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/).

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

Without W-8BEN on file, the full 30% applies from the first dividend.

For the full treaty mechanics, see our [US-Morocco tax treaty guide](/taxes/us-morocco-tax-treaty-investors/).

## Step 3: Capital Gains - 20% on US Stocks, Not the 15% You'll See First

Morocco's Tax on Capital Gains from Securities (TPCVM) applies **15%** to gains from selling Casablanca Stock Exchange-listed shares - the figure most general tax summaries lead with. **Gains from disposing of securities held abroad are taxed at 20%** instead, a materially higher rate that applies directly to Yassine's US stock sales.

**Why the distinction exists:** Morocco's securities tax regime was built with the domestic Casablanca exchange as its reference point. The higher rate on foreign-held securities reflects that these disposals sit outside the domestic market's reporting and withholding infrastructure, placing the compliance burden and a higher rate on the investor instead.

**The practical takeaway:** when Yassine compares "Morocco taxes stock gains at 15%" against his actual US brokerage statements, he needs to use 20%, not 15% - a 5-point gap easy to miss if research stops at the first figure found.

---

## Step 4: Dividends and Interest - 15% Flat, But You Self-Declare

For dividends and interest sourced abroad, Morocco applies a flat **15%** rate - matching the treaty rate coincidentally, but the mechanism is different from what a domestic investor is used to. **There is no prior withholding on foreign-source income at all.** The gross dividend arrives in full; the 15% Moroccan obligation is not collected automatically.

**The obligation shifts to Yassine:** foreign-source dividend and interest income must be declared on his annual Moroccan income tax return, with the 15% computed and paid based on that declaration - an active self-reporting requirement, not a passive one.

**Why this self-declaration model creates real, practical risk beyond just remembering to file:** unlike a domestic Casablanca-listed dividend, where the paying institution handles withholding and reporting automatically, a foreign dividend arriving in full, with nothing withheld, can feel to an inexperienced investor like it's simply "his money" with no further obligation attached - a genuinely easy mistake to make precisely because nothing on the statement itself signals that a Moroccan tax liability exists. Building a habit of treating every foreign dividend as provisionally 15% Morocco's from the moment it arrives - setting that portion aside mentally or literally - avoids the unpleasant surprise of a larger-than-expected bill at annual filing time.

Confirm directly with Office des Changes or a Moroccan financial advisor what limits currently apply before transferring meaningful funds abroad for this purpose.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Assuming the 15% Casablanca capital gains rate applies to US stocks.** It does not - foreign-held securities are taxed at 20% under TPCVM.

**Forgetting to self-declare foreign dividends.** They arrive with no Moroccan withholding, and the reporting obligation is entirely yours.

**Assuming an unlimited or specific transfer allowance for brokerage funding.** Confirm the current rules with Office des Changes rather than guessing.

**Forgetting W-8BEN.** Without it, 30% withholding applies instead of the treaty's 15%.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Morocco tax my US stock gains at 15%, like Casablanca-listed shares?**
No. Gains on securities held abroad, including US stocks, are taxed at 20% under Morocco's TPCVM - a higher rate than the 15% that applies specifically to Casablanca Stock Exchange-listed shares.

**Will my broker withhold Moroccan tax on my US dividends?**
No. Foreign-source dividends arrive with no Moroccan withholding at all. You must self-declare them on your annual return and pay the 15% based on that declaration.

**What's the US withholding rate on my dividends?**
15%, under the US-Morocco tax treaty, once Form W-8BEN is filed. Without it, the default 30% applies.

**How much can I transfer abroad to fund a US brokerage account?**
This guide could not confirm a specific allowance dedicated to brokerage funding - confirm current limits directly with Office des Changes or a Moroccan financial advisor before transferring meaningful funds.

**Can I buy VOO and QQQ directly as a Moroccan investor?**
Yes. Morocco is not subject to the EU's PRIIPs regulation, so any US-listed stock or ETF your broker offers is directly accessible.
