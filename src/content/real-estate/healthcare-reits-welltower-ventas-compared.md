Our [net lease REIT guide](/real-estate/net-lease-reits-realty-income-explained/) covers Realty Income and NNN REIT as landlords whose entire income is a fixed, contractual rent check - stable, predictable, and mostly disconnected from how well the underlying business inside the building is actually doing. Welltower, the largest healthcare REIT by market cap, deliberately breaks that model for a meaningful chunk of its own portfolio, and understanding why explains almost everything about how differently it behaves as an investment.

---

## The Rule That Normally Stops This: REITs Can't Operate Businesses

**A standard REIT is legally barred from directly operating a business inside the properties it owns** - that's precisely what makes it a REIT rather than an operating company, and it's the same structural logic behind Realty Income's tenants handling all their own operational costs under a triple net lease. For hospitals, senior living communities, and skilled nursing facilities, though, Congress created a specific carve-out: RIDEA, the REIT Investment Diversification and Empowerment Act of 2007.

**Under RIDEA, a healthcare REIT can lease a property to its own taxable REIT subsidiary (TRS)** - a fully taxable corporate entity the REIT is allowed to own - and that subsidiary then hires an independent third-party operator to actually run the facility day to day. The REIT still isn't directly operating the business, satisfying the letter of the rule, but through the TRS structure it now participates in the property's actual operating economics - occupancy rates, staffing costs, resident fees - rather than collecting a fixed rent check regardless of how full or empty the building is.

---

## Why This Makes Welltower Look Nothing Like Realty Income

**Welltower's Seniors Housing Operating Portfolio (SHOP) segment, built on exactly this RIDEA structure, accounts for 66.1% of the company's net operating income** across more than 1,800 properties - meaning most of Welltower's business is genuinely operating-sensitive, not fixed-rent, unlike the net lease REITs covered elsewhere on this site. The upside has been real: SHOP segment NOI grew 20.5% year over year going into 2026, a growth rate no fixed-rent net lease portfolio could plausibly deliver, since a net lease landlord's rent only rises by whatever the lease's built-in escalator specifies, not by how much better the underlying business performs.

**That operating exposure shows up directly in the yield, and it's the opposite direction from what a first-time REIT investor might expect.** Welltower's dividend, even after a 15% increase to $0.85 per share in 2026, yields somewhere in the low-single-digit percentage range - meaningfully below Realty Income's roughly 5% yield covered in our net lease guide. This isn't Welltower being a worse income investment; it's a structurally different one. A company retaining more cash to fund growth and absorbing real operating variability pays a lower, more growth-oriented yield than a company whose entire model is passing through a fixed, contractual rent stream with minimal reinvestment need.

---

## Ventas: A More Traditional Blend

**Ventas takes a more mixed approach** - a portfolio spanning senior housing communities, medical office buildings, hospitals, and life science facilities, combining both RIDEA-style operating exposure and more traditional net-lease-style contracts depending on the property type. The company has paid a dividend for 28 consecutive years, with a current yield reported around 2.35% as of recent data, though yield figures for Ventas have varied noticeably across sources and time periods as the company has adjusted its payout following the senior housing sector's post-pandemic recovery - worth confirming directly with current data before relying on any single cited figure. With a market capitalization around $43 billion and revenue growth of roughly 21% in recent results, Ventas sits as a genuine scale peer to Welltower, just with a somewhat more diversified property mix and a payout policy that has been more conservative through the sector's recovery.

---

## The Demographic Case Underneath Both Companies

**The investment thesis for healthcare REITs generally, and senior housing operators specifically, rests on a real, well-documented demographic trend, and the numbers behind it are specific rather than a vague "aging population" gesture.** The US population aged 80 and older is projected to grow roughly 36.6% over the 2025-to-2035 decade, against total US population growth of only around 5% over the same period - the oldest baby boomers turn 80 in 2026, marking the start of that surge working through the system. Supply hasn't kept pace: quarterly senior housing construction starts fell to roughly 1,076 units in the first quarter of 2025, the lowest level recorded since the second quarter of 2009, constrained by higher construction-lending rates and an average build cycle of about 29 months - meaning any project started in 2026 doesn't open until 2028 at the earliest. Industry estimates put the shortfall at roughly 549,000 additional senior housing units needed by 2028 and 806,000 by 2030, against a delivery pace tracking at only about a third of that pace.

**Existing operators - the properties Welltower and Ventas already own, not hypothetical future supply - benefit directly from that imbalance.** Occupancy across the sector is projected to climb above 90% in 2026, potentially the highest rate recorded in the 20 years industry tracker NIC MAP has followed the data, and for RIDEA-structured properties specifically, rising occupancy translates directly into rising per-resident fee revenue rather than sitting outside a fixed rent contract. That's the operating-level mechanism behind Welltower's 20.5% SHOP NOI growth - a real supply-demand story with specific, current numbers behind it, not a one-time or accounting-driven figure.

## Dividend Withholding and the Same Rules That Apply Elsewhere

**Welltower and Ventas are both US-domiciled REITs subject to the identical NRA dividend withholding framework** covered throughout this site's [taxes category](/taxes/) - the standard treaty rate or 30% default, unaffected by whether the underlying income came from a fixed net lease rent check or a RIDEA-structured operating property. The distinction covered in this article is about yield size, growth character, and operating risk, not tax treatment, which stays consistent across the REIT sector regardless of the specific structure generating the income.

---

## Choosing Between the Two Models

**An investor prioritizing current income and predictability** is better served by the net lease REITs covered separately on this site - the entire point of that structure is minimizing operating variability, and healthcare REITs with heavy RIDEA exposure work directly against that goal by design. **An investor comfortable trading current yield for growth exposure to a real, multi-decade demographic trend** has a genuine case for Welltower's operating-heavy model specifically, understanding that the same structure amplifying today's occupancy-driven growth would just as directly transmit a downturn in senior housing demand or a spike in operating costs, in a way a fixed-rent net lease landlord simply wouldn't feel to the same degree.

---

## Bottom Line

Healthcare REITs aren't a single category behaving one way - Welltower's RIDEA-structured SHOP portfolio makes it genuinely participate in senior housing operating economics, delivering real growth (20.5% SHOP NOI growth) at the cost of a lower yield than a net lease REIT like Realty Income offers, while Ventas blends that same operating exposure with a more traditional, diversified healthcare property mix. The demographic tailwind underneath both is real and well-documented, but which company's structure actually captures it - and how much yield you give up to get growth exposure instead - is the real decision, not a simple "healthcare REIT" label.
