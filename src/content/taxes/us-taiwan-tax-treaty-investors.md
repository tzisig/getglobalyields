Taiwan is the **largest trading partner the United States has with no income tax treaty** - not because negotiations failed, but because the US does not maintain formal diplomatic relations with Taiwan, and traditional tax treaties require exactly that kind of government-to-government recognition. For a Taiwanese investor in US stocks, the practical result is the same as any other no-treaty country: the full **30%** US withholding applies to dividends, with no reduced rate available via [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/).

That may be changing, but hasn't yet. This guide covers the current 30% reality, the pending legislation that would fix it, and what Taiwan's own tax rules mean for the resulting investment income.

---

## Why There's No Treaty - And What H.R. 33 Would Do

Ordinary US tax treaties are negotiated government-to-government and ratified by the Senate. Because the US doesn't formally recognize Taiwan as a sovereign state, that process isn't available - so Congress has instead pursued a legislative workaround: a US law that would grant treaty-like relief without requiring a formal bilateral treaty.

**The US-Taiwan Expedited Double-Tax Relief Act (H.R. 33)** passed the House of Representatives on January 15, 2025, by a landslide 423-1 vote - about as close to unanimous bipartisan support as major tax legislation gets. It would authorize treaty-like withholding relief: a **15% rate on dividends** (reducible to 10% for qualifying 10%+ ownership), and 10% on most other covered income types, down from the current 30% default.

**As of this update (2026), H.R. 33 has not been enacted into law.** It cleared the House with overwhelming support but has not completed the remaining legislative steps needed to take effect. There is no guaranteed timeline for enactment. If you're researching this topic and find older or newer sources describing different rates, check the date carefully - this is a live legislative situation, not a settled one, and the correct current answer as of this update is that the 30% default still applies.

## Until Then: The 30% Default

Without treaty-like relief in force, dividends from US stocks are withheld at the full **30%** for a Taiwanese investor, regardless of how W-8BEN is completed - the form still certifies non-US person status and prevents the higher backup withholding regime from applying, but there's no reduced rate to claim yet.

**Capital gains:** the US generally does not tax capital gains realized by a non-resident alien on US securities under domestic law (IRC §871), independent of treaty status - this part of the picture is unaffected by whether H.R. 33 eventually passes.

---

## Taiwan's Side: Worldwide Income for Residents

Taiwanese tax residents are taxed on worldwide income, including foreign dividends and capital gains from US stocks, under Taiwan's income tax framework - though the interaction between Taiwan's Basic Income Tax (a form of alternative minimum tax that captures certain offshore income) and ordinary income tax rules can be fact-specific for investment income sourced outside Taiwan. Given both the pending federal legislation on the US side and the specifics of how Taiwan taxes offshore securities income, this is a case where current, personalized advice from a Taiwanese tax advisor is worth more than a general guide - the rules on both sides of this relationship are less settled than in most other countries covered in this series.

## Bottom Line

Taiwan's situation is unusual in this series: not an ordinary no-treaty country by choice or oversight, but a major economy blocked from a normal treaty by a diplomatic-recognition technicality, with bipartisan US legislation (H.R. 33) sitting one step short of fixing it. Until that changes, the 30% default withholding applies with no form-based workaround. Worth checking back on this specific guide periodically - of everything covered in this series, this is one of the situations most likely to change in the near term, and when it does, the effective rate for Taiwanese investors could improve substantially.
