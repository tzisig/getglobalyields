It is a **factual explainer, not a how-to guide** - because for Iran, the honest, accurate answer isn't a set of steps for opening and funding a US brokerage account. It's an explanation of why that generally isn't possible under current US law, and why this site won't provide guidance on working around that.

---

## Iran Is Under Comprehensive US Sanctions

Iran is one of only four countries - alongside Cuba, North Korea, and Syria - currently subject to a **comprehensive US embargo**, administered by the Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) under the Iranian Transactions and Sanctions Regulations. Unlike the *targeted* sanctions programs covered elsewhere in this series (Russia, Belarus, Venezuela, Myanmar), which restrict specific individuals, entities, or sectors, a comprehensive embargo prohibits virtually all transactions between US persons and the sanctioned country as a whole, subject to narrow, specifically authorized exceptions.

**This directly covers US brokerage services.** A "US person" for these purposes includes US-incorporated financial firms - which means Interactive Brokers, eToro's US-regulated entities, and every other US-regulated broker referenced throughout this series are themselves legally barred from opening or maintaining an account for a person in Iran, not merely reluctant to do so as a matter of business policy.

**This isn't theoretical.** Interactive Brokers - the broker most frequently mentioned across this entire series - paid an **$11.8 million settlement with OFAC** for providing brokerage and investment services to persons in Iran, Cuba, Syria, and Crimea between July 2016 and January 2024. That enforcement action illustrates concretely that this restriction is actively monitored and enforced, not a dormant rule.

---

## No Tax Treaty Either

There is no US-Iran income tax treaty. In the ordinary circumstances covered elsewhere in this series, that would mean the default 30% US withholding rate applies to dividends. For Iran, this is largely academic: the comprehensive embargo means the underlying brokerage relationship generally can't legally exist in the first place, so the treaty question doesn't reach a practical scenario for most readers.

## A Fast-Moving, Currently Restrictive Situation

The sanctions landscape around Iran has shifted even within 2026. Following a Memorandum of Understanding, OFAC briefly issued a general license (General License X) providing some sanctions relief - but **OFAC revoked that license in July 2026**, replacing it with a narrow wind-down authorization (GL X1) rather than continuing the relief. As of this writing, the restrictive baseline remains firmly in place, and the direction of recent change has been toward tightening, not easing.

## Bottom Line

There is no US-Iran tax treaty, and more fundamentally, Iran is one of only four countries under a comprehensive US sanctions embargo - a legal framework that generally prohibits US-regulated brokers, including the ones discussed throughout this series, from providing services to persons in Iran, with real enforcement history behind it. This article exists to state that clearly and factually, not to work around it. Anyone with a genuinely complex situation should consult a qualified sanctions attorney rather than rely on general guidance.
