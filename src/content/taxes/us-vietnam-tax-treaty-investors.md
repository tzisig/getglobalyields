Vietnam has signed double tax agreements with 81 countries - the United States is not one of them. For dividends from US stocks, the full **30%** default withholding applies with no treaty reduction, and Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) serves only to certify non-US status, not to reduce that rate.

More consequential for timing this article: Vietnam's **Personal Income Tax Law No. 109/2025/QH15 took effect July 1, 2026** - a genuine overhaul, not a minor update, implemented alongside Government Decree 253/2026/ND-CP and Ministry of Finance Circular 87/2026/TT-BTC. It raises personal deductions, introduces a simplified five-band tax schedule, and revises how capital transfers are taxed. What's well documented is how it treats Vietnamese company shares. What's genuinely unclear from available guidance - and this guide says so directly rather than guessing - is exactly how the new law's capital-transfer mechanics apply to a Vietnamese resident's gains on foreign-listed securities like US stocks specifically.

---

## What's Confirmed: Vietnamese Company Shares Under the New Law

For share and capital transfers involving Vietnamese entities, the new regime sets out clear, specific rates:

- **Listed or non-listed joint stock company (JSC) shares:** a flat **0.1%** of gross transfer proceeds - a turnover-based tax, not a gain-based one.
- **Limited liability company (LLC) capital contributions:** **20%** of the actual capital gain for tax residents, or **2% of gross proceeds** where the acquisition cost can't be determined.

These figures are specifically documented around Vietnamese corporate structures - a JSC and an LLC are categories under Vietnamese company law. They are not a generic "securities tax rate" that self-evidently extends to a US-listed stock bought through Interactive Brokers or a similar foreign broker.

**Personal deductions under the new law:** a resident taxpayer can claim VND 15.5 million per month (VND 186 million per year) as a personal deduction, with VND 6.2 million per month per eligible dependant - relevant to how your overall taxable income, including any investment income that is taxed on a progressive rather than flat basis, is calculated.

## What's Genuinely Unclear: US-Listed Securities

Here is the honest gap in this guide, stated directly rather than smoothed over: current published guidance on Vietnam's new Personal Income Tax Law - law firm summaries, KPMG and Baker McKenzie coverage, and general PIT explainers - documents the JSC/LLC mechanics above in detail, but does not clearly specify how a Vietnamese tax resident's capital gain on a foreign-listed security (a US stock, held through a foreign broker, with no Vietnamese JSC or LLC involved at any point) is taxed under the new regime.

**What's established, independent of that gap:** Vietnamese tax residents (present in Vietnam 183+ days in a year, or 12 consecutive months from arrival) are taxed on worldwide income, and "other income sources such as investment income and capital transfers are subject to flat tax rates as prescribed by law" - confirming that foreign investment income is in scope conceptually, without pinning down which specific flat rate applies to a foreign-brokerage capital gain that isn't a JSC or LLC transfer under Vietnamese company law.

**What to do with this uncertainty:** don't assume the 0.1% turnover-based JSC rate applies to a US stock sale just because both are "listed shares" - the JSC provision is written around Vietnamese joint stock companies specifically. Get written confirmation from a Vietnamese tax advisor on how gains from a foreign brokerage account are classified and taxed under the post-July 2026 regime before relying on any specific rate. This is a genuinely new law; authoritative guidance on this specific cross-border question may simply not be fully settled yet.

## Bottom Line

The US side is simple and fixed: no treaty, 30% withholding on dividends, nothing to optimize with a form. The Vietnamese side is genuinely in flux - a brand-new Personal Income Tax Law took effect July 1, 2026, and while it clearly defines how Vietnamese company shares are taxed, how it treats a resident's gains on foreign-listed securities like US stocks is not something this guide can state with confidence based on currently available sources. That's a real gap worth closing with a Vietnamese tax advisor before assuming any specific rate applies to your situation, not a detail to guess past.
