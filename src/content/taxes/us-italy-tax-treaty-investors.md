Italy requires reporting of foreign financial assets - a US brokerage account included - on **Quadro RW** if the total value exceeds **€5,000** at any point during the year, and the disclosure obligation for the account's existence applies even for a single foreign share worth as little as €1. This is a dramatically lower threshold than Spain's €50,000 Modelo 720 or similar reporting rules elsewhere in this series, and it's a genuinely easy requirement for a new Italian resident to miss simply because the trigger amount is so small relative to what most investors would assume needs reporting.

This guide covers Quadro RW, the **IVAFE** wealth tax on foreign financial assets it feeds into, Italy's flat 26% tax on dividends and capital gains, and the treaty's standard 15% withholding rate.

---

## Quadro RW: Reporting From the First Euro, Effectively

Quadro RW is the section of Italy's tax return where Italian tax residents declare foreign financial assets, foreign real estate, and certain other foreign holdings. **All foreign financial assets must be reported if their total value exceeds €5,000 at any point during the year** - a threshold covering bank accounts, brokerage accounts, and investments collectively, not per-asset. Reporting is required regardless of whether the assets generated any income that year.

**This applies to an ordinary US brokerage account without exception.** Even a modest position well below what most investors would think of as requiring formal foreign-asset disclosure can trigger the Quadro RW obligation once the account (combined with any other foreign financial holdings) crosses €5,000 - a threshold reached quickly by almost any actively-used investment account.

---

## IVAFE: An Annual Tax on the Account Itself

Quadro RW reporting feeds into **IVAFE** (Imposta sul Valore delle Attività Finanziarie all'Estero) - a wealth-tax-style annual levy on the value of foreign financial assets, including a US brokerage account, independent of whether the account generated any dividends or gains that year.

**The standard rate is 0.2%** of the account's value. A significant change to watch: this rate is set to **increase to 0.4%** for certain financial products under recent budget measures - confirm with a current Italian tax advisor which specific product categories the higher rate applies to for your holdings, since the increase is not uniform across all foreign financial assets.

**What this means concretely:** a US brokerage account worth €200,000 generates an IVAFE liability of roughly €400/year at the 0.2% rate (potentially higher depending on which holdings fall under the increased rate) - a real, recurring cost layered on top of the income tax described below, assessed purely on the account's value.

## Dividends and Capital Gains: A Flat 26%

Italy taxes dividends and capital gains on financial assets - including US stocks - at a flat **26% substitute tax (imposta sostitutiva)**, applied uniformly regardless of income level or holding period. This is simpler than the progressive scales used in several other countries covered in this series, though it offers no lower-bracket relief for smaller investors the way a progressive system would.

**Worked example - a $2,000 US dividend:**
- US withholding at 15% (treaty rate, W-8BEN on file): $300
- Italian imposta sostitutiva at 26% on the gross $2,000: $520
- Foreign tax credit for the US withholding already paid: -$300
- Net Italian tax owed: $220
- Total tax paid (US + Italy): $300 + $220 = $520 - Italy's 26% flat rate applied once, no double taxation

## The Treaty: 15% on Dividends

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Italy Income Tax Convention; IRS treaty text; PwC Italy Tax Summaries (2026).*

Claiming the 15% rate requires Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) filed with your broker. Without it, the full 30% default applies from the first dividend payment.

## Broker Access

Both Interactive Brokers and eToro are confirmed to accept Italian residents for account opening. Neither changes the Quadro RW reporting obligation or the IVAFE calculation, both of which are triggered by account value and holdings regardless of which broker holds them.

## Practical Checklist

**Italian resident investing in US stocks:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] File Quadro RW if your total foreign financial assets exceed €5,000 at any point in the year - a low threshold most active investors will cross
- [ ] Budget for IVAFE at 0.2% (or 0.4% for certain financial products under recent changes) of your foreign account value annually, regardless of investment performance
- [ ] Report dividends and capital gains at the flat 26% imposta sostitutiva rate
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for the 15% US withholding against the 26% Italian liability on dividends
- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro before applying

## Bottom Line

The treaty rate is standard - 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN. What makes Italy distinctive among countries in this series is the combination of a very low reporting threshold (Quadro RW at €5,000, far below Spain's €50,000 or similar rules elsewhere) with IVAFE, an annual wealth-style tax on the account's value independent of performance, on top of a flat 26% tax on the dividends and gains themselves. None of these three layers is optional or avoidable for a genuine Italian tax resident holding US stocks - all three need to be planned around together, not just the headline dividend rate.
