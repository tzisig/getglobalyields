Italy requires reporting of foreign financial assets - a US brokerage account included - on **Quadro RW**, and for securities there is **no de minimis at all**: a single foreign share worth €1 is reportable. Law 97/2013 removed the old monetary floor, so the €5,000 figure still quoted in many guides no longer gates the securities reporting obligation; it survives in a different rule, as an exemption from the *IVAFE* wealth tax on foreign **deposit and current accounts**, which is where the confusion comes from. Compared with Spain's €50,000 Modelo 720 this is dramatically stricter, and it is a genuinely easy requirement for a new Italian resident to miss.

This guide covers Quadro RW, the **IVAFE** wealth tax on foreign financial assets it feeds into, Italy's flat 26% tax on dividends and capital gains, and the treaty's standard 15% withholding rate.

---

## Quadro RW: Reporting From the First Euro, Effectively

Quadro RW is the section of Italy's tax return where Italian tax residents declare foreign financial assets, foreign real estate, and certain other foreign holdings. **All foreign financial assets held for investment must be reported regardless of value** - there is no aggregate threshold to clear first, and no per-asset floor. Reporting is required regardless of whether the assets generated any income that year.

**This applies to an ordinary US brokerage account without exception.** A position far below what most investors would think of as requiring formal foreign-asset disclosure still triggers the Quadro RW obligation, because the obligation does not depend on size. Opening the account and buying one share is enough.

---

## IVAFE: An Annual Tax on the Account Itself

Quadro RW reporting feeds into **IVAFE** (Imposta sul Valore delle Attività Finanziarie all'Estero) - a wealth-tax-style annual levy on the value of foreign financial assets, including a US brokerage account, independent of whether the account generated any dividends or gains that year.

**The standard rate is 0.2%** of the account's value. A significant change to watch: this rate is set to **increase to 0.4%** for certain financial products under recent budget measures - confirm with a current Italian tax advisor which specific product categories the higher rate applies to for your holdings, since the increase is not uniform across all foreign financial assets.

**What this means concretely:** a US brokerage account worth €200,000 generates an IVAFE liability of roughly €400/year at the 0.2% rate (potentially higher depending on which holdings fall under the increased rate) - a real, recurring cost layered on top of the income tax described below, assessed purely on the account's value.

## Dividends and Capital Gains: A Flat 26%

Italy taxes dividends and capital gains on financial assets - including US stocks - at a flat **26% substitute tax (imposta sostitutiva)**, applied uniformly regardless of income level or holding period. This is simpler than the progressive scales used in several other countries covered in this series, though it offers no lower-bracket relief for smaller investors the way a progressive system would.

**Worked example - a $3,400 US dividend:**
- US withholding at 15% (treaty rate, W-8BEN on file): $510
- Italian imposta sostitutiva at 26% on the gross $3,400: $884
- Foreign tax credit for the US withholding already paid: -$510
- Net Italian tax owed: $374
- Total: $884, which is exactly 26% of the dividend. The US withholding is not an extra layer here - it is a prepayment of the Italian bill, and the credit hands the rest to Rome.

---

## 15% on Dividends Under the Italy Treaty

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Italy Income Tax Convention; IRS treaty text; PwC Italy Tax Summaries (2026).*

The treaty sets the rate; W-8BEN is what delivers it to you. Whether you pay the default or the treaty rate comes down to that one form.

---

## Onboarding from Italy

Italian residents can open accounts with both Interactive Brokers and eToro. Neither changes the Quadro RW reporting obligation or the IVAFE calculation, both of which are triggered by account value and holdings regardless of which broker holds them.

---

## The Short List for Italy Investors

**Holding US stocks from Italy - cover these:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] File Quadro RW for a US brokerage account regardless of its size - securities carry no de minimis, so there is no balance small enough to skip the filing
- [ ] Budget for IVAFE at 0.2% (or 0.4% for certain financial products under recent changes) of your foreign account value annually, regardless of investment performance
- [ ] Report dividends and capital gains at the flat 26% imposta sostitutiva rate
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for the 15% US withholding against the 26% Italian liability on dividends
- [ ] Verify current onboarding requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro yourself

## The Italy Picture in Short

The treaty rate is standard - 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN. What makes Italy distinctive among countries in this series is the combination of the absence of any reporting threshold at all (Quadro RW applies to securities from the first euro, where Spain only asks at €50,000) with IVAFE, an annual wealth-style tax on the account's value independent of performance, on top of a flat 26% tax on the dividends and gains themselves. None of these three layers is optional or avoidable for a genuine Italian tax resident holding US stocks - all three need to be planned around together, not just the headline dividend rate.
