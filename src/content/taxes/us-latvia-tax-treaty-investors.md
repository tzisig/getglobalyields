Latvia taxes capital gains from selling shares - US stocks included - at a flat **25.5%**, one of the higher flat rates covered in this series, with an additional **3%** surcharge on the portion of total annual income above **€200,000**. Latvia does have a 0% personal dividend rate, but it applies specifically to dividends already subject to Latvian corporate income tax or personal income tax - a condition built around Latvia's domestic corporate tax system that doesn't straightforwardly extend to a foreign dividend like one from a US company, where no Latvian tax was withheld at the corporate level in the first place.

This guide covers both numbers clearly, alongside the treaty's standard 15% dividend withholding rate.

---

## Capital Gains: A Flat 25.5%, Plus a Surcharge Above €200,000

Latvia's personal income tax on capital income - including capital gains from disposing of shares - is a flat **25.5%**, applied to gains from selling US stocks the same as gains from any other capital asset. There's no holding-period exemption reducing this rate the way several other countries in this series offer.

**The high-income surcharge:** an additional **3%** PIT applies to the portion of an individual's total annual income - not just investment income, but overall income - that exceeds **€200,000**. For a high earner with a large capital gain in the same year as substantial other income, this can push the effective rate on the gain to **28.5%** for the portion above that combined threshold.

---

## Dividends: Why the 0% Domestic Rate Likely Doesn't Apply to US Dividends

This is the detail most likely to be misunderstood by a Latvian investor researching this topic casually.

**Latvia's 0% dividend rate** applies specifically to dividends paid from profits on which Latvian corporate income tax (or personal income tax) has already been withheld - a mechanism designed to avoid taxing the same corporate profit twice within Latvia's own tax system (Latvia has used a 0%-on-retained-earnings, tax-on-distribution corporate model since 2018). **Dividends that don't meet this condition are taxed at the standard 25.5%.**

**For a US dividend specifically:** the underlying profit was subject to US corporate tax, not Latvian corporate tax - Latvia's domestic "already-taxed-in-Latvia" condition for the 0% rate isn't naturally satisfied by a foreign company's dividend. The more likely outcome is that US dividends fall under Latvia's standard 25.5% rate for "other dividends" rather than the domestic 0% exemption, though the exact classification and any treaty-specific nuance is worth confirming directly with a Latvian tax advisor rather than assuming either outcome from general descriptions of the 0% rate, which are usually written with domestic Latvian corporate distributions in mind.

## Halving the Withholding: Latvia's 15%

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Latvia Income Tax Convention; IRS treaty text; PwC Latvia Tax Summaries (2026).*

A filed W-8BEN is the only thing standing between you and the default rate. Leave it unfiled and the full 30% applies from the outset. Assuming the standard 25.5% domestic rate applies (per the discussion above), the foreign tax credit for the 15% US withholding would offset part of that liability, leaving roughly 10.5 percentage points of additional Latvian tax due on the same dividend - confirm this calculation with a Latvian tax advisor given the classification uncertainty noted above.

## What a Latvian Investor Should Actually Do

**Latvian resident, US portfolio - the short list:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Budget for Latvia's flat 25.5% capital gains rate on US stock disposals, with no holding-period exemption available
- [ ] Track total annual income against the €200,000 threshold for the additional 3% surcharge
- [ ] Confirm with a Latvian tax advisor whether your US dividends qualify for the 0% domestic rate or fall under the standard 25.5% - don't assume the 0% rate applies without checking, since it's built around Latvia's own corporate tax mechanism
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for the 15% US withholding against whichever Latvian dividend rate applies to your situation
- [ ] Establish what the broker requires from a Latvian resident before opening the application

## Where Latvia Investors Stand

The treaty rate is standard - 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN. The Latvian side carries two things worth getting right: a flat 25.5% capital gains rate (28.5% above the €200,000 income surcharge threshold) with no long-term holding relief, and a dividend classification question where the well-known 0% Latvian dividend rate is built around domestic corporate tax mechanics that don't obviously extend to a foreign company's dividend - confirm which rate actually applies to your US dividends before assuming the more favorable one does.
