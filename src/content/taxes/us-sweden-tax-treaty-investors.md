Most Swedish investors hold their portfolios inside an **ISK** (Investeringssparkonto) - a structure that replaces ordinary Swedish capital gains and dividend tax with a single flat annual levy based on a government-set deemed yield, regardless of what your holdings actually returned. For 2026, that works out to roughly **1.065%** of your account's average value, calculated from a formula tied to the government borrowing rate. What an ISK does **not** do is eliminate the US withholding on dividends from US stocks - that still applies at source under the treaty, separately from whatever the ISK charges on the Swedish side.

This guide covers the treaty's standard 15% dividend rate and how it interacts with the ISK's flat-tax mechanics, since the two operate independently rather than one replacing the other.

---

## The ISK: A Flat Tax on Deemed Value, Not Actual Returns

The ISK replaces Sweden's ordinary capital gains and dividend taxation with a **schablonskatt** (presumed-yield tax): instead of taxing your actual realized gains and dividends, Sweden taxes a formula-based estimate of what your account should have earned, applied to your account's average value across the year.

**How the calculation works for 2026:** the deemed yield (schablonintäkt) is based on the government borrowing rate plus one percentage point (2.55% + 1.00% = 3.55% for 2026), taxed at a flat 30% - producing an effective rate of roughly **1.065%** of your account's average capital base for the year. The capital base itself is calculated from quarterly average balances plus a portion of deposits made during the year, not simply the year-end value.

**A new tax-free threshold is coming, but not yet:** a 2026 reform introduces a **SEK 300,000 tax-free threshold**, but it takes effect starting **tax year 2027**, not immediately. Through the 2026 tax year, plan around the ISK's flat rate applying from the first krona of capital base, without that allowance yet in effect.

**Buying and selling inside the ISK is genuinely free of transaction-level tax** - there's no capital gains event to report when you trade within the account, which is the ISK's real simplification value relative to a standard taxable account requiring K4 form reporting for every disposal.

## What the ISK Doesn't Change: US Withholding Still Applies

This is the detail worth being explicit about, since it's easy to assume a flat-tax wrapper eliminates every other tax layer the way some accounts elsewhere in this series do.

**US dividends held inside an ISK are still subject to US withholding at source**, the same 15% treaty rate (with W-8BEN filed) that applies to a standard taxable Swedish account. The ISK's schablonskatt is a Swedish-side calculation layered on top of your account's overall value - it doesn't reach back and cancel the US withholding that already happened before the dividend landed in your account. Treaty relief for the US withholding is claimed through the normal W-8BEN process with your broker, separately and independently from how the ISK taxes your account overall.

**The practical picture:** an ISK simplifies Swedish-side reporting and taxes your account at a flat, often modest rate regardless of actual performance - a real advantage in a strong year, a real cost in a flat or down year, similar in spirit to the deemed-return systems covered for [Denmark's aktiesparekonto](/taxes/us-denmark-tax-treaty-investors/) and the [Netherlands' Box 3](/taxes/us-netherlands-tax-treaty-investors/) elsewhere in this series. But the 15% US withholding on dividends from US-listed stocks happens regardless of which Swedish account structure holds them.

## The Treaty: 15% on Dividends

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Sweden Income Tax Convention; IRS treaty text; Skatteverket and PwC Sweden guidance (2026).*

Claiming the 15% rate requires Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) filed with your broker, whether the account is a standard taxable account or an ISK - the form and the treaty claim work the same way regardless of the Swedish account wrapper holding the position.

## Broker Access

Both Interactive Brokers and eToro are confirmed to accept Swedish residents for account opening. Confirm whether your chosen broker supports ISK-equivalent account structures specifically, since not every international broker offers a Swedish ISK wrapper - some Swedish investors use IBKR or similar platforms for a standard taxable account instead, while keeping ISK holdings with a Swedish or Nordic broker that supports the structure directly.

## Practical Checklist

**Swedish resident investing in US stocks:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements - this applies the same way inside or outside an ISK
- [ ] Understand the ISK's schablonskatt (~1.065% effective for 2026) applies to your account's average value regardless of actual performance, and does not replace or reduce the US withholding
- [ ] Track the SEK 300,000 tax-free threshold taking effect from tax year 2027 - not yet in force for 2026
- [ ] If using a standard taxable account instead of an ISK, report actual capital gains and dividends via the K4 form as usual, with the foreign tax credit for US withholding
- [ ] Confirm your broker supports the ISK structure if that's your intended account type, since not all international brokers offer it
- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro before applying

## Bottom Line

The treaty rate is standard - 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN regardless of which Swedish account holds the position. The ISK's flat, deemed-yield tax is a genuine simplification of Swedish-side reporting and can be favorable in strong years, but it operates independently of and does not reduce the US withholding on dividends - the two tax layers apply on separate tracks, not as a single combined calculation.
