If you're researching Portugal because of its Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) tax regime's reputation for favorable treatment of foreign investment income, the first thing to know is that **NHR closed to new applicants in 2024** and is no longer available. What replaced it - a narrower scheme called **IFICI** (informally "NHR 2.0"), aimed specifically at scientific research and innovation professionals rather than the broad range of retirees and remote workers NHR previously attracted - does not automatically deliver the same outcome for a typical foreign investor, and sources currently disagree on exactly how it treats foreign dividend income for those who do qualify.

This guide covers what's clearly established: the treaty's standard 15% dividend rate, NHR's closure, and IFICI's narrower scope. Where sources genuinely conflict on IFICI's specific tax treatment of foreign dividends, this guide says so directly rather than picking a side.

---

## NHR Is Closed - IFICI Is Not a Like-for-Like Replacement

Portugal's NHR regime, which for years drew retirees and remote workers with the promise of favorable tax treatment on foreign income, **stopped accepting new applicants in 2024**. The transitional period that followed has now concluded, and NHR is not available to someone establishing Portuguese tax residency now.

Its replacement, **IFICI** (Incentivo Fiscal à Investigação Científica e Inovação - the Tax Incentive for Scientific Research and Innovation), is a **materially narrower scheme**. It targets a specific professional profile connected to scientific research and innovation activity, not the general expat or investor population that NHR served. If your interest in Portugal is driven by NHR's old reputation for investment-friendly tax treatment, confirm directly whether you'd even qualify for IFICI before assuming any of NHR's old benefits still apply to your situation - for most investors moving to Portugal now, they don't.

**Where sources genuinely conflict, for anyone who does qualify for IFICI:** some descriptions state that US dividends and interest paid to a Portuguese resident under IFICI are exempt from Portuguese tax, reasoning from the treaty's allocation of taxing rights to the US as the source country. Other descriptions of the same regime state foreign dividends and interest face a 28% withholding rate in Portugal. These are directly contradictory positions on the same question, from material published around the same time. This guide cannot resolve that conflict from outside Portugal - if IFICI eligibility is relevant to you, get written confirmation of your specific dividend tax treatment from a Portuguese tax advisor before relying on either description.

**Once granted, IFICI is valid for ten years** - a long commitment worth getting right at the outset given the stakes involved.

## The Treaty: 15% on Dividends

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Portugal Income Tax Convention (1994); IRS treaty text.*

This is the standard, uncontested part of the picture: claiming the 15% rate requires Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) filed with your broker. Without it, the full 30% default applies from the first dividend payment - this applies the same way regardless of whether you're under Portugal's ordinary tax regime or a special scheme like IFICI, since it's a function of the US side of the transaction.

**For investors under Portugal's ordinary tax regime** (i.e., not IFICI-qualifying), foreign dividends and capital gains are generally taxed as part of Portugal's standard investment income framework, with a foreign tax credit available for the US withholding already paid - the conventional structure used across most treaty countries in this series.

## Bottom Line

The treaty rate is standard and uncontested: 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN. What requires real care in Portugal right now is the special-regime question - NHR, the scheme most investors associate with favorable Portuguese tax treatment, is closed to new applicants, and its narrow replacement (IFICI) targets a different professional profile with tax treatment that current sources describe inconsistently. Don't plan a move to Portugal around assumed NHR-style benefits without confirming, specifically and in writing, what regime you'd actually qualify for and how it treats foreign investment income.
