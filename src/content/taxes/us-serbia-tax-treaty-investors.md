There's no US-Serbia income tax treaty - despite Serbia having double tax treaties with more than 60 countries, the US isn't one of them - so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Serbia-resident investor. Serbia taxes its residents on **worldwide income**, meaning US dividends and capital gains don't escape Serbian tax the way they do in some territorial-system countries covered elsewhere in this series. The one piece of real, meaningful relief: capital gains become **fully exempt** once you've held the asset for more than **10 consecutive years** - one of the longer holding-period exemptions in this series, longer than Czech Republic's 3 years but shorter than Slovenia's staircase out to 15.

This guide covers all of it.

---

## Serbia Is Not on the Treaty List, So Dividends Take the Full 30%

Serbia's treaty network runs to more than sixty countries; the United States is not among them, and the 1957 Yugoslavia convention does not carry over. US dividends therefore take the full **30% statutory withholding**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** The number stays 30%. The form buys correct non-US documentation instead of a backup-withholding classification.

---

## Serbia's Side: Worldwide Income, Flat Rates, and the 10-Year Exemption

Serbia taxes residents on worldwide income - unlike the several territorial-system countries covered elsewhere in this series, US-sourced dividends and capital gains don't fall outside Serbia's tax net just because they're foreign.

**Dividends** are generally taxed at a flat **15%**. **Capital gains** are also generally taxed at **15%**, applying to both residents and non-residents on the relevant gains.

**The exemption that matters for long-term holders:** capital gains become **fully exempt from Serbian tax once the asset has been held for more than 10 consecutive years.** This is a genuinely long holding period compared to several peers in this series - well beyond Czech Republic's 3-year exemption or Slovakia's 1-year threshold - but it's a real, absolute exemption once you clear it, not a sliding scale.

**Confirm the foreign-dividend mechanics with a Serbian tax advisor** before assuming the flat 15% domestic dividend rate applies identically to a US dividend - available guidance describes the rate broadly rather than drawing an explicit domestic/foreign distinction, but given how often that distinction has mattered in other countries covered in this series, it's worth a direct confirmation rather than an assumption either way.

## The Dinar, Briefly

The Serbian dinar (RSD) has traded within a notably tight range against the euro for an extended period, with the National Bank of Serbia actively managing the float rather than letting it move freely - a de facto stability that search interest in "Serbia dinar exchange rate" suggests plenty of Serbian investors already track closely when planning USD conversions for a brokerage account.

## What the 10-Year Clock Is Actually Worth

Serbia's decade-long holding exemption is easy to dismiss as impractically long. Priced out, it is one of the larger single tax breaks in this series.

Take a position bought for **$40,000** and sold for **$110,000** - a **$70,000** gain:

| Held | Serbian capital gains tax | Kept |
|---|---|---|
| Under 10 years | $10,500 (15%) | $99,500 |
| Over 10 years | **$0** | **$110,000** |

The exemption is absolute once cleared, not tapered - the day the tenth year completes, $10,500 of liability disappears. For an investor already holding a long-term index position, the planning implication is unusually concrete: **the sale date, not the strategy, decides whether that money is yours.**

Dividends get no equivalent relief. A **$2,000** US dividend loses **$600** to US withholding at 30%, with Serbia's flat 15% potentially applying on top - the crediting mechanics being exactly what the section above says to confirm rather than assume.

**Serbian resident, US portfolio - the short list:**

- [ ] Expect 30% US withholding on every dividend payment, with no treaty-based reduction available
- [ ] Track your holding period per lot - clearing 10 consecutive years makes a capital gain fully exempt from Serbian tax

## Serbia Investors: The Realistic View

There's no US-Serbia tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - the W-8BEN documents who you are; it does not change what you pay. Because Serbia taxes worldwide income rather than running a territorial system, US dividends and capital gains don't escape Serbian tax by default - but the 10-year capital gains exemption is real, absolute relief for genuinely long-term holders, sitting in the middle of the pack among holding-period exemptions covered across this series.
