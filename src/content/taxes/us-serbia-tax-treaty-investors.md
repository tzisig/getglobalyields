There's no US-Serbia income tax treaty - despite Serbia having double tax treaties with more than 60 countries, the US isn't one of them - so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Serbia-resident investor. Serbia taxes its residents on **worldwide income**, meaning US dividends and capital gains don't escape Serbian tax the way they do in some territorial-system countries covered elsewhere in this series. The one piece of real, meaningful relief: capital gains become **fully exempt** once you've held the asset for more than **10 consecutive years** - one of the longer holding-period exemptions in this series, longer than Czech Republic's 3 years but shorter than Slovenia's staircase out to 15.

This guide covers all of it.

---

## No Treaty: Full 30% US Withholding

With no income tax treaty between the US and Serbia, there's no treaty article to reduce the default rate. Every US dividend paid to a Serbia-resident investor is withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** It won't bring the rate below 30%, but it certifies your non-US status to your broker and avoids the risk of default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account.

---

## Serbia's Side: Worldwide Income, Flat Rates, and the 10-Year Exemption

Serbia taxes residents on worldwide income - unlike the several territorial-system countries covered elsewhere in this series, US-sourced dividends and capital gains don't fall outside Serbia's tax net just because they're foreign.

**Dividends** are generally taxed at a flat **15%**. **Capital gains** are also generally taxed at **15%**, applying to both residents and non-residents on the relevant gains.

**The exemption that matters for long-term holders:** capital gains become **fully exempt from Serbian tax once the asset has been held for more than 10 consecutive years.** This is a genuinely long holding period compared to several peers in this series - well beyond Czech Republic's 3-year exemption or Slovakia's 1-year threshold - but it's a real, absolute exemption once you clear it, not a sliding scale.

**Confirm the foreign-dividend mechanics with a Serbian tax advisor** before assuming the flat 15% domestic dividend rate applies identically to a US dividend - available guidance describes the rate broadly rather than drawing an explicit domestic/foreign distinction, but given how often that distinction has mattered in other countries covered in this series, it's worth a direct confirmation rather than an assumption either way.

## Bottom Line

There's no US-Serbia tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN still matters for documentation, not for a lower rate. Because Serbia taxes worldwide income rather than running a territorial system, US dividends and capital gains don't escape Serbian tax by default - but the 10-year capital gains exemption is real, absolute relief for genuinely long-term holders, sitting in the middle of the pack among holding-period exemptions covered across this series.
