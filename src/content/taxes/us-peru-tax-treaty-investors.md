Peru has income tax treaties with Brazil, Canada, Chile, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Portugal, and Switzerland - the United States is not on that list. **No US-Peru income tax treaty exists**, which means the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Peru-resident investor, with no reduced rate to claim. On the Peru side, US dividends and capital gains from US stocks don't get Peru's well-known flat 5% domestic dividend rate either - that rate applies specifically to Peruvian-source dividends. Foreign-source income, US stocks included, is instead folded into your regular annual return and taxed at Peru's **progressive rates, up to 30%**.

This guide covers both pieces clearly.

---

## No Treaty: Full 30% US Withholding

Because no income tax treaty exists between the US and Peru, there's no treaty article to reduce the default US withholding rate. Every US dividend paid to a Peru-resident investor is withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** It won't bring the rate below 30% - there's no treaty benefit to claim - but it certifies your non-US status to your broker and avoids the risk of default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account.

---

## Peru's Side: Foreign-Source Income Taxed Progressively, Not at the Flat 5% Rate

This is the detail most likely to trip up a Peru-resident investor who's seen Peru's dividend tax quoted as a flat 5%.

**Peru's flat 5% dividend withholding rate applies to Peruvian-source dividends** - dividends paid by Peruvian companies, withheld at the source. **It does not apply to foreign-source dividends**, including dividends from US-listed companies.

**Foreign-source dividends** are instead added together with your other income (including employment income) and declared on your Annual Income Tax Return (Declaración Jurada Anual), taxed at Peru's progressive rates running from **8% up to 30%** depending on your total income level for the year.

**Foreign-source capital gains** from selling securities work the same way - gains from selling US-listed stocks are generally treated as foreign-source income, consolidated with your other income, and taxed progressively. The one exception is securities traded through Peru's MILA (Mercado Integrado Latinoamericano) integration agreements with Chile, Mexico, and Colombia, which get separate treatment - but that exception doesn't extend to US-listed securities, which sit outside the MILA framework entirely.

**What this means in practice:** a Peru-resident investor's actual rate on US dividends and capital gains depends on their total annual income, not a single flat figure - someone in a lower income bracket may pay meaningfully less than 30%, while a higher earner sees their US investment income taxed at the top of the scale, stacked on top of salary and other income for bracket purposes.

## Practical Checklist

**Peru resident investing in US stocks:**
- [ ] File Form W-8BEN with your broker anyway - it won't reduce the 30% rate, but avoids default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account
- [ ] Expect 30% US withholding on every dividend payment, with no treaty-based reduction available
- [ ] Don't assume Peru's flat 5% domestic dividend rate applies to your US dividends - it doesn't; foreign-source dividends are taxed progressively instead
- [ ] Declare foreign-source dividends and capital gains from US stocks on your Annual Income Tax Return (Declaración Jurada Anual), consolidated with other income

## Bottom Line

There's no US-Peru tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN still matters for documentation, not for a lower rate. On the Peru side, the mistake to avoid is assuming the well-known flat 5% Peruvian dividend rate applies to US dividends - it doesn't. Foreign-source dividends and capital gains are folded into your regular income and taxed progressively, up to 30%, which can mean a meaningfully different - and for higher earners, higher - liability than the flat domestic rate most quick searches turn up.
