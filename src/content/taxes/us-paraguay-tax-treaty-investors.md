There's no US-Paraguay income tax treaty, so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Paraguay-resident investor. Paraguay's own system is clean and explicit: it taxes only Paraguayan-source income, and **foreign dividends received by tax residents, along with gains on foreign-asset disposals, fall outside the personal income tax entirely** - not a gray area requiring interpretation, but a stated exclusion. This is also, increasingly, a deliberate selling point: Paraguay has been actively marketed in recent years as an accessible, low-cost residency option specifically because of this rule.

This guide covers all three pieces.

---

## No Treaty: Full 30% US Withholding

With no income tax treaty between the US and Paraguay, there's no treaty article to reduce the default rate. Every US dividend paid to a Paraguay-resident investor is withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** It won't bring the rate below 30%, but it certifies your non-US status to your broker and avoids the risk of default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account.

---

## Paraguay's Side: A Clean, Explicit Foreign-Income Exclusion

Paraguay's personal income tax (IRP) applies to Paraguayan-source income. Domestically, dividends paid by Paraguayan companies face a **15%** withholding rate for non-resident shareholders and **8%** for resident shareholders, and capital gains on Paraguayan-asset disposals fall under a separate schedular category (IRP-RGC) at a flat **8%** on the net gain.

**None of that applies to your US stocks.** Foreign salaries, foreign dividends, foreign pensions, and gains on foreign-asset disposals are explicitly stated to fall **outside** the IRP for Paraguayan tax residents. This is a clear exclusion, not merely a low rate - Paraguay's tax authority doesn't currently reach US dividend or capital gains income held by a resident individual at all.

**This makes Paraguay one of several countries in this series - alongside Panama, Guatemala, and Jordan - where the 30% US withholding is, for practical purposes, the entire tax cost** of investing in US stocks. What's specific to Paraguay is how directly its own guidance states the exclusion, rather than requiring inference from a broader territorial framing.

## Why This Has Become a Residency Selling Point

This rule isn't just a technical footnote in Paraguay's tax code anymore - it's become a core part of how Paraguay markets itself to prospective residents. Paraguay offers a comparatively accessible and low-cost path to permanent residency, and combined with the 0% treatment of foreign dividends and foreign-asset capital gains, it's increasingly promoted in expat and relocation circles as a straightforward, low-friction base for someone whose income and investments are generated outside the country - a similar positioning to Panama's, reached through Paraguay's own specific combination of an easy residency process and an explicit foreign-income exclusion rather than a Panama-style constitutional territorial principle.

**The practical takeaway for an investor already resident in Paraguay, or considering it:** the foreign-income exclusion is a real, currently-stated rule, not a marketing exaggeration - but as with any tax rule tied to residency status, confirm your specific qualifying status and the current rule's exact scope with a Paraguayan tax advisor before relying on it for a large amount.

## Bottom Line

There's no US-Paraguay tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN still matters for documentation, not for a lower rate.
