Paraguay's rule is unusually unambiguous for this series. The country taxes Paraguayan-source income only, and **foreign dividends received by tax residents, along with gains on foreign-asset disposals, fall outside the personal income tax entirely** - not a gray area requiring interpretation, but a stated exclusion. That clarity has become a product in its own right: Paraguay has been actively marketed in recent years as an accessible, low-cost residency option specifically because of this rule. What Paraguay cannot do is reach the American end of the trade, where the absence of a US-Paraguay income tax treaty leaves every US dividend withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

Each of those is worked through below.

---

## No Treaty, and the 30% It Costs Paraguayan Investors

Paraguay and the United States have no income tax treaty. The consequence is simple and fixed: **30%** withheld from every US dividend, with no form or filing that lowers it.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** Nothing changes on the rate, but the account is properly papered as non-US.

---

## Paraguay's Side: A Clean, Explicit Foreign-Income Exclusion

Paraguay's personal income tax (IRP) applies to Paraguayan-source income. Domestically, dividends paid by Paraguayan companies face a **15%** withholding rate for non-resident shareholders and **8%** for resident shareholders, and capital gains on Paraguayan-asset disposals fall under a separate schedular category (IRP-RGC) at a flat **8%** on the net gain.

**None of that applies to your US stocks.** Foreign salaries, foreign dividends, foreign pensions, and gains on foreign-asset disposals are explicitly stated to fall **outside** the IRP for Paraguayan tax residents. This is a clear exclusion, not merely a low rate - Paraguay's tax authority doesn't currently reach US dividend or capital gains income held by a resident individual at all.

**This makes Paraguay one of several countries in this series - alongside Panama, Guatemala, and Jordan - where the 30% US withholding is, for practical purposes, the entire tax cost** of investing in US stocks. What's specific to Paraguay is how directly its own guidance states the exclusion, rather than requiring inference from a broader territorial framing.

## Why This Has Become a Residency Selling Point

This rule isn't just a technical footnote in Paraguay's tax code anymore - it's become a core part of how Paraguay markets itself to prospective residents. Paraguay offers a comparatively accessible and low-cost path to permanent residency, and combined with the 0% treatment of foreign dividends and foreign-asset capital gains, it's increasingly promoted in expat and relocation circles as a straightforward, low-friction base for someone whose income and investments are generated outside the country - a similar positioning to Panama's, reached through Paraguay's own specific combination of an easy residency process and an explicit foreign-income exclusion rather than a Panama-style constitutional territorial principle.

**The practical takeaway for an investor already resident in Paraguay, or considering it:** the foreign-income exclusion is a real, currently-stated rule, not a marketing exaggeration - but as with any tax rule tied to residency status, confirm your specific qualifying status and the current rule's exact scope with a Paraguayan tax advisor before relying on it for a large amount.

---

## What the Exclusion Is Worth Against Paraguay's Own Rates

Paraguay's foreign-income exclusion is easiest to value by comparing it with what the same money would cost if it were earned domestically. Take **$6,000** in dividends and a **$25,000** gain.

| | If Paraguayan-source | Because it is foreign-source |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends $6,000 | 8% resident rate = $480 | **$0** |
| Capital gain $25,000 | 8% IRP-RGC = $2,000 | **$0** |
| Paraguayan tax | $2,480 | **$0** |

Against that, the US takes **$1,800** from the dividend at the 30% statutory rate and nothing from the gain. So the whole tax cost of a $31,000 year of investment income is **$1,800** - an effective **5.8%**, all of it American.

**Why the comparison matters:** it shows the exclusion is not a low rate but an absence of one. Paraguay charges its own investors 8% on this income and charges you nothing on the same income earned abroad. That is a stated statutory exclusion, not an administrative gap that might close quietly.

**Where the risk actually sits** is therefore not the tax rate but your residency status. The exclusion attaches to being a Paraguayan tax resident holding foreign assets; if that status is not properly established, none of the zeros above apply. Confirming residency is worth more attention than optimising anything on the US side, where the 30% is fixed regardless.

## How This Adds Up for Paraguay Residents

There's no US-Paraguay tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN remains worth filing as documentation of foreign status. Paraguay's own rule is refreshingly explicit: foreign dividends and gains on foreign-asset disposals fall outside its personal income tax entirely for residents, a clear exclusion rather than an inferred one - which is exactly why it's become a genuine, actively marketed reason people choose Paraguayan tax residency in the first place.
