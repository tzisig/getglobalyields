There's no US-Zambia income tax treaty - Zambia has DTAs with 22 countries, and the US isn't one of them - so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Zambia-resident investor. Zambia's domestic tax picture splits cleanly in two: **capital gains aren't taxed at all** in Zambia, regardless of source, but **Zambian residents are taxed on foreign-sourced dividend income at 15%** - meaning a US stock's dividends and its capital gains face genuinely different Zambian tax treatment.

This guide covers both sides.

---

## Zambia Has No Treaty - So 30% It Is

The US and Zambia have no income tax treaty in force. US dividends are withheld at the full **30% statutory rate** - a fixed cost that sits alongside a Zambian system with some genuinely unusual features.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** Expect no rate change; the value is proper classification as a non-US person and protection from backup withholding.

---

## Capital Gains: 0%, Full Stop

Zambia does not levy any tax on capital gains - a clean, absolute rule that applies regardless of what's sold or where it's located. Selling US stocks through a foreign brokerage account produces a gain that simply isn't part of Zambia's tax base, the same as it wouldn't be for a domestic Zambian asset.

---

## Dividends: A Different Story - 15% on Foreign-Sourced Income

Unlike capital gains, dividend income doesn't get the same clean pass. **Zambian residents are taxed on foreign-sourced dividend and interest income at 15%** - and for residents, this withholding is treated as a **final tax**, meaning it settles the liability rather than being an advance payment reconciled later.

**The practical mechanics:** because a US dividend isn't paid through a Zambian withholding agent, this 15% is something a Zambian resident investor needs to self-assess and remit to the Zambia Revenue Authority (ZRA) directly, rather than have automatically deducted the way a Zambian-source dividend would be. **Confirm the exact declaration process and any available credit for the US withholding already paid with a Zambian tax advisor**, since the mechanics of crediting foreign tax already paid against this domestic 15% aren't detailed in general guidance and Zambia has no treaty specifying it.

## The Two Zambian Outcomes, Side by Side

Zambia splits sharply between the two kinds of return, and the split is worth seeing on a single portfolio. Take **$4,000** of US dividends in a year plus a **$15,000** gain on a sale.

| | Dividends ($4,000) | Capital gain ($15,000) |
|---|---|---|
| US withholding | $1,200 (30%) | $0 |
| Zambian tax | $600 (15%, self-assessed) | **$0** |
| Total | **$1,800** | **$0** |
| Effective rate | 45% - if the US tax does not credit | 0% |

**That 45% figure is the number to take seriously.** It is what a Zambian investor pays on dividend income if the 15% domestic charge sits on top of the 30% already withheld rather than crediting against it - and because there is no treaty, nothing on paper settles which it is. If the US withholding does credit, the dividend cost falls back to 30%. The 15-point spread rests entirely on an answer this guide cannot give you.

Capital gains have no such ambiguity: zero on both sides, full stop.

**What follows from that:** the shape of the portfolio matters more here than in almost any other country in this series. Every dollar routed into growth rather than distributions moves from a column that might cost 45% into one that costs nothing.

## Tidying Up the Zambia Details

**Working through it as a Zambian resident:**
- [ ] File W-8BEN as a documentation step - the 30% stands, but the form is what stops backup withholding attaching to the account
- [ ] Expect 30% US withholding on every dividend payment, with no treaty-based reduction available
- [ ] Don't expect a Zambian capital gains tax obligation on US stock sale proceeds - Zambia doesn't tax capital gains from any source
- [ ] Self-declare and remit Zambia's 15% tax on foreign-sourced dividend income to ZRA, since it won't be withheld automatically the way a Zambian-source dividend would be
- [ ] Confirm with a Zambian tax advisor how the 30% US withholding factors into your 15% Zambian dividend liability, given there's no treaty defining the credit mechanism
- [ ] Verify that Zambian residents are currently accepted, and what documents are wanted, before applying

## How This Adds Up for Zambian Residents

There's no US-Zambia tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN is documentation here, not a rate reduction. Zambia's own system is a genuine split: capital gains from selling US stocks face 0% Zambian tax, full stop, while dividends from those same stocks face a real, separate 15% domestic tax that has to be self-declared rather than withheld automatically. Knowing which side of that split a given transaction falls on is the practical thing to get right.
