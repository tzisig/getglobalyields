There's no US-Cambodia income tax treaty - the two countries signed a reciprocal trade agreement in October 2025 and have a FATCA information-sharing arrangement, but neither is a tax treaty - so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Cambodia-resident investor. Cambodia's law technically imposes a flat **20% capital gains tax** on residents' gains from capital located both inside and outside the country, explicitly reaching a US stock sale - but the tax's actual implementation has been **postponed repeatedly**, most recently pushed further back in January 2026. Separately, Cambodia's well-known heavy US dollar usage is itself in a genuine state of change, as the government actively promotes riel use for the first time in years.

This guide covers all three pieces.

---

## No Treaty: Full 30% US Withholding

With no income tax treaty between the US and Cambodia, there's no treaty article to reduce the default rate. Every US dividend paid to a Cambodia-resident investor is withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** It won't bring the rate below 30%, but it certifies your non-US status to your broker and avoids the risk of default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account.

---

## Capital Gains: A 20% Tax That Keeps Getting Delayed

Cambodian law applies capital gains tax to resident individuals on gains from capital located **both within and outside Cambodia** - explicitly reaching a US stock sale, at a flat **20%**. On paper, this is a real, current obligation for a Cambodian resident investor.

**In practice, the tax's rollout has been delayed multiple times.** Implementation was originally scheduled for September 2025, postponed to January 1, 2026, and then deferred again in a further announcement that same month - with capital gains tax on immovable property specifically now pushed to January 1, 2027. **This is a moving target**: confirm the current implementation status directly with a Cambodian tax advisor or the General Department of Taxation before assuming either that the tax is currently in effect or that it will remain postponed indefinitely, since it could take effect with limited additional notice given how close it's already come multiple times.

---

## Dividends: A Territorial System, With a Caveat

Cambodia generally operates a territorial tax system for individuals, under which foreign-sourced income is often described as tax-free for residents. **This general description applies most clearly to earned/employment-type income** - the treatment of foreign investment dividends specifically is less consistently documented in available guidance, and given that Cambodia's capital gains rule explicitly reaches assets "outside Cambodia," it would be a mistake to assume dividends automatically get more favorable treatment without confirming directly. **Check with a Cambodian tax advisor on how your specific US dividend income is classified**, rather than assuming the general territorial framing extends cleanly to it.

## Cambodia's Dollar Usage Is Genuinely Changing

Cambodia has long been one of the most heavily dollarized economies outside a formal dollarization arrangement, with the US dollar dominating larger transactions while the riel handles smaller, everyday purchases - the riel itself has traded at a remarkably stable informal rate of roughly **4,000-4,100 riel per dollar**.

**That balance is actively shifting.** The National Bank of Cambodia has been pushing de-dollarization: salary payments in riel began in 2026, and the government has moved to phase out small-denomination US banknotes and restrict ATM withdrawals to larger $100 bills - changes explicitly designed to nudge everyday transactions toward the riel. **There's no indication USD is being eliminated**, and it should remain the practical currency for funding a foreign brokerage account for the foreseeable future, but a Cambodian investor shouldn't assume the dollar-heavy banking environment of the past will look identical going forward.

## Bottom Line

There's no US-Cambodia tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN still matters for documentation, not for a lower rate. Cambodia's own 20% capital gains tax technically reaches a US stock sale, but its implementation has been delayed enough times that "check the current status before you sell" is the only responsible advice right now. And the heavily dollarized banking environment that's made Cambodia relatively easy for USD-based investing is itself in a genuine state of transition as the government pushes riel adoption.
