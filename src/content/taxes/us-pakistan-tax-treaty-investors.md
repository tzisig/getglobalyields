Most guides in this series lead with the tax rate. For Pakistan, the tax rate isn't the interesting part - the US-Pakistan treaty, dating to **1957**, caps dividend withholding at a straightforward **10%**, one of the lower rates in this series. The part worth understanding first is different: **Pakistani residents generally need State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) approval to hold foreign securities**, and funding a foreign brokerage account from Pakistan runs into capital controls that are meaningfully tighter than most countries covered in this series. Search interest in "Interactive Brokers Pakistan" is real and substantial - but the honest first question for a Pakistani resident isn't "what's the tax rate," it's "can I legally fund this account through authorized channels."

This guide covers the access question first, then the tax treatment.

---

## The Real Barrier: SBP Capital Controls, Not Tax

Pakistan's foreign exchange regime operates under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act (FERA), administered by the State Bank of Pakistan. **A Pakistani tax resident generally needs SBP approval to hold shares in a foreign company** - this includes funding a foreign brokerage account to buy US stocks. Foreign exchange transactions must go through authorized dealers, for permitted purposes, through SBP- and SECP-regulated channels; using an unauthorized route to remit funds abroad for this purpose can expose a resident to action under FERA.

**This is a meaningfully tighter regime than most peer economies.** Pakistan's capital account controls on residents sending money abroad for portfolio investment are more restrictive than comparable South and Southeast Asian markets covered elsewhere in this series.

**One narrow, confirmed channel:** authorized dealers can open a Foreign Currency Value Account (FCVA) for a resident Pakistani individual who already holds assets abroad, as declared in their latest wealth statement filed with the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) - a route for regularizing and operating already-existing foreign holdings, not necessarily a general-purpose mechanism for a first-time transfer to open a new foreign brokerage account from scratch.

**What this guide could not confirm:** a specific, current annual limit for a Pakistani resident individual to remit funds abroad specifically for opening or funding a foreign securities account through fully authorized channels. Given how consequential getting this wrong can be under FERA, **this is not a question to answer from a general guide** - confirm the current, permitted route and any limit directly with an SBP-authorized dealer bank or a Pakistani foreign-exchange lawyer before transferring any meaningful amount, rather than assuming either that it's freely permitted or that it's entirely blocked.

## The Treaty: 10% on Dividends

Once funds are legally in a foreign brokerage account, the tax side is comparatively simple.

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **10%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Pakistan Income Tax Convention (1957); IRS treaty tables; PwC Pakistan Corporate Withholding Taxes (2026).*

Claiming the 10% rate requires Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) filed with your broker. Without it, the full 30% default applies from the first dividend payment.

**Pakistan's domestic side:** Pakistan taxes its residents on worldwide income, which includes foreign-source dividends and capital gains from a foreign brokerage account - these are generally folded into your normal taxable income under Pakistan's progressive individual income tax slabs, rather than taxed at a separate flat rate. A **foreign tax credit is available**, capped at the lesser of the foreign tax actually paid (the 10% US withholding) or the Pakistani tax otherwise payable on that same income - confirm your specific bracket placement and the credit calculation with a Pakistani tax advisor, since this depends on your total income for the year.

## Practical Checklist

**Pakistani resident investing in US stocks:**
- [ ] Confirm the currently permitted route and any limit for remitting funds abroad to fund a foreign brokerage account with an SBP-authorized dealer bank before transferring money
- [ ] If you already hold foreign assets declared on your FBR wealth statement, ask your bank whether an FCVA is the right structure for your situation
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker once funded; verify 10%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Declare worldwide income including foreign dividends and capital gains on your Pakistani tax return, and claim the foreign tax credit for the 10% already withheld by the US
- [ ] Don't rely on an unauthorized remittance channel to move money abroad for this purpose - FERA exposure is a real risk, not a formality
- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro separately from the funding question

## Bottom Line

The tax side of investing in US stocks from Pakistan is genuinely simple: a 10% treaty dividend rate, one of the more favorable in this series, claimed with W-8BEN, credited against Pakistan's own worldwide-income tax. The part that actually determines whether this works for you is upstream of tax entirely - State Bank of Pakistan capital controls on residents sending money abroad for portfolio investment, which are real, actively enforced under FERA, and not something to route around informally. Get the funding channel confirmed with an authorized dealer first; the tax treatment afterward is the easy part.
