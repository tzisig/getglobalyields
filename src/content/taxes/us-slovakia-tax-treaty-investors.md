Slovakia exempts capital gains from selling securities entirely once two conditions are both met: the investor held the securities for more than **one year**, and the securities themselves have been listed on a regulated market - "or a similar foreign regulated market" - for more than a year as well. That second phrase matters directly for this guide's audience: unlike a few other countries in this series where the exemption is written narrowly around EU/EEA markets specifically, Slovak guidance describes the exemption as extending to securities on a comparable foreign regulated market, which US exchanges like the NYSE and NASDAQ are understood to qualify as.

This guide covers that exemption, Slovakia's notably low domestic dividend rate, and the treaty's standard 15% withholding rate.

---

## The 1-Year Exemption, and Why It Should Reach US Stocks

**The exemption:** income from selling securities accepted for trading on a regulated market (or a similar foreign regulated market) is exempt from Slovak income tax if more than one year has passed both since acquisition **and** since the securities were first accepted for trading on that market. For a major US-listed stock or ETF - which has typically been trading on the NYSE or NASDAQ for years before you bought it - the second condition is essentially automatic; the first condition (your own holding period) is the one that actually requires tracking.

**Why NYSE/NASDAQ should qualify:** the "similar foreign regulated market" language is broader than an EU/EEA-only restriction, and current Slovak guidance describes major foreign exchanges as falling within scope. This is a more favorable reading for US-market investors than the comparable, narrower EU/EEA-specific wording found in a few neighboring countries' equivalent rules - though as with any cross-border tax question resting on how domestic law characterizes a specific foreign exchange, confirming this with a Slovak tax advisor before relying on it for a large transaction is worth the extra step.

**Selling within a year:** the gain is taxed as ordinary income, with a small annual exemption of up to **€500** available regardless of holding period, and progressive rates applying above that - **19%** on income up to roughly **€48,441** (2026), with a higher rate applying above that threshold.

**Income sold within one year is not automatically at the 19% flat rate for all amounts** - the €48,441 figure is a bracket threshold within Slovakia's broader progressive personal income tax structure, not a separate capital-gains-specific cap, so total income (not just the gain) determines which portion falls into which bracket.

## Dividends: A Notably Low 7% Domestic Rate

Slovakia taxes dividends at a flat **7%** for individuals - a lower domestic rate than most countries covered in this series, where domestic dividend rates commonly range from 15% to 30% or more.

**The interaction with US withholding:** since the 15% US treaty withholding already exceeds Slovakia's 7% domestic dividend rate, the foreign tax credit fully absorbs the Slovak liability on the same dividend - similar in mechanism to Bulgaria's situation covered elsewhere in this series, where the US treaty rate also exceeds the domestic rate it's credited against. The practical result: a Slovak investor's total effective rate on US dividends is close to the 15% US withholding itself, with no meaningful additional Slovak tax layered on top, though (as with Bulgaria) the excess between the 15% paid and the 7% domestic rate isn't refunded - it's simply the cost of the treaty rate exceeding the domestic one.

## A Working List for Slovakia Residents

**Working through it as a Slovak resident:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Track acquisition dates per lot - clearing 1 year of your own holding period is the practical condition to watch for the capital gains exemption
- [ ] Get written confirmation from a Slovak tax advisor that your specific NYSE/NASDAQ-listed holdings qualify as "similar foreign regulated market" securities before relying on the exemption for a large sale
- [ ] Use the €500 annual exemption for gains realized within a year of purchase, with progressive rates (19% up to ~€48,441 of total income, higher above) applying to the remainder
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for the 15% US withholding against Slovakia's 7% domestic dividend rate - the credit fully absorbs the domestic layer given the US rate is higher
- [ ] Check what the broker asks of Slovak applicants before opening the application

## So What Should a Slovakia Investor Do?

The treaty rate is standard - 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN, and effectively the entire tax cost on dividends given Slovakia's low 7% domestic rate is fully absorbed by the credit. The real prize for a Slovak investor in US stocks is the 1-year capital gains exemption, which - unlike some neighboring countries' EU/EEA-only versions of the same idea - is understood to extend to NYSE- and NASDAQ-listed securities. Clearing a single year of holding time converts a capital gain from taxable to fully exempt, making Slovakia one of the more accessible long-term-holding exemptions in this series.
