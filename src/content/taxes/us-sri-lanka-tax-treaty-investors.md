Sri Lanka's capital gains tax for individuals just changed: the **Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026**, effective **June 3, 2026**, raised the individual CGT rate from **10% to 15%** - a real, recent, dateable increase, not a stable long-standing figure. On the treaty side, the US-Sri Lanka convention caps dividend withholding at a flat **15%** - unusually, with no reduced rate for larger qualifying shareholders, unlike most treaties in this series that offer a lower tier for 10%+ corporate holders.

This guide covers the new CGT rate, Sri Lanka's dividend treatment, and the Outward Investment Account mechanism residents use to fund foreign brokerage accounts.

---

## The New 15% Capital Gains Rate (June 2026)

Effective **June 3, 2026**, Sri Lanka's Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026 raised the capital gains tax rate for individuals and partnerships from **10% to 15%** - a straightforward increase, not a restructuring, applying to gains realized from the effective date forward. Gains realized before June 3, 2026 remain subject to the earlier 10% rate; anything sold after that date - including US stock sales - falls under the new 15% rate.

**For a Sri Lankan resident selling US stocks today, 15% is the current, correct figure to use** - not the 10% rate that may still appear in older guides or outdated summaries that predate this mid-2026 change.

---

## Dividends: A Flat 15% Domestically, and the Same 15% on the Treaty

Sri Lanka's domestic dividend withholding tax has been a flat **15%**, treated as a final withholding payment, since January 1, 2023 - a rate that applies clearly to Sri Lankan-source dividends paid by Sri Lankan companies.

**For foreign-source dividends specifically** - a US stock's dividend included - Sri Lankan residents are taxed on worldwide income, and general guidance indicates foreign-source income is taxed the same way as domestic-source income for resident taxpayers. Whether that means the flat 15% WHT mechanism applies directly to a US dividend, or whether it's instead assessed through your annual return alongside other income, is a distinction worth confirming with a Sri Lankan tax advisor (IRD-registered) rather than assuming - the underlying rate is the same 15% either way, but the mechanism (withheld vs. self-assessed) affects how and when you account for it.

## The Treaty: 15% on Dividends, No Reduced Tier

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Sri Lanka Income Tax Convention (1985, amended by 2002 Protocol); IRS treaty tables.*

Unlike most treaties in this series, the US-Sri Lanka convention doesn't offer a separately reduced rate for qualifying 10%+ corporate shareholders - portfolio and qualifying dividends are treated the same at **15%**, a simpler, flatter structure than the two-tier rate most other countries get.

Claiming the 15% rate requires Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) filed with your broker. Without it, the full 30% default applies from the first dividend payment.

## Funding an Account: The Outward Investment Account (OIA)

Sri Lanka's Central Bank designates a specific mechanism - the **Outward Investment Account (OIA)** - for resident individuals to channel funds abroad for eligible overseas investments, explicitly including shares, units, and debt securities. This is a real, named, legitimate channel, not an informal workaround.

**The context worth knowing:** outward investment through the OIA framework was tightened during Sri Lanka's 2022 economic crisis and has been progressively relaxed since, with the government easing restrictions on outward remittances beginning around mid-2023 as the domestic foreign exchange market stabilized. Given the pace of change in this area post-crisis, **confirm the current OIA limits and eligibility conditions directly with an authorized dealer bank or the Central Bank of Sri Lanka's Department of Foreign Exchange** before initiating a transfer, rather than relying on a figure that may already be outdated by the time you read this.

## Practical Checklist

**Sri Lankan resident investing in US stocks:**
- [ ] Confirm current OIA eligibility and limits with an authorized dealer bank or the Central Bank of Sri Lanka before transferring funds to a foreign broker
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Use the 15% CGT rate for any US stock sale after June 3, 2026 - not the older 10% rate that predates the 2026 amendment
- [ ] Confirm with an IRD-registered tax advisor whether your US dividends are captured by the flat 15% WHT mechanism or assessed through your annual return instead
- [ ] Claim relief for the 15% US withholding against your Sri Lankan liability on the same dividend income
- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro before applying

## Bottom Line

The treaty rate is a flat 15% on dividends - simpler than most peers in this series, with no separate lower tier for larger holders, claimed with W-8BEN. The number to get right on the Sri Lankan side just changed: capital gains tax for individuals rose from 10% to 15% effective June 3, 2026, so any US stock sale from that date forward uses the new rate. Funding the account in the first place runs through the Outward Investment Account framework - a real, legitimate channel, but one whose exact limits have been in flux since the 2022 crisis and are worth confirming fresh rather than assuming from an older source.
