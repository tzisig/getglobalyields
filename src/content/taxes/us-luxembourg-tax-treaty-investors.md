Luxembourg draws one of the sharpest lines in this series between short-term and long-term capital gains treatment: hold a US stock for more than **six months**, and the gain on sale is **fully exempt** from Luxembourg tax - no cap on the amount, provided you (and your household) haven't held more than 10% of the company's shares at any point in the preceding five years. Sell within six months, and the same gain is reclassified as "speculative income," taxed at ordinary progressive rates reaching **45.78%** including surcharges.

Six months is a short holding period to clear compared to similar rules elsewhere in this series - Czech Republic's exemption requires three years, for comparison - which makes Luxembourg's version unusually accessible for an ordinary buy-and-hold investor. This guide covers that exemption in detail, alongside the treaty's standard 15% dividend rate.

---

## Six Months: A Genuinely Short Bar to Clear

**Held more than 6 months:** the capital gain on a private individual's disposal of shares - US stocks included - is exempt from Luxembourg tax entirely, with no cap on the exempted amount. The only condition is the ownership-threshold test: you and your household must not have held more than 10% of the company's share capital at any point during the five years before the sale - a condition that describes essentially every individual retail investor holding US-listed stocks, since 10% of a major US company is far beyond any retail position.

**Held 6 months or less:** the gain is classified as speculative income (not simply "short-term capital gain") and taxed at Luxembourg's standard progressive personal income tax rates, which reach **45.78%** once the 9% solidarity surcharge and 1.4% dependency contribution are included - a dramatic difference from the 0% that applies just past the six-month mark.

**The practical takeaway:** for a buy-and-hold investor in US stocks, clearing six months of holding time before selling converts a potentially large tax liability into zero Luxembourg tax on the gain. This is one of the more favorable, and more quickly achievable, long-term holding exemptions covered anywhere in this series - worth building into a deliberate minimum-hold discipline for any position you're considering selling.

## The Treaty: 15% on Dividends

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) | Direct Corporate (10%+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** | **5%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Luxembourg Income Tax Convention (1996), Protocol (2009); IRS treaty text.*

Claiming the 15% rate requires Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) filed with your broker. Without it, the full 30% default applies from the first dividend payment. Dividend income itself is not affected by the 6-month capital gains exemption - it's taxed on Luxembourg's ordinary basis regardless of how long the underlying shares have been held, with a foreign tax credit available for the 15% US withholding already paid.

## Practical Checklist

**Luxembourg resident investing in US stocks:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Track acquisition dates per lot - the 6-month clock runs from purchase, and clearing it converts the entire gain to tax-exempt status in Luxembourg
- [ ] Avoid selling a position within 6 months of purchase where avoidable - the tax difference between speculative-income treatment (up to 45.78%) and the exemption (0%) is the largest such gap covered anywhere in this series
- [ ] Confirm your household's cumulative ownership stays under 10% of any single company at all times over the trailing five years (essentially automatic for retail-sized positions in large US companies)
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for the 15% US withholding against Luxembourg tax on dividend income, which remains taxable regardless of holding period
- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements with your chosen broker before applying

## Bottom Line

The treaty rate is standard - 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN. What makes Luxembourg stand out in this series is how quickly its capital gains exemption becomes available: six months, versus multi-year holding periods required for similar exemptions elsewhere. For a genuine buy-and-hold investor in US stocks, that's a low bar that converts capital gains tax exposure to zero for nearly any position held with normal patience - dividend income remains taxable throughout, but the capital gains side of a Luxembourg-resident US stock portfolio can be remarkably tax-efficient with minimal planning.
