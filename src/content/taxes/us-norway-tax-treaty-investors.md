Norway's popular tax-deferral account for share investing, the **aksjesparekonto (ASK)**, only permits holdings in equity funds and individual stocks based in the **EEA** - direct US stocks don't qualify. A Norwegian investor wanting US market exposure inside an ASK has to go through a Norwegian-domiciled fund tracking a US index, such as a Norwegian S&P 500 fund, rather than holding Apple or an S&P 500 ETF directly. This is a hard structural limit, not a minor inconvenience, and it shapes how most Norwegian investors end up accessing US markets at all.

This guide covers that ASK restriction, Norway's wealth tax (formuesskatt) as it applies to share holdings, and the treaty's standard 15% dividend rate for whatever portion of your US exposure sits outside the ASK.

---

## ASK: A Real Tax Advantage, With a Hard EEA Limit

Inside an ASK, gains and dividends on eligible holdings aren't taxed as they accrue - tax is deferred until you actually withdraw gains from the account, and your original deposit can always be withdrawn tax-free. When gains are eventually withdrawn, the 2026 rate is **37.84%**, reduced by a "shielding deduction" (skjermingsfradrag) of roughly 3.6% that accounts for a risk-free return already built into the calculation.

**The EEA restriction is the detail that catches investors:** ASK eligibility is limited to equity funds and individual stocks based in the EEA. Direct US stocks and US-domiciled ETFs don't qualify for ASK inclusion. For a Norwegian investor specifically drawn to this site's coverage of individual US stocks or US-domiciled ETFs, that means the ASK's tax-deferral advantage simply isn't available on those holdings - they have to sit in a regular taxable account instead, or the investor accesses US market exposure indirectly through a Norwegian or EEA-domiciled fund that itself invests in US equities.

**Wealth tax and the ASK's valuation discount:** Norway's wealth tax (formuesskatt) applies to net worldwide assets above **NOK 1,900,000** for a single filer (NOK 3,800,000 for a married couple), at a combined municipal-and-state rate of roughly **1.0%-1.1%** (rising to a higher state-only component above NOK 21.5 million in net wealth). Holdings inside an ASK get a **20% valuation discount** for wealth tax purposes - assessed at 80% of market value rather than full value - a real advantage, but again one that only applies to the EEA-based holdings actually eligible for ASK in the first place. Direct US stocks in a regular account are assessed at full market value for wealth tax purposes, with no equivalent discount.

## The Treaty Rate for Norwegian Residents Is 15%

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Norway Income Tax Convention; IRS treaty text; PwC Norway Tax Summaries (2026).*

Get the form lodged and the withholding drops accordingly. Until it is on file, the 30% default governs every payment. Since direct US stocks generally sit in a regular taxable account rather than an ASK given the EEA restriction, this 15% withholding is the relevant US-side number for most Norwegian holders of individual US shares.

## Getting Set Up as a Norway Investor

Both Interactive Brokers and eToro currently accept applications from Norwegian residents. Neither changes the ASK eligibility question, which is a Norwegian account-structure rule independent of broker choice - and note that ASK accounts themselves are typically offered through Norwegian or Nordic brokers (such as Nordnet) rather than through IBKR or eToro directly, which is worth confirming if an ASK specifically (for your EEA-eligible holdings) is part of your plan.

## The Short List for Norway Investors

**The Norwegian investor's short list:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Don't assume direct US stocks or US-domiciled ETFs can go in an ASK - they can't; the account is EEA-restricted
- [ ] If you want ASK's tax deferral for US market exposure, look at Norwegian or EEA-domiciled funds tracking US indices rather than direct US holdings
- [ ] Track your net wealth against the NOK 1,900,000 (single) / NOK 3,800,000 (married) wealth tax threshold, remembering direct US stocks are valued at full market value, not the 80% ASK-eligible discount
- [ ] Report US dividends and capital gains under Norway's regular share taxation rules for holdings outside the ASK
- [ ] Verify current onboarding requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro yourself

## Pulling the Norway Threads Together

The treaty rate is standard - 15% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN. What actually shapes a Norwegian investor's approach to US stocks is the ASK's EEA restriction: Norway's best tax-deferral vehicle for share investing simply isn't available for direct US holdings, pushing them into a regular taxable account that also loses the ASK's 20% wealth-tax valuation discount. Anyone drawn to this site specifically for individual US stocks or US-domiciled ETFs should plan around holding them outside the ASK from the start, rather than discovering the restriction after the fact.
