For every other country in this series, tax is the main question and currency access is a footnote. For Lebanon, that's reversed. Lebanon's banking system has been in crisis since **October 2019**: the lira has lost more than **98% of its value**, the official exchange rate has moved from a pre-crisis peg of 1,507.5 to roughly **89,500 LBP per dollar**, and an estimated **$72 billion in deposits** remains effectively frozen under informal capital controls that were never formalized into law. There's also no US-Lebanon income tax treaty, so full 30% US withholding applies regardless - but for most readers of this guide, the tax rate isn't the obstacle. Getting money out of a Lebanese bank and into a foreign brokerage account, or funding one at all, is.

This guide addresses that honestly before getting to tax.

---

## The Banking Crisis: What This Guide Can and Can't Tell You

Since October 2019, Lebanese banks have imposed informal withdrawal restrictions that have never been codified into formal capital controls law. **If you hold pre-crisis Lebanese-lira or dollar deposits in a Lebanese bank, accessing that money has meant accepting severe losses** - Lebanese-pound withdrawals from dollar accounts are typically processed at conversion rates far below the market rate, in some cases costing depositors 75-85% of the deposit's real value in the process. **This guide cannot tell you how to recover or access frozen legacy deposits** - that requires a Lebanese financial advisor or lawyer who specializes in this specific, severe, and still-unresolved situation, not general investing guidance.

**If your situation involves new income going forward** - rather than money already trapped in the system - the practical question of whether you can currently wire fresh funds out of Lebanon to a foreign broker, and through what channel, is also something that needs to be confirmed directly and currently with a Lebanese bank, given how much this has shifted since 2019 and continues to shift.

**Where things stand politically:** the post-2024-war political reconfiguration - a weakened Hezbollah and President Joseph Aoun in office since January 2025 - has created what analysts describe as "modest new room for movement" on reform. As of April 2026, however, proposed actions had not yet cleared parliament. **This is an active, unresolved situation, not a settled one** - confirm current conditions before making any assumption based on this guide or any other general source.

## 30% Withholding, and No Treaty to Reduce It

No US-Lebanon income tax treaty exists. For anyone able to fund and hold a foreign brokerage account at all, US dividends arrive net of the full **30% statutory rate** - a fixed cost that ranks well below the banking-access problem covered below.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** Filing brings no discount - only correct status and protection from backup withholding.

---

## Domestic Tax: A Secondary Question Right Now

Given the scale of the banking crisis, Lebanon's specific domestic tax treatment of foreign dividends and capital gains is, realistically, a secondary concern for most readers compared to the access question above. This guide did not find reliable, current, specific guidance on how Lebanese resident individuals' foreign investment income is taxed domestically amid the ongoing crisis - **confirm this directly with a Lebanese tax advisor** rather than relying on pre-crisis assumptions, since Lebanon's fiscal administration itself has been affected by the broader collapse.

## What It Comes Down To in Lebanon

There's no US-Lebanon tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - but for most people reading this, that's not the binding constraint. Lebanon's banking system has been in an unresolved crisis since October 2019, with the lira down more than 98% and tens of billions in deposits still effectively frozen. Reform efforts have gained some momentum since early 2025 but had not cleared parliament as of April 2026. Anyone in this situation - whether dealing with frozen legacy deposits or trying to move new income abroad - needs current, specialized Lebanese financial and legal guidance well before the tax questions in this article become relevant.
