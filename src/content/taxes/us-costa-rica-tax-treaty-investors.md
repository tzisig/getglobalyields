There's no US-Costa Rica income tax treaty, so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Costa Rica-resident investor. Costa Rica's own territorial tax system exempts foreign-source dividends and capital gains for individuals entirely - the familiar zero-tax-on-foreign-income picture seen elsewhere in this series. What's genuinely timely right now is the currency: the Costa Rican colón has appreciated roughly **12% against the US dollar over the past year**, reaching its strongest levels in over a decade in early 2026 - a real, current shift that changes the math for anyone converting colones to fund a US brokerage account today.

This guide covers all three pieces.

---

## No Treaty: Full 30% US Withholding

With no income tax treaty between the US and Costa Rica, there's no treaty article to reduce the default rate. Every US dividend paid to a Costa Rica-resident investor is withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** It won't bring the rate below 30%, but it certifies your non-US status to your broker and avoids the risk of default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account.

---

## Costa Rica's Territorial System: 0% on Foreign Investment Income

Costa Rica's tax system runs on territoriality: only Costa Rica-source income is taxable. **Foreign-source dividends, interest, and capital gains - including returns from US stocks - are tax-exempt for individuals.**

**One caveat that doesn't apply to a typical individual investor:** Costa Rica has an anti-avoidance rule under which passive income (dividends, interest, capital gains, and similar) can become taxable if it flows through a multinational-group entity that fails to meet substance requirements in its jurisdiction. This targets corporate structuring, not a personal brokerage account - an individual holding US stocks directly through Interactive Brokers or eToro isn't the intended target and generally isn't affected by it.

## The Colón's 2026 Surge: What It Means for Funding an Account Now

This is the timely, current piece of this guide. Costa Rica's colón was the strongest-performing currency in Central America in 2025, appreciating roughly **4%** against the dollar that year - and the trend accelerated into 2026, with the exchange rate reaching around **₡449 per dollar** in the first half of the year, a more than decade-high level, and roughly **12%** appreciation over the trailing year according to central bank data.

**What's driving it:** a sustained, heavy supply of dollars into Costa Rica's relatively shallow currency market - from strong tourism receipts, resilient goods and services exports (goods exports were up 15% year-over-year through November 2025), and continued foreign direct investment - has pushed the colón's value up faster than the local economy can easily absorb, to the point that exporters and the tourism sector have publicly flagged it as a real competitiveness problem.

**What this means for an investor converting CRC to USD right now:** a stronger colón means **fewer colones are needed to buy the same amount of US dollars** than a year ago - a favorable moment, on the currency-conversion side specifically, to fund a US brokerage account. Analysts expect some moderation back toward roughly ₡500/USD during 2026, so this specific window is not guaranteed to persist - worth acting on with that in mind rather than assuming today's rate is the new permanent baseline.

## Bottom Line

There's no US-Costa Rica tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN still matters for documentation, not for a lower rate. Costa Rica's territorial system means there's no domestic layer on top for a typical individual investor. The genuinely current story is the colón's sharp 2026 appreciation to decade highs - a real, dateable, favorable moment for CRC-to-USD conversion that's already showing signs of straining the broader economy and may not hold at current levels through the rest of the year.
