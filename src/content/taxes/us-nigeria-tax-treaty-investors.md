Nigeria has double tax agreements with the UK, Canada, China, France, the Netherlands, South Africa, and several others - the United States is not among them. For dividends from US stocks, the full **30%** default withholding applies, with no treaty rate to claim via [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/).

What makes 2026 a genuinely important year to get this right: Nigeria's tax system was substantially overhauled by a new Tax Act, effective **January 1, 2026**, which restructured personal income tax rates, folded capital gains into the progressive income tax framework, and - the detail most relevant to this guide's audience - **retained a long-standing exemption for foreign dividends, interest, rent, and royalties remitted to Nigeria through approved channels**. This guide covers what that means for a Nigerian investor in US stocks, alongside the treaty-free 30% withholding and the new capital gains rules.

---

## No Treaty: The 30% Is Fixed

Without a treaty, W-8BEN doesn't reduce the withholding rate the way it does in treaty countries - it still certifies non-US person status and prevents the higher backup withholding regime from applying, but the number on your dividend statement will read 30%.

**Capital gains:** the US generally does not tax capital gains realized by a non-resident alien on US securities under domestic law (IRC §871), independent of treaty status - this part of the picture doesn't depend on a US-Nigeria treaty existing at all.

## The 2026 Tax Act: What Changed for Investors

Nigeria's new Tax Act took effect January 1, 2026, and two changes matter directly to someone investing in US stocks.

**Foreign dividends: the exemption survived the reform.** Nigeria has long exempted foreign-sourced dividends, interest, rent, and royalties from Nigerian tax when the income is remitted into Nigeria through government-approved banking channels - and reporting on the 2026 reform confirms this relief was retained, not repealed, in the new framework. **This is worth taking seriously rather than assuming it doesn't apply to an individual retail investor:** if it extends to your situation, your US dividend income - already reduced by the 30% US withholding - may face no additional Nigerian tax at all, provided it's brought into Nigeria properly documented (this is typically where a Certificate of Capital Importation or equivalent approved-channel documentation becomes relevant). Confirm the specific mechanics and whether they apply to your account structure with a Nigerian tax advisor before assuming the exemption covers you automatically - the documentation requirements matter for whether the relief actually attaches.

**Capital gains: folded into progressive income tax.** Nigeria previously taxed capital gains at a separate flat rate. The 2026 Tax Act moves capital gains into the ordinary progressive personal income tax framework instead - a structural shift, not just a rate change. Separately, Nigeria's new capital gains provisions for foreign investors in *Nigerian* equities introduced a much higher rate (up to 30%, with exceptions for reinvestment) - but that specific change targets foreign money coming into Nigerian securities, not a Nigerian resident's gains on foreign (US) stocks, which is a different question governed by the general worldwide-income and progressive-rate rules described above. Don't conflate the two when researching this further; a lot of 2026 Nigerian tax commentary is written from the inbound-foreign-investor perspective, not the resident-investing-abroad perspective this guide covers.

**Personal income tax rates:** the new progressive structure means individuals earning up to roughly ₦1.2 million annually pay no tax, with progressive rates applying above that threshold - relevant context for how any capital gains not covered by the foreign-income exemption would ultimately be taxed if they don't qualify for the approved-channel relief.

## Residency Determines Worldwide Taxation

Nigerian tax residents - determined by domicile, habitual abode, family and economic ties, or physical presence in Nigeria for at least 183 days - are taxed on worldwide income under the new Tax Act. Non-residents are taxed only on Nigeria-sourced income. If you're a Nigerian citizen who doesn't meet Nigeria's residency tests because you live and work permanently abroad, your foreign investment income generally falls outside Nigeria's tax net regardless of the foreign-dividend exemption question above - the exemption matters specifically for residents whose worldwide income is otherwise in scope.

## Bottom Line

The US side is fixed and simple: no treaty, 30% withholding on dividends, no US tax on capital gains. The Nigerian side changed meaningfully in 2026 - capital gains now run through the progressive income tax system rather than a flat rate, and the retained foreign-dividend exemption for income remitted through approved channels is a real, favorable provision worth confirming applies to your situation rather than assuming it does or doesn't without checking. Given how recent and structurally significant the 2026 Tax Act is, this is a case where getting current, specific advice from a Nigerian tax professional is worth more than relying on any general guide, including this one.
