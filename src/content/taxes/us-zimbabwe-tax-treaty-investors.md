Two things about Zimbabwe matter more to a US-stock investor than its tax code does. The first is the currency story, which is genuinely remarkable right now: the **Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG)**, backed by foreign exchange reserves held mainly in gold and introduced in April 2024, has held broadly stable through 2026, with annual inflation falling to **4.1% in January 2026** - single digits for the **first time since 1997**. The second is that **targeted US sanctions on specific Zimbabwean individuals and entities create real, practical friction for cross-border banking**, even though the sanctions program doesn't target the country, its people, or its banking sector as a whole. The tax layer is the straightforward part: no US-Zimbabwe income tax treaty exists, so the full **30% statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend.

Those three threads run through the rest of this guide.

---

## Zimbabwe Is Not on the Treaty List, So Dividends Take the Full 30%

No income tax treaty exists between the United States and Zimbabwe. Every US dividend paid to a Zimbabwean resident is withheld at **30%**, the full statutory rate, with no reduction available.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** Filing changes nothing about the 30% - it changes your documented status, and keeps backup withholding off the account.

---

## Zimbabwe's Domestic Tax: Worldwide Income, With a Rate Question

Zimbabwe taxes residents on worldwide income. Foreign dividends - a US stock's dividend included - are deemed Zimbabwe-source income for a person ordinarily resident in Zimbabwe when received, and taxed at **20%**.

**Capital gains are more complicated to classify.** Zimbabwe's capital gains rates differ sharply by category: **20%** on unlisted marketable securities and immovable property (5% if acquired before February 2009), versus a much lower **1%** on *listed* marketable securities. Available guidance describes "listed" in the context of the Zimbabwe Stock Exchange specifically - **it's not clear from general sources whether a US-listed stock, held through a foreign broker, would be treated as a "listed marketable security" for this purpose, or fall under the higher unlisted rate.** This is a genuinely consequential classification question - a 19-point rate gap - worth resolving directly with a Zimbabwean tax advisor (ZIMRA-registered) before assuming either outcome.

## The ZiG Turnaround

Zimbabwe has a well-known history of currency instability - multiple currency collapses and a famous hyperinflation episode remain part of how the country is generally perceived. The current chapter looks different. The **Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG)**, introduced in April 2024 and backed by foreign exchange reserves held mainly in gold, now accounts for roughly **40% of daily transactions**, with the government continuing gold purchases through 2026 to build reserves supporting the currency.

**The results so far:** the USD/ZiG rate has held in a relatively narrow band through 2026 - roughly **25.6 to 26.3 ZiG per dollar** - and annual inflation fell to **4.1% in January 2026**, a single-digit figure not seen since 1997. **Zimbabwe continues to operate a multi-currency system**, with US dollars still widely used and accepted alongside ZiG for many transactions - a practical detail that may make USD-denominated brokerage funding more familiar to Zimbabwean residents than the "ZiG-to-USD conversion" framing alone suggests.

---

## A Direct Note on Sanctions

**US sanctions on Zimbabwe are targeted, not comprehensive** - they're aimed at specific individuals and entities implicated in human rights abuses, corruption, or undermining democratic processes, administered under ZIDERA and OFAC. **They do not target Zimbabwe as a country, its people generally, or its banking sector as a whole.**

**In practice, this distinction hasn't fully protected ordinary cross-border transactions.** International banks and payment platforms, wary of inadvertently processing a transaction connected to a sanctioned party, have at times intercepted, blocked, or delayed payments involving Zimbabwean banks and individuals with no direct connection to any sanctioned person - a form of over-compliance ("de-risking") that creates real friction disconnected from the sanctions' actual legal scope.

**What this means for opening and funding a US brokerage account:** most ordinary individual investors are not affected by the sanctions themselves, but should expect the possibility of extra friction, delay, or additional documentation requests during international transfers - not because of anything specific to them, but because of this broader over-compliance pattern. This guide does not provide, and will not provide, guidance on working around sanctions restrictions; if your situation involves any connection to a sanctioned individual or entity, or if a transaction is repeatedly blocked without clear explanation, consult a sanctions-specialist lawyer rather than attempting to resolve it informally.

## A Zimbabwe Investor's Checklist

**If you're a Zimbabwean resident holding US stocks:**
- [ ] File W-8BEN with your broker - no treaty rate to claim, but the form keeps the account documented and out of backup withholding
- [ ] Expect 30% US withholding on every dividend payment, with no treaty-based reduction available
- [ ] Confirm with a ZIMRA-registered tax advisor whether your US stock gains are treated as "listed" (1%) or "unlisted" (20%) marketable securities under Zimbabwean law
- [ ] Budget for Zimbabwe's 20% domestic tax on foreign dividend income, deemed Zimbabwe-source for residents
- [ ] Expect possible extra scrutiny or delay on international transfers due to broader bank de-risking around Zimbabwe, even if you have no connection to any sanctioned party
- [ ] Consult a sanctions-specialist lawyer, not a general guide, if any transaction is blocked or your situation involves a potentially sanctioned party
- [ ] Verify current onboarding requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro yourself

## Zimbabwe, in a Paragraph

There's no US-Zimbabwe tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - the W-8BEN serves as proof of foreign status, nothing more. Zimbabwe's domestic side carries a real, unresolved classification question on capital gains (1% vs. 20%) worth confirming before you sell, plus a straightforward 20% on foreign dividends. The genuinely positive current story is the ZiG - a real, dateable stabilization bringing single-digit inflation for the first time in nearly three decades - tempered by honest acknowledgment that targeted sanctions, while not aimed at ordinary investors, do create real practical friction in cross-border banking that's worth planning around rather than being surprised by.
