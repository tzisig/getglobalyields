Most treaties in this series cap portfolio dividends at 15%. **Tunisia's doesn't.** The US-Tunisia tax convention sets the maximum withholding rate on **portfolio** dividends at **20%** - higher than almost every other country covered here - while dividends from a **direct investment** (a shareholder owning 25% or more) get the lower **14%** rate, an unusual inversion of the pattern where the smaller individual investor typically gets the better deal. Separately, Tunisia's **2026 Finance Law** eased the rules for opening a foreign currency account, removing a prior-authorization requirement that used to apply.

This guide covers both pieces.

---

## The Treaty: 20% on Portfolio Dividends, Not 15%

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) | Treaty Rate (25%+ Direct Investment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **20%** | **14%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only | - |

*Sources: US-Tunisia Income Tax Convention; IRS treaty text.*

**Why this matters practically:** an individual investor holding US stocks through a personal brokerage account - the portfolio case - faces 20% withholding once Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is on file, not the 15% that's become the de facto standard across most of the countries in this series. Without W-8BEN filed, the un-treatied default of 30% applies instead, so filing the form is still clearly worth doing - it just doesn't get you as far as it does elsewhere.

**The direct-investment rate is genuinely lower, not a typo:** a 25%+ corporate shareholder gets 14%, below the portfolio rate - the reverse of the usual pattern where corporate holders need a large stake just to match what an individual gets automatically. For virtually every reader of this guide, the 20% portfolio rate is the one that applies.

## What the 20% Costs Against the Series Standard

Tunisia's 20% portfolio rate is five points above the 15% that most treaties in this series deliver, and the gap compounds quietly on a dividend-paying portfolio.

On **$7,000** of annual dividends:

| | Withheld | Reaching the account |
|---|---|---|
| No W-8BEN (30% default) | $2,100 | $4,900 |
| **Tunisia's treaty rate (20%)** | **$1,400** | **$5,600** |
| A typical 15% treaty country | $1,050 | $5,950 |

Filing the form is still clearly worth **$700 a year** here. But a Tunisian investor also runs a permanent **$350 a year** disadvantage against an investor in a 15% country holding the identical portfolio - not something any form corrects.

**The inversion is worth restating because it reads like an error:** a corporate shareholder with 25%+ of the payer gets **14%**, below the individual's 20%. In most treaties the large holder must clear a high bar merely to match what a retail investor receives automatically. Here the retail investor is the one paying more, and there is no election or structure available to an ordinary individual that reaches the 14%.

## The 2026 Finance Law: Foreign Currency Accounts Without Prior BCT Approval

Tunisia's foreign exchange framework is administered by the Banque Centrale de Tunisie (BCT), and it has historically required prior BCT authorization for certain foreign currency account arrangements. **Tunisia's 2026 Finance Law changed this**: foreign currency accounts (Compte Personne Physique Résidente, or PPR accounts) can now be opened through authorized financial intermediaries **without prior BCT approval** - a genuine easing compared to the previous requirement.

**What this account actually is:** a PPR account is a bank account that lets a Tunisian resident legally hold, receive, and use foreign currency or convertible dinars, supervised under a defined BCT regulatory framework. Interest earned on funds in the account is subject to a small **0.01%** tax withholding.

**What this guide could not confirm:** whether this specific account type, or the eased opening process, extends cleanly to funding a foreign securities brokerage account specifically, as opposed to general foreign-currency holding and use. Tunisia is also currently pursuing a broader "nouveau code des changes" (new exchange control code) structural reform, reported as underway as of March 2026 - meaning the rules in this area are actively in motion. **Confirm the current, specific process for funding a foreign brokerage account with an authorized Tunisian intermediary bank before transferring funds**, rather than assuming the general PPR account easing automatically covers this use case.

## The Short Version for Tunisia

The US-Tunisia treaty is a genuine outlier in this series: 20% on portfolio dividends, not the 15% most readers of this series have come to expect, with W-8BEN still worth filing to avoid the full 30% default. On the practical-access side, Tunisia's 2026 Finance Law eased the rules for opening a foreign currency account - a real, recent liberalization - though whether it specifically simplifies funding a foreign brokerage account is worth confirming directly given the framework is still evolving.
