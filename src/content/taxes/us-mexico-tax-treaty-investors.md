Mexican investors who trade on the Bolsa Mexicana de Valores (BMV) are used to a favorable, flat 10% tax on net gains. The natural assumption - that the same 10% applies to gains on US stocks bought through a foreign broker - is wrong, and it's one of the more common, costly misreadings of Mexican tax law among investors moving into US markets. Gains on foreign stocks are taxed as ordinary income at Mexico's progressive rate, up to 35%, not at the preferential BMV rate.

The treaty side is more straightforward: the US-Mexico tax treaty caps dividend withholding at **10%** for individual portfolio investors, claimed with [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/). This guide covers that rate, the capital gains distinction that catches BMV-accustomed investors off guard, and the self-assessment reality of investing through a foreign broker.

---

## Mexico's 10% Dividend Rate

The US-Mexico income tax treaty (1992, amended by protocols) caps US withholding on dividends paid to Mexican resident individuals at **10%** for portfolio investors (under 10% ownership) - reduced from the 30% non-treaty default. Direct-investment dividends to a corporate holder owning 10% or more of the voting stock get a lower 5% rate, and dividends to an 80%+ owned corporate subsidiary or a qualifying pension fund can reach 0% - neither of which describes an individual retail investor.

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Individual Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **10%** |
| Dividends (10%+ corporate holder) | 30% | 5% |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Mexico Income Tax Convention (1992) and protocols; IRS treaty text; JCT explanation of the treaty and protocol.*

**A condition worth knowing exists, even if it rarely bites an individual investor:** treaty benefits require satisfying Article 17's Limitation on Benefits provisions. For an individual who is a genuine Mexican tax resident investing through their own personal brokerage account, this is not an obstacle - LOB clauses are aimed at third-country residents routing income through Mexico specifically to access treaty rates, not at ordinary resident investors.

10% is available only against a Form W-8BEN your broker holds on file. Skip the form and it is 30% from the first dividend on.

## Capital Gains: Why the BMV's 10% Rate Doesn't Apply to US Stocks

This is the detail most likely to cost a Mexican investor real money if assumed rather than checked.

**Mexican-listed shares (BMV):** gains from selling shares on the Bolsa Mexicana de Valores, when the transaction goes through a Mexican resident intermediary, are taxed at a preferential flat **10%** on the net gain.

**US stocks through a foreign broker:** that preferential rate does not carry over. Gains from selling US stocks - NYSE, NASDAQ, any foreign exchange - are treated as ordinary income and accumulated with your other income, taxed at Mexico's progressive individual rate schedule, from **1.92% up to 35%** (the top bracket starts at **$4,511,707.44 MXN** of annual income under the 2026 tariff published in Anexo 8 of the Resolución Miscelánea Fiscal, and the threshold is re-indexed for inflation most years - check the current DOF table rather than an older figure). For an investor already in a high income bracket from salary or business income, a US stock gain can be taxed at the full marginal rate - potentially 3.5x the BMV rate on the same-sized gain.

**No automatic withholding with a foreign broker.** Unlike a Mexican resident intermediary, which handles withholding and reporting automatically, a foreign broker like Interactive Brokers does not withhold Mexican tax or report to Mexico's tax authority (SAT) on your behalf. The investor is responsible for calculating the gain, converting to pesos at the appropriate exchange rate, and declaring and paying the tax directly - there's no passive mechanism doing this for you the way there is for BMV trades through a local broker.

**Who this applies to:** Mexican tax residents are taxed on worldwide income, including foreign capital gains, regardless of where the gain is realized or whether the proceeds ever reach a Mexican bank account. Non-residents are generally taxed only on Mexican-source income - a distinction that matters if your Mexican tax residency status itself is in question, which is a separate determination from citizenship.

## Dividends: 10% US, Then Mexican Tax

**US side:** 10% withheld with a valid W-8BEN.

**Mexican side:** foreign dividends are included in a Mexican tax resident's worldwide taxable income at the same progressive rate schedule that applies to capital gains, with a foreign tax credit generally available for the US withholding already paid, subject to the standard credit limitation (capped at the Mexican tax otherwise due on that income). As with capital gains, a foreign broker does not handle this calculation or filing for you - self-assessment applies.

Keep your broker's Form 1042-S (issued by March 15 each year) as your documentation of US withholding for the Mexican credit claim.

## Brokerage Options Open to Mexico Residents

Interactive Brokers and eToro each accept Mexican residents. Neither changes the treaty rate itself or the self-assessment obligation described above - those are functions of the treaty and Mexican domestic law respectively, not something broker choice affects.

---

## Your Next Steps as a Mexico Resident

**Working through it as a Mexican resident:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 10%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Do not assume the BMV's preferential 10% capital gains rate applies to US stock gains - it doesn't; they're taxed as ordinary income at your progressive marginal rate, up to 35%
- [ ] Track and self-report gains and dividends to SAT - a foreign broker does not withhold or report Mexican tax on your behalf the way a local intermediary would
- [ ] Convert gains and dividends to pesos at the appropriate exchange rate for Mexican reporting purposes
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for US withholding using Form 1042-S as documentation
- [ ] Verify current onboarding requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro yourself

## Where Mexico Investors Stand

The treaty delivers a solid, standard result on dividends: 10% US withholding once W-8BEN is filed, among the better rates in this series. The part that actually requires planning is capital gains - the assumption that US stock gains get the same treatment as BMV trades is wrong and can mean a much higher effective tax rate than expected, and the self-assessment burden with a foreign broker means the calculation and filing responsibility sits entirely with the investor rather than being handled automatically.
