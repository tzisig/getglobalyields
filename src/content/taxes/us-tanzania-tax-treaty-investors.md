There's no US-Tanzania income tax treaty and no bilateral investment agreement either, so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a Tanzania-resident investor. On Tanzania's own side, capital gains aren't taxed at a special, lower rate the way many countries in this series structure it - **gains are folded into business or investment income and taxed at the standard 30% rate**, among the higher capital gains treatments covered in this series. A foreign tax credit is available for the US withholding already paid, but it's capped at the Tanzanian tax rate applicable to that same income.

This guide covers both pieces.

---

## No Treaty: Full 30% US Withholding

With no income tax treaty between the US and Tanzania, there's no treaty article to reduce the default rate. Every US dividend paid to a Tanzania-resident investor is withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** It won't bring the rate below 30%, but it certifies your non-US status to your broker and avoids the risk of default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account.

---

## Capital Gains: Taxed as Ordinary Income at 30%

Tanzania doesn't have a separate, preferential capital gains tax category for individuals - gains from disposing of an asset, including US stocks sold through a foreign broker, are included in business or investment income and taxed at the standard **30%** rate. There's no holding-period discount reducing this, unlike several countries covered elsewhere in this series.

**The foreign tax credit:** Tanzania grants resident individuals a credit for foreign income tax paid on foreign-sourced income - directly relevant here, since the US withholds at source on dividends (and, in principle, tax could apply to gains depending on how they're characterized). **The credit is capped at the amount of Tanzanian tax otherwise due on that same income** - so it can offset the Tanzanian liability but won't generate a refund beyond what Tanzania would have charged anyway. Given the US rate (30% on dividends) and the Tanzanian rate (30% general) are similar in magnitude here, the credit is likely to substantially offset double taxation on dividends specifically - confirm the exact calculation with a Tanzanian tax advisor.

## Dividends: 10% Is the Domestic Rate, Not Necessarily the Foreign One

Tanzania withholds tax on dividends paid to nonresidents at **10%** - relevant to a foreign investor receiving Tanzanian-source dividends, not directly to a Tanzanian resident's own US dividend income, which instead falls under the general worldwide-income and foreign-tax-credit framework described above rather than this specific nonresident rate. **Confirm with a Tanzanian tax advisor exactly how your US dividends are classified and taxed domestically**, since general guidance addresses the nonresident-inbound case more clearly than the resident-receiving-foreign-dividends case.

---

## A Note on the Shilling

The Tanzanian shilling floats against the US dollar rather than being pegged or dollarized, and real search interest in "Tanzania shilling exchange rate" suggests Tanzanian investors already track it closely when planning currency conversions for a brokerage account - worth checking current rates directly before a large transfer, given the shilling doesn't have the fixed-rate stability some other currencies covered in this series offer.

- [ ] Budget for Tanzania's 30% tax on capital gains from US stock sales, treated as ordinary business/investment income with no holding-period discount

## Bottom Line

There's no US-Tanzania tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN still matters for documentation, not for a lower rate. The standout fact on Tanzania's side is the 30% capital gains rate - among the highest covered in this series, with no preferential treatment or holding-period relief - offset in part by a foreign tax credit for the US withholding already paid, capped at Tanzania's own tax rate on the same income.
