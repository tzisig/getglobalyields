The UAE has no income tax treaty with the United States. For dividends from US stocks, the full **30%** default withholding applies - there's no reduced rate to claim with [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/), regardless of how long you've lived in Dubai or Abu Dhabi.

That's the one genuinely unfavorable number in this picture. Everything else works in a UAE resident's favor: the UAE itself levies **no personal income tax, no capital gains tax, and no dividend tax**. For most UAE-resident investors, that 30% US withholding is the entire tax bill on US stock returns - there's no second layer of domestic tax stacked on top of it the way almost every other country in this series has.

This guide covers that combination, plus the practical account-access question: both Interactive Brokers and eToro accept UAE residents directly.

---

## No Treaty, No Domestic Tax Either - What That Nets Out To

**US side:** 30% withholding on dividends, always. Interest from US sources faces the same 30% default, subject to the same domestic IRC §871(k) exemptions available to any non-treaty country for interest-related and short-term capital gain distributions from US-domiciled funds - see the [US dividend withholding guide](/taxes/taxes-us-dividends-foreign-investors/) for how those work. Capital gains realized by a non-resident alien on US securities are generally not taxed by the US at all under domestic law (IRC §871), independent of treaty status.

**UAE side:** nothing. The UAE does not impose personal income tax on individuals, and there is no capital gains tax or dividend tax for individual investors. This means a UAE resident's after-tax outcome on US capital gains is the full gain, untaxed by either country - and on dividends, the 30% US withholding is the only layer, with no UAE tax added on top and, correspondingly, no foreign tax credit needed on the UAE side since there's no UAE tax to credit against.

**The comparison worth making explicitly:** a UAE resident nets a worse outcome than a 15%-treaty-country investor on dividends specifically (70% net vs. 85% net on the same gross dividend), but a comparable or better outcome than many treaty-country investors once domestic tax is factored in, since most treaty countries still tax the dividend at the resident's marginal rate on top of the reduced US withholding. The UAE's zero domestic layer is doing real work here, even though the treaty side of the equation is the least favorable in this series.

## Broker Access: Interactive Brokers and eToro Both Work

Interactive Brokers and eToro both open accounts for UAE residents, making the UAE one of the more straightforward countries in this series on pure access grounds - unlike, for example, [Uganda](/taxes/us-uganda-tax-treaty-investors/), where eToro specifically does not accept residents.

**A reporting point worth understanding, not a tax one:** Interactive Brokers, like other major brokers, participates in FATCA and CRS international reporting frameworks. This means account information can be reported to the tax authorities of a client's country of citizenship, not just their country of residence. A UAE resident who holds citizenship elsewhere - a common situation given the UAE's large expatriate population - should confirm what their citizenship country's tax obligations actually are, since UAE tax-residency status alone doesn't determine what a US citizen, for instance, still owes the IRS regardless of where they live.

---

## W-8BEN: Still Required, No Rate Benefit

Form W-8BEN goes to your broker, not the IRS. Without a treaty, it doesn't reduce the 30% withholding rate the way it does in treaty countries - but it still certifies your non-US person status and prevents the backup withholding regime from applying - a lower 24% rate, but one that can reach gross sale proceeds, not just dividends, so filing it correctly remains worthwhile even though the number on your dividend statement won't improve.

## The UAE Investor's Running List

**The UAE investor's short list:**
- [ ] Expect 30% dividend withholding with no treaty reduction available - file W-8BEN anyway to certify status and avoid backup withholding
- [ ] Confirm whether any US bond ETF distributions qualify for the IRC §871(k) interest-related dividend exemption
- [ ] Remember the UAE itself imposes no tax on the resulting dividends, interest, or capital gains - there's no second domestic layer to plan around
- [ ] If you hold citizenship in another country, confirm that country's tax obligations separately - UAE tax residency doesn't override a citizenship-based tax system elsewhere (most relevantly, US citizens and Green Card holders remain fully taxable by the US regardless of UAE residence)
- [ ] Both Interactive Brokers and eToro currently accept UAE residents - confirm current account-opening requirements directly, since broker policies can change

## The Short Version for the UAE

The lack of a US-UAE tax treaty caps out at a fixed, real cost: 30% withholding on US dividends, with nothing to optimize via W-8BEN. But the UAE's own zero-tax structure on income, dividends, and capital gains means that 30% is, for most residents, the entire tax bill on US stock investing - not one layer among several. Combined with straightforward account access at both Interactive Brokers and eToro, the UAE ends up a more workable base for US stock investing than the "no treaty" label alone would suggest.
