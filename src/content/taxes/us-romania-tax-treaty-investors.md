Romania's own domestic dividend withholding tax has climbed sharply in a short window: **8%** through 2024, up to **10%** for dividends paid from January 1, 2025, and up again to **16%** effective January 1, 2026. This is Romania's domestic rate - the rate that shapes how Romania taxes the Romanian side of the picture once a US dividend actually reaches a Romanian resident - and it's now meaningfully higher than the **10% US treaty withholding rate** on the same dividend's US side, a gap that didn't exist as recently as two years ago.

This guide covers both numbers clearly, since they answer different questions, and the recent Romanian-side increase changes the combined math for a Romanian investor in US stocks more than anything happening on the US side.

---

## Two Separate 10%s, and a New 16%

**The US-Romania tax treaty** (dating to 1973 and still the operative agreement) caps US withholding on dividends paid to Romanian residents at **10%** - claimed with Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/). Without it, the full 30% non-treaty default applies.

**Romania's own domestic dividend tax** - separate from the US treaty rate, and the rate that applies once the dividend reaches Romania and is assessed there - has moved in two steps recently: from 8% (through 2024) to 10% (dividends paid from January 1, 2025), and then to **16%** (effective January 1, 2026). This domestic rate applies broadly to dividend income, and while the specifics of how it interacts with foreign-source dividends and the treaty credit for a Romanian resident deserve direct confirmation from a Romanian tax advisor, the headline change is clear: Romania's own layer of dividend taxation is now noticeably higher than it was even a year prior, and higher than the 10% US treaty rate itself.

**Why this matters for planning:** the foreign tax credit for the 10% US withholding offsets Romanian tax on the same dividend income, but with Romania's own rate now at 16% rather than 10% or 8%, the credit covers a smaller share of the total combined burden than it did before 2025. A Romanian investor evaluating US dividend income today should use the current 16% domestic figure, not an older 8% or 10% assumption that may appear in less recently updated material.

## Dividends Under the Romania Treaty: 10%

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **10%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Romania Income Tax Convention (1973); IRS treaty text and technical explanation.*

The treaty gives you 10%; Form W-8BEN is how you actually take it. Payments made before the filing takes effect are withheld at the full 30%.

---

## What the 16% Did to the Credit

Romania's domestic dividend rate has moved twice in two years, and the effect on a Romanian investor is not the headline rate - it is how much of the total the US credit still absorbs.

On a **$5,000** US dividend, with W-8BEN filed so US withholding sits at the treaty's 10%:

| Romanian domestic rate | US withheld | Romanian tax due after credit | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8% (through 2024) | $500 | $0 - credit exceeds it | **$500** (10%) |
| 10% (2025) | $500 | $0 - credit exactly covers it | **$500** (10%) |
| **16% (from 2026)** | $500 | **$300** | **$800** (16%) |

Under the old 8% rate the US credit more than covered the Romanian charge, so the effective cost was the 10% treaty rate and nothing more. At 16%, the credit covers only ten of those sixteen points and the investor writes a cheque for the remaining six.

**The practical instruction is narrow and specific:** any calculation using 8% or 10% for the Romanian side is now wrong, and understates the bill by roughly six percentage points of every dividend. Material published before 2025 will still show the old figures.

## Romania: The Balance of It

The US treaty side is fixed and simple: 10% on dividends, claimed with W-8BEN. The moving part is Romania's own domestic dividend tax, which has climbed from 8% to 16% in the space of about two years - a change worth incorporating into any estimate of what a Romanian resident actually nets from US dividend income, since older sources describing the pre-2025 rates no longer reflect current law.
