There's no US-Dominican Republic income tax treaty, so the full **30% US statutory withholding** applies to every US dividend paid to a DR-resident investor. The Dominican Republic runs a territorial tax system like several neighbors covered in this series - but with a real, ticking difference: **foreign-source income, including US dividends and capital gains, is exempt only for a new resident's first three tax years.** Starting the fourth year, the Dominican Republic taxes worldwide income, and that US investment income becomes subject to Dominican tax for the first time.

This guide covers both phases clearly.

---

## No Treaty: Full 30% US Withholding

With no income tax treaty between the US and the Dominican Republic, there's no treaty article to reduce the default rate. Every US dividend paid to a DR-resident investor is withheld at the full **30% statutory rate**.

**Filing Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) is still worth doing.** It won't bring the rate below 30%, but it certifies your non-US status to your broker and avoids the risk of default backup withholding treatment on an undocumented account.

---

## Years 1-3: Foreign Income Exempt Under Ley 11-92

Under the Dominican Tax Code (Ley 11-92), an individual who becomes a Dominican tax resident is exempt from Dominican tax on foreign-source income - dividends and capital gains from a US brokerage account included - for their **first three tax years** of residency. Tax residency itself is triggered by spending more than **182 days** (consecutive or not) in the Dominican Republic during a fiscal year.

**During this window, the 30% US withholding is the entire tax cost** of investing in US stocks from the DR - there's no domestic layer to add.

---

## Year 4 Onward: Worldwide Income Taxation Kicks In

Once a resident passes the three-year mark, the exemption ends, and **foreign-source income becomes taxable as part of worldwide income** - at the Dominican Republic's progressive individual tax rates, up to **25%**:

| Taxable Income (DOP) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to 330,301 | 0% |
| 330,301.01 - 495,450 | 15% |
| 495,450.01 - 688,125 | 20% |
| Over 688,125.01 | 25% |

**What this means for US dividends and capital gains after year three:** they get folded into your total taxable income and taxed at whichever bracket that total lands in - potentially up to 25% - on top of the 30% US withholding already applied at source. This is a genuinely different structure from a country that's either always territorial or always worldwide-income: the Dominican Republic changes categories partway through your residency, on a fixed, predictable schedule.

**Confirm the specific credit mechanics with a Dominican tax advisor** for how the 30% already withheld by the US factors into your Dominican liability once the exemption period ends, since there's no treaty defining this and domestic relief provisions should be checked directly.

## Bottom Line

There's no US-Dominican Republic tax treaty, so the full 30% US withholding applies with no reduction available - W-8BEN still matters for documentation, not for a lower rate. What sets the Dominican Republic apart from the purely territorial neighbors covered elsewhere in this series is the three-year clock: foreign dividends and capital gains are genuinely tax-free for new residents at first, but that changes automatically once you cross into your fourth tax year, when worldwide income taxation - up to 25% - applies to the same US investment income going forward.
