When looking for the [best broker in Israel for US stocks](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-israel-step-by-step-2026/), ETFs, or options strategies, your biggest cost usually isn't commissions - it's what adds up quietly in the background: currency conversion, [dividend withholding](/taxes/taxes-us-dividends-foreign-investors/), spreads, and how a broker handles idle cash. Small differences here can cost hundreds to thousands of dollars a year, and they rarely show up on a fee schedule's headline number.

This comparison covers the three platforms most commonly searched by [Israeli](/taxes/us-israel-tax-treaty-explained-capital-gains-dividends-2026/) investors: Interactive Brokers, Saxo Bank, and eToro.

*⚠️ Important Disclaimer: Tax treatment depends on your personal situation and may vary by broker structure and jurisdiction. Always verify details with a qualified tax advisor.*

## Interactive Brokers (IBKR): Best Overall for Israeli Investors

*Best for: active investors, options traders, portfolios above roughly $10K-$25K*

**Key advantages:**

- **Currency conversion:** IBKR's manual conversion route (IDEALPRO) runs at roughly 0.002% - effectively the interbank rate, with a $2 minimum. This is the rate to use for any conversion above a few hundred dollars. IBKR's *automatic* conversion (triggered when you buy an asset in a currency you don't already hold) adds a separate markup of roughly 0.03% - still cheap relative to most brokers, but noticeably worse than the manual route for larger amounts. Convert manually via IDEALPRO before a large purchase rather than letting the platform convert automatically.
- **Full access to US markets:** stocks, ETFs, options, and futures - the same access American investors get.
- **Options trading support:** multi-leg strategies, real-time Greeks, low commissions. If you're using strategies like [covered calls](/options-income/selling-covered-calls-tqqq/), [TQQQ](/taxes/tqqq-tax-international-investors/) positions, or cash-secured puts, this matters directly.

**Estate tax note:** non-US investors holding US-domiciled ETFs like [TQQQ](/strategies/buying-tqqq-vs-qqq-which-is-right-for-international-investors-in-2026/) may be exposed to US estate tax on assets above roughly $60,000 - a real consideration for long-term holders, independent of which broker is used. Confirm the current threshold and your specific exposure with a tax advisor before building large positions.

**Downsides:** the Trader Workstation (TWS) platform has a real learning curve and isn't the easiest entry point for a complete beginner.

## Saxo Bank: Premium Alternative

*Best for: large portfolios, investors prioritizing stability and institutional infrastructure*

**Strengths:** a regulated European bank ([Denmark](/taxes/us-denmark-tax-treaty-investors/)), strong custody and infrastructure, and access to global markets beyond just the US.

**Weaknesses:** higher commissions than IBKR, roughly 0.25% FX conversion cost - meaningfully higher than IBKR's manual rate - and less competitive for active or frequent trading.

Saxo is positioned closer to a wealth management platform than an active trading one. It suits investors who prioritize institutional stability over minimizing cost.

## eToro: Entry-Level Platform

*Best for: beginners, small accounts, passive investors*

**Strengths:** simple interface, copy trading features, easy onboarding.

**Limitations:** no options trading, wider spreads (a hidden cost per trade), and less control over execution than a platform like IBKR.

**Tax handling - an important nuance:** for Israeli investors, eToro applies the [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) treaty rate (25%, per the US-Israel treaty - not 15%, a figure sometimes seen in outdated guides) on dividends from shares held directly. eToro offers both real shares and CFDs - if you hold a CFD, you don't own the underlying security, and the tax treatment differs. Always confirm which product type a given position actually is before assuming the 25% treaty rate applies to it.

"Zero commission" doesn't mean free - the cost shows up in spreads and pricing. Suitable for getting started, less so for advanced strategies.

## The Hidden Cost Categories, By the Numbers

**Currency conversion:** IBKR (~0.002% manual, ~0.03% automatic) < Saxo (~0.25%) < eToro (spread-based, often the highest of the three).

**Dividends:** 25% US withholding applies across all three platforms for Israeli investors with a valid W-8BEN - this is a function of the US-Israel treaty, not something any broker can improve on. It's unavoidable on dividend-paying positions, more impactful on [high-yield ETFs](/strategies/best-high-yield-etfs-2026/) than on low-yield holdings like TQQQ.

**Spreads:** matter most on eToro, where they substitute for a visible commission and increase the effective cost per trade.

**Cash and interest:** brokers handle uninvested cash differently - some pay meaningful interest on idle balances, others don't. This affects total return on cash sitting in the account and is worth checking directly with each platform, since policies change.

**Choose Interactive Brokers if:** you trade options, want to minimize FX and commission costs, and want full control over execution.
