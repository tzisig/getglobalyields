A Spanish tax resident with a US brokerage account worth more than **€50,000** at any point in the year has a filing obligation that has nothing to do with the treaty rate: **Modelo 720**, Spain's foreign asset declaration, due every March 31. Filing it doesn't itself generate a tax bill - but not filing it, or filing it incorrectly, has historically carried some of the harshest penalty exposure of any reporting requirement covered in this series, and it's a rule many new Spanish residents genuinely don't know applies to an ordinary US brokerage account.

This guide covers Modelo 720's actual mechanics, the treaty's standard 15% dividend rate (confirmed by the 2019 Protocol), and Spain's progressive savings income tax scale that applies to the resulting dividends and capital gains.

---

## Modelo 720: The Reporting Obligation Most New Residents Miss

Modelo 720 requires Spanish tax residents to declare foreign-held assets across three separate categories, each assessed independently against a **€50,000** threshold:

- **Category C1:** foreign bank accounts
- **Category C2:** foreign investments and pensions - this is where a US brokerage account holding stocks and ETFs falls
- **Category C3:** foreign real estate

If your US brokerage account (Category C2) exceeds €50,000 at any point, you must file Modelo 720 by **March 31** of the following year - no extensions. Once you've filed, you generally don't need to re-file every subsequent year unless a category's value changes by more than **€20,000**, or you acquire or dispose of assets within that category.

**What Modelo 720 does not do:** it's a disclosure requirement, not a tax return - filing it correctly does not by itself create a tax liability. The actual tax on your dividends and capital gains is calculated separately under Spain's ordinary income tax rules, described below. The risk with Modelo 720 is procedural: missing the deadline or filing incorrectly, not the disclosure itself.

**Why this matters more than it might seem:** a US brokerage account is an obvious candidate for Category C2, but investors moving to Spain for the first time frequently aren't aware the obligation exists at all, since it's not tied to any specific tax bill and easy to miss amid other relocation paperwork. Confirm your filing obligation with a Spanish tax advisor (gestor or asesor fiscal) as soon as your account crosses the threshold, rather than waiting until a filing deadline is imminent.

## The Treaty: 15% on Dividends, Confirmed by the 2019 Protocol

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) | Direct Corporate (10%+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **15%** | **5%** (0% for qualifying 80%+ parent-subsidiary holdings) |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Spain Income Tax Convention, Protocol entered into force November 27, 2019; IRS treaty text.*

The 2019 Protocol modernized the original treaty and reduced or eliminated withholding on several income categories. For an individual portfolio investor, the operative rate is **15%**, claimed with Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/). Without it, the full 30% default applies from the first dividend payment.

---

## Spain's Savings Income Scale: Progressive, Not Flat

Dividends and capital gains from US stocks fall under Spain's **renta del ahorro** (savings income) category, taxed on a progressive scale for 2026:

| Savings Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to €6,000 | **19%** |
| €6,000 - €50,000 | **21%** |
| €50,000 - €200,000 | **23%** |
| €200,000 - €300,000 | **27%** |
| Above €300,000 | **30%** |

*Source: PwC Spain Tax Summaries - Individual Taxes on Personal Income (2026).*

This is a genuine progressive scale, not a flat rate - dividends and capital gains are combined into the same savings-income base and taxed through these brackets together, with a foreign tax credit available for the 15% US withholding already paid on dividends, capped at the Spanish tax otherwise due on that income.

## Broker Access

Both Interactive Brokers and eToro are confirmed to accept Spanish residents for account opening. Neither changes the Modelo 720 filing obligation, which is triggered by account value regardless of which broker holds the assets.

## Practical Checklist

**Spanish resident investing in US stocks:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify 15%, not 30%, on dividend statements
- [ ] Track your US brokerage account value against the €50,000 Modelo 720 threshold for Category C2
- [ ] File Modelo 720 by March 31 if the threshold is crossed, and re-file if the value changes by more than €20,000 in a later year
- [ ] Report US dividends and capital gains under Spain's progressive savings income scale (19%-30%), combining both income types into the same base
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for the 15% US withholding on dividends
- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro before applying

## Bottom Line

The treaty rate is settled and favorable - 15% on dividends, confirmed by the 2019 Protocol modernization. The detail most likely to catch a Spanish resident off guard is procedural, not a tax rate at all: Modelo 720's €50,000 reporting threshold applies to an ordinary US brokerage account, and missing it is a compliance risk independent of how much tax is actually owed. Combined with Spain's progressive savings income scale reaching 30% at the top, getting both the treaty mechanics and the reporting obligation right is worth real attention for anyone relocating to Spain with an existing US investment portfolio.
