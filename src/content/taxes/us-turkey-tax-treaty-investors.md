Worth correcting directly, since it's a common mix-up: the US-Turkey tax treaty caps dividend withholding for an **individual portfolio investor at 20%**, not 15%. The lower 15% rate in Article 10 is reserved for corporate shareholders owning at least 10% of the paying company - a threshold that doesn't describe an individual retail investor, who instead gets the higher 20% figure. It's an easy number to get backwards, since 15% is the far more common individual rate across this treaty series generally, and some general reference tables cite it for Turkey without the ownership qualifier attached.

This guide covers that 20% rate accurately, along with how Turkish tax residents are taxed on the resulting foreign investment income, and broker access for Turkish residents using Interactive Brokers or eToro.

---

## The Treaty: 20% on Dividends for Individuals

| Income Type | Default US Rate | Treaty Rate (Individual/Portfolio) | Direct Corporate (10%+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dividends | 30% | **20%** | **15%** |
| Capital gains (securities) | Generally not US-taxable for non-residents | Residence country only | Residence country only |

*Sources: US-Turkey Income Tax Treaty (1996); IRS treaty text; JCT explanation of the treaty.*

Claiming the 20% rate (instead of the 30% default) still requires Form [W-8BEN](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/) filed with your broker - the mechanism is identical to every other country in this series, even though the resulting rate is less favorable than the 15% most European investors receive. Without W-8BEN, the full 30% applies from the first dividend payment; check your dividend statement to confirm which rate is actually withheld, since 20% is the correct figure to look for here, not 15%.

## Turkish Tax on the Resulting Income

Turkish tax residents are taxed on worldwide income, including foreign dividends and capital gains from US stocks, under Turkey's progressive income tax structure for individuals. A foreign tax credit is generally available for the US withholding already paid, subject to the standard credit limitation (capped at the Turkish tax otherwise due on that same income) - the same structural principle used across most treaty countries in this series, applied here against a higher starting US withholding rate than most.

Given Turkey's history of significant currency volatility against the US dollar, tracking both the USD-denominated gain or dividend and its TRY-equivalent value at the relevant dates matters more here than in many other countries covered in this series - confirm with a Turkish tax advisor (mali müşavir) exactly how currency conversion is handled for foreign investment income reporting, since exchange rate mechanics can materially affect the calculated Turkish tax base independent of the underlying US-dollar performance of your holdings.

## Why the 15% You Read Elsewhere Is Not Yours

The single most consequential fact for a Turkish investor is a number that is wrong nearly everywhere it appears. The US-Turkey treaty gives **20%** to portfolio investors; the **15%** widely quoted belongs to corporate holders meeting an ownership threshold no individual reaches.

On **$10,000** of annual dividends, the three figures produce three different outcomes:

| Rate applied | Withheld | Reaching you |
|---|---|---|
| 30% - no W-8BEN filed | $3,000 | $7,000 |
| **20% - your actual treaty rate** | **$2,000** | **$8,000** |
| 15% - the rate you may have been told | $1,500 | $8,500 |

**Filing W-8BEN is worth $1,000 a year on this portfolio.** Expecting 15% and receiving 20% is worth another $500 of disappointment that no paperwork resolves.

**How to check rather than assume:** your dividend statement shows the rate actually applied. If it reads 20%, the treaty is working correctly and there is nothing further to claim. If it reads 30%, the form is missing or lapsed. If it reads 15%, something is wrong on the broker's side and is likely to be corrected later - a correction that arrives as a bill.

## What Needs Doing Before You Invest from Turkey

**What a Turkish investor needs to have covered:**
- [ ] Confirm W-8BEN is on file with your broker; verify **20%**, not 30% and not 15%, on dividend statements - 15% is a common but incorrect figure for individual investors under this specific treaty
- [ ] Renew W-8BEN before the three-year expiry
- [ ] Report US dividends and capital gains as part of worldwide income on your Turkish tax return
- [ ] Claim the foreign tax credit for the 20% US withholding already paid, capped at the Turkish tax otherwise due
- [ ] Track TRY-equivalent values carefully given currency volatility - confirm the correct conversion methodology with a Turkish tax advisor
- [ ] Verify current onboarding requirements with Interactive Brokers or eToro yourself

## Stepping Back from the Turkey Detail

The single most important correction in this guide is the rate itself: individual Turkish investors get 20% under the US-Turkey treaty, not the 15% commonly (and incorrectly) cited elsewhere. Beyond that, the mechanics are standard - worldwide taxation for Turkish residents with a foreign tax credit for the US withholding - with currency volatility as the practical complication worth planning around given Turkey's economic history.
