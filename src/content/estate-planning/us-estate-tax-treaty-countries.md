Every country-specific guide on this site's [taxes category](/taxes/) covers income tax treaties - a network the US maintains with dozens of countries. Estate tax treaties are a genuinely different, much shorter list. If your country of residence has one, it can meaningfully change your estate tax exposure beyond the standard [$60,000 non-resident alien exemption](/estate-planning/us-estate-tax-non-resident-aliens-60000-exemption/). If it doesn't, the base rules apply without modification.

---

## The Countries With US Estate Tax Treaties

As of 2026, the US maintains estate tax treaties with a relatively short list of countries, including Australia, Austria, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands, Norway, South Africa, Sweden, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom. **This is a meaningfully shorter list than the income tax treaty network** covered throughout this site's country-specific guides - a country having a US income tax treaty does not mean it also has a US estate tax treaty, and the two lists should not be assumed to match.

**Confirm your specific country's status directly** rather than assuming either way based on its income tax treaty status - the estate tax treaty network was negotiated separately and covers a distinctly smaller set of countries.

---

## What Estate Tax Treaties Generally Do

Estate tax treaties exist for the same fundamental reason income tax treaties do: preventing the same assets from being taxed in full by two countries at the same time. The specific mechanics vary by treaty, but commonly include:

- **Clarifying domicile** for estate tax purposes, resolving cases where both countries might otherwise claim taxing rights
- **In some treaties, allowing a pro-rated exemption** based on the ratio of US-situs assets to the decedent's total worldwide estate - potentially delivering meaningfully more relief than the flat $60,000 non-resident alien exemption, depending on the specific estate's composition
- **Reducing or eliminating double taxation** where both the US and the treaty partner country might otherwise impose estate-type tax on the same assets

**This guide is not in a position to specify the exact mechanics for each individual treaty** - they genuinely differ from one agreement to the next, and applying the wrong one incorrectly (or assuming a generic treaty benefit that your specific treaty doesn't actually provide) is a real risk. If your country is on the treaty list, this is a case for a cross-border estate planning attorney to confirm the specific provisions that apply to your situation, not a generic summary.

## If Your Country Isn't on the List

**The standard $60,000 exemption and rates up to 40%, covered in our [dedicated guide](/estate-planning/us-estate-tax-non-resident-aliens-60000-exemption/), apply without treaty modification.** This describes the majority of countries covered throughout this site's country-specific [us-investing](/us-investing/) and [taxes](/taxes/) guides - most readers of this site are in countries without a US estate tax treaty, making the base exemption and rate structure, along with the mitigation approaches covered in our [avoidance strategies guide](/estate-planning/avoiding-us-estate-tax-brokerage-account/), the relevant planning framework.
