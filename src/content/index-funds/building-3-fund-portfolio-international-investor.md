The classic 3-fund portfolio - a US total stock market fund, an international stock fund, and a bond fund - is one of the most widely recommended simple portfolio structures in personal finance content. It was designed around a US resident's financial position, and adapting it thoughtfully for an international investor means reconsidering what each of the three pieces is actually for, not simply substituting equivalent tickers.

---

## The Original 3-Fund Logic

The standard version combines a US total market fund (like [VTI](/index-funds/vti-total-market-etf-review/)), an international stock fund (like [VXUS](/index-funds/vxus-international-diversification/)), and a bond fund (like the [BND/AGG options](/index-funds/bond-etfs-compared-bnd-agg-tlt/) covered elsewhere in this category) in proportions matched to the investor's risk tolerance and time horizon - genuine diversification across geography and asset class in exactly three holdings, simple enough to maintain without constant attention.

---

## Rethinking the "International" Piece

**This is the part that genuinely changes for this site's readers, as covered in detail in our [VXUS guide](/index-funds/vxus-international-diversification/).** For a US resident, the "international" fund provides real diversification away from an otherwise US-concentrated financial life. For an investor who already lives outside the US, the "US" fund in this structure is already the internationally diversifying piece relative to their home-country economic exposure - the standard framework's logic needs to be applied to your actual starting position, not imported unchanged.

**A reasonable adaptation:** think of the three funds not as "US / International / Bonds" but as "primary equity exposure / additional geographic diversification / stability," and decide the actual proportions based on how concentrated your total financial life already is in your home country versus the US, not by defaulting to a standard US-resident allocation split.

---

## Rethinking the "Bond" Piece

**Bond ETF selection benefits from the specific guidance covered in our [bond ETF comparison](/index-funds/bond-etfs-compared-bnd-agg-tlt/)** - BND or AGG for a genuinely diversified, moderate-duration stability role, rather than reaching for a higher-yielding but more volatile option like TLT if genuine portfolio stability is the goal.

**The favorable withholding treatment** covered in that same guide - most bond fund interest income arrives with reduced or zero US withholding under the portfolio interest exemption - makes the bond allocation a genuinely tax-efficient piece of an international investor's portfolio, worth confirming on your actual statements.

---

## Rethinking Fund Domicile

**For EU/UK investors, all three pieces of a 3-fund portfolio need to be sourced from UCITS-compliant equivalents**, covered in detail in our [UCITS vs US-domiciled comparison](/index-funds/ucits-vs-us-domiciled-etfs-tax-comparison/) - direct US-domiciled versions of VTI, VXUS, and BND are not accessible. For investors outside the EU/UK, this is a genuine choice rather than a forced one, with the same UCITS considerations (withholding rate, estate tax situs) applying as covered throughout that guide.

---

## A Worked Example Structure

**For an investor with a long time horizon and high risk tolerance:** 60% primary US equity exposure, 25% additional international/home-region diversification, 15% bonds - adjusted based on your specific circumstances rather than treated as a universal formula.

**For an investor closer to a planned withdrawal phase:** shift progressively toward the bond allocation, following the same de-risking logic covered in our [sequence of returns risk guide](/strategies/sequence-of-returns-risk-leveraged-etf-withdrawals/) and [life-event exit planning guide](/strategies/exit-strategy-life-events-leveraged-etf/) - the general principle of reducing volatile exposure as a known need approaches applies to a 3-fund portfolio exactly as it does to a leveraged position.

---

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Choose your three funds based on your actual home-country financial exposure, not a default US-resident allocation split
- [ ] Select bond exposure specifically for stability (BND/AGG) rather than yield-chasing (TLT), unless you have a specific reason to want long-duration exposure
- [ ] If in the EU/UK, source all three pieces from UCITS-compliant equivalents
- [ ] Reassess your allocation proportions as you approach any known withdrawal need, following the same de-risking principles covered elsewhere on this site
- [ ] Keep the structure genuinely simple - the entire appeal of a 3-fund portfolio is low maintenance, which is undermined by adding complexity back in

---

## Bottom Line

A 3-fund portfolio remains a genuinely sound, low-maintenance structure for an international investor - but each piece deserves reconsideration from your actual starting position rather than a direct import of US-resident advice. Your "US" fund may already be your international diversification; your bond fund should be chosen for stability specifically; and your fund domicile choice depends on where you live, not a universal default.
