References to UCITS ETFs versus their US-domiciled equivalents are scattered throughout this site's European [us-investing guides](/us-investing/) and this [estate planning category](/estate-planning/) - this article pulls the comparison together in one place, since the differences between a fund like CSPX (Irish-domiciled, S&P 500) and VOO (US-domiciled, identical index) are genuinely substantial across three separate dimensions, not just the access question most guides lead with.

---

## Access: Where the Comparison Usually Starts

For EU and UK investors, the PRIIPs regulation blocks direct retail access to US-domiciled ETFs entirely - covered throughout this site's European country guides. This is the reason UCITS ETFs enter the conversation for those investors in the first place. **For investors outside the EU/UK, this access restriction doesn't apply**, and US-domiciled ETFs are directly available - meaning the rest of this comparison is a genuine choice, not a forced one, for the majority of this site's readers.

---

## Dividend Withholding: A Real, Structural Difference

**US-domiciled ETF (VOO):** the fund itself, as a US entity, receives dividends from its US stock holdings with no withholding. It then distributes to you, subject to the standard 30%-or-treaty-rate withholding described throughout this site's [taxes category](/taxes/) - 15% for most treaty countries, more or less depending on your specific country.

**UCITS ETF (CSPX):** the fund receives US dividends from its underlying stocks subject to the US-Ireland treaty's 15% withholding rate, applied at the fund level. Ireland itself does not withhold on the fund's distributions to non-Irish residents in most cases. **The practical result: your effective US withholding is capped at 15% through the fund structure, regardless of your own country's specific treaty rate with the US** - even if your country's own treaty rate would otherwise be higher or non-existent.

**Who this favors:** investors from countries with a worse-than-15% treaty rate, or no treaty at all - covered throughout this site's country guides for places like Israel (25%), Brazil (30%, no treaty), or the UAE (30%, no treaty). For these investors, a UCITS ETF can deliver a meaningfully better effective US withholding rate than holding the equivalent US-domiciled fund directly, entirely independent of the PRIIPs access question.

**Who this doesn't help:** investors from countries with a treaty rate already at or below 15% - Japan (10%) or Mexico (10%), for example - where the US-domiciled fund's direct treaty rate is already as good as or better than the UCITS fund's embedded 15%.

---

## Estate Tax: A Second, Separate Advantage

**As covered in detail in our [avoiding US estate tax guide](/estate-planning/avoiding-us-estate-tax-brokerage-account/), a non-US-domiciled fund like CSPX is generally not treated as a US-situs asset for US estate tax purposes, while a US-domiciled fund like VOO is.** This is a genuinely separate, structural benefit from the withholding question above - relevant even to investors whose treaty rate makes the withholding comparison a wash, if estate tax exposure above the $60,000 non-resident alien exemption is a concern.

---

## Cost: The Trade-Off Working Against UCITS Funds

UCITS ETFs generally carry somewhat higher expense ratios than their US-domiciled equivalents - CSPX's expense ratio, for example, runs higher than VOO's, though both remain low by historical standards. This is a real, ongoing cost that needs to be weighed against the withholding and estate tax advantages above, not ignored.

---

## The Full Comparison, Summarized

| | US-Domiciled (VOO) | UCITS (CSPX) |
|---|---|---|
| EU/UK retail access | Blocked (PRIIPs) | Available |
| US withholding | Your country's treaty rate | Capped at 15% via US-Ireland treaty |
| US estate tax situs | Yes | Generally no |
| Expense ratio | Lower | Somewhat higher |

---

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] If you're in the EU or UK, UCITS access is not optional - confirm which specific UCITS funds are available on your broker
- [ ] If outside the EU/UK, compare your country's actual treaty rate against the UCITS fund's embedded 15% rate before assuming either option is automatically better
- [ ] Factor in estate tax exposure separately from the withholding comparison - a UCITS fund can be worth it for estate tax reasons even if withholding rates are similar
- [ ] Weigh the modest additional expense ratio against the combined withholding and estate tax benefits for your specific situation

---

## Bottom Line

The choice between UCITS and US-domiciled ETFs tracking the identical index is a genuine, three-dimensional trade-off - access (forced for EU/UK investors, optional elsewhere), dividend withholding (favors UCITS specifically for investors from high-withholding or no-treaty countries), and estate tax situs (favors UCITS broadly) - weighed against UCITS funds' modestly higher ongoing cost. For investors outside the EU/UK with a treaty rate already at or below 15%, the case for UCITS narrows to the estate tax consideration alone; for investors with a worse treaty rate, the case is genuinely stronger on both dimensions.
