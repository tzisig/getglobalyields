VOO, SPY, and IVV all track the exact same underlying index - the S&P 500 - which means their actual holdings and index performance are effectively identical. The differences that matter for choosing between them are cost, fund structure, and liquidity, not what's inside the fund.

---

## What Each Fund Actually Is

**SPY (SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust)** was the first US ETF ever launched and remains the most heavily traded, structured as a unit investment trust with a longer operating history than either competitor.

**VOO (Vanguard S&P 500 ETF)** is Vanguard's S&P 500 offering, generally carrying the lowest expense ratio of the three.

**IVV (iShares Core S&P 500 ETF)** is BlackRock/iShares' offering, with an expense ratio close to VOO's and meaningfully below SPY's.

---

## The Real Difference: Cost

**VOO and IVV both carry meaningfully lower expense ratios than SPY.** This is not a trivial rounding difference over a long holding period - a lower ongoing expense ratio compounds into real savings the longer you hold the position, with no offsetting benefit from SPY's higher fee for a buy-and-hold investor.

**Why SPY still commands enormous trading volume despite the higher cost:** its status as the original, most heavily traded S&P 500 ETF makes it the default choice for institutional and options traders who prioritize maximum liquidity and the deepest options market over the small ongoing cost difference - the same dynamic covered in our [QQQ vs QQQM comparison](/strategies/qqq-vs-qqqm-vs-vgt-comparison/) regarding QQQ's continued dominance despite QQQM's lower cost.

---

## Liquidity and Options Markets

**SPY has the deepest, most liquid options market of the three by a wide margin.** For an investor planning to run options income strategies (covered throughout our [options-income category](/options-income/)) against an S&P 500 position specifically, SPY's superior options liquidity can be worth its higher expense ratio, similar to the QQQ-vs-QQQM liquidity trade-off.

**For a pure buy-and-hold position with no options overlay**, VOO or IVV's lower cost wins with no meaningful downside, since the underlying index exposure is identical to SPY's.

---

## A Structural Note Worth Knowing

SPY's unit investment trust structure means it cannot reinvest dividends internally between distribution dates and has certain structural constraints VOO and IVV, as standard open-end ETFs, don't share - generally not a meaningful practical difference for most retail investors, but worth knowing the three aren't structurally identical products despite tracking the same index.

---

## Practical Guidance

**If your plan is pure buy-and-hold with no options trading:** VOO or IVV deliver identical S&P 500 exposure to SPY at a lower ongoing cost - choose between them based on which broker's platform you're using or minor preference, since their expense ratios are close.

**If you plan to run options strategies against your S&P 500 position:** SPY's superior liquidity is likely worth the higher expense ratio.

---

## Bottom Line

VOO, SPY, and IVV are functionally the same investment - identical index, identical underlying holdings - differentiated by cost (VOO and IVV lower) and liquidity (SPY deeper, particularly for options). Choose based on whether you're optimizing for cost as a long-term holder or liquidity as an active options trader, not by assuming any meaningful difference in the underlying exposure.
