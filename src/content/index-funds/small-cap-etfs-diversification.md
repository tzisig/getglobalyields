Small-cap ETFs like IJR (iShares Core S&P Small-Cap ETF) and VB (Vanguard Small-Cap ETF) hold hundreds of smaller US companies genuinely excluded from mega-cap-focused funds like [QQQ](/strategies/qqq-vs-qqqm-vs-vgt-comparison/) and largely underrepresented even in broad-market funds like [VOO](/index-funds/voo-vs-spy-vs-ivv-sp500-etf-comparison/), given how heavily market-cap weighting concentrates those funds in the largest names.

---

## Why Small-Caps Are Genuinely Different Exposure

**Market-cap-weighted funds concentrate heavily in the largest companies by design.** Even a fund technically including small-cap stocks, like [VTI](/index-funds/vti-total-market-etf-review/), allocates a small fraction of total weight to them relative to mega-cap names, since market-cap weighting means larger companies dominate the fund's actual exposure regardless of how many smaller companies are nominally included. A dedicated small-cap fund like IJR or VB is the only way to get meaningful, deliberate exposure to this segment specifically.

**The practical implication:** an investor holding QQQ, TQQQ, or even a broad fund like VOO is getting essentially no small-cap exposure, regardless of how "diversified" those funds might otherwise feel - genuine small-cap exposure requires a dedicated allocation.

---

## The Risk Profile Is Genuinely Different

**Small-cap stocks carry higher volatility than large-cap stocks**, generally reflecting less analyst coverage, less liquidity, more sensitivity to economic cycles, and less-established business models compared to mega-cap companies. This isn't a reason to avoid small-caps entirely, but it's a genuinely different risk profile than the large-cap-dominated core holdings covered throughout the rest of this category - not simply a smaller version of the same thing.

---

## The Historical Size Premium - and Why It's Not Guaranteed

**Small-cap stocks have, over long historical periods, shown a tendency to outperform large-cap stocks** - a documented pattern sometimes called the "size premium," part of the broader factor-investing literature. **This premium has been genuinely inconsistent in more recent periods**, with extended stretches where large-caps (particularly mega-cap technology names) have significantly outperformed small-caps instead. Treat the historical size premium as a documented pattern worth knowing about, not a reliable, guaranteed return driver to plan around with confidence.

---

## IJR vs VB: A Minor Distinction

IJR and VB both track broad small-cap indices with similar overall characteristics - the specific index methodology differs slightly between them (IJR uses S&P's small-cap index, VB uses a CRSP-based methodology), but the practical difference between the two for most investors is smaller than the difference between either fund and a large-cap-focused alternative.

---

## Practical Guidance

**A deliberate, sized small-cap allocation** can add genuine diversification value to a portfolio otherwise concentrated in large-cap names, given how different this segment's risk and return drivers are from the mega-cap-dominated core.

**Sizing matters** - given small-cap's higher volatility, a large allocation carries meaningfully more risk than the same percentage in a broad large-cap fund, worth factoring into your overall risk budget rather than treating it as a straightforward substitute for large-cap exposure.

---

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Recognize that broad market funds like VOO or VTI provide minimal genuine small-cap exposure, given market-cap weighting's concentration in large companies
- [ ] Size a dedicated small-cap allocation deliberately, accounting for its higher volatility relative to large-cap exposure
- [ ] Treat the historical size premium as a documented pattern, not a guaranteed future return driver
- [ ] Choose between IJR and VB based on minor index methodology preference rather than expecting a meaningful practical difference

---

## Bottom Line

Small-cap ETFs like IJR and VB provide genuinely different market exposure than the large-cap-dominated funds covered throughout the rest of this category - a real diversification tool, not a redundant addition to an already-diversified-feeling portfolio. The trade-off is real, higher volatility, and a historical outperformance pattern that has been inconsistent enough in recent years to treat as a documented tendency rather than a dependable planning assumption.
