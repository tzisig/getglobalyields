MSTR, MARA, and RIOT all show up on the same "how to get bitcoin exposure through a stock" lists, treated as roughly interchangeable ways to bet on bitcoin without holding it directly. They aren't interchangeable. One is a leveraged bitcoin holding company. The other two are operating mining businesses whose economics depend on electricity costs and network difficulty as much as bitcoin's price - and one of them is actively pivoting away from bitcoin mining altogether.

---

## MSTR (Strategy): A Leveraged Bitcoin Balance Sheet

The company formerly known as MicroStrategy rebranded to Strategy in February 2025, reflecting what it had already become: a bitcoin accumulation vehicle wrapped around a small legacy software business. As of early July 2026, Strategy holds 843,775 bitcoin, acquired at an average cost of roughly $66,385 per coin, for a total cost basis around $33.1 billion.

**The accumulation has been funded almost entirely through capital markets, not software revenue.** Strategy raised $25.3 billion in 2025 alone through a mix of convertible debt and at-the-market equity offerings - making it the largest equity issuer among all US public companies for a second consecutive year. The company currently carries roughly $8.2 billion in total debt, with about $6 billion of that in convertible notes collateralized by its bitcoin holdings. This is the core of what "MSTR as a bitcoin proxy" actually means: it's a leveraged wrapper, using borrowed and newly-issued capital to hold more bitcoin than an equivalent direct investment would, which amplifies both gains and losses relative to bitcoin's own price movement.

---

## The mNAV Reversal Most Coverage Still Doesn't Reflect

**Here's the current, real number that changes how this stock should actually be evaluated: as of early August 2026, Strategy trades at a discount to the value of its own bitcoin holdings, not a premium.** The metric that captures this - mNAV, or the ratio of the company's market value to the value of its bitcoin holdings - sat around 0.68-0.70x in early August 2026. A ratio below 1.0 means the market is valuing the entire company, software business included, at less than what its bitcoin alone is worth.

This is a genuine reversal, not a minor fluctuation. As recently as November 2024, MSTR traded at roughly 3.4x its bitcoin NAV - a 240% premium. Investors were willing to pay more than three dollars for a dollar of the company's underlying bitcoin, presumably valuing the leverage, the accumulation strategy, and the optionality of continued purchases. That premium evaporated in 2026, with the mNAV crossing below 1.0 on June 27, 2026.

**Why this matters beyond being an interesting data point:** Strategy's entire capital-raising model depends on trading at a premium to NAV. Issuing new shares to buy more bitcoin only benefits existing shareholders when those new shares sell for more than the bitcoin they'll buy - equivalent to a company issuing stock above book value. Once the stock trades at a discount, the same mechanism runs in reverse: issuing equity to buy bitcoin at a discounted share price dilutes existing holders' claim on the company's bitcoin per share, rather than growing it. A prolonged discount doesn't just make the stock cheaper - it undermines the specific mechanism that built the company's bitcoin position in the first place.

## MARA and RIOT: Operating Businesses, Not Balance Sheets

Marathon Digital (MARA) and Riot Platforms (RIOT) are structurally different animals. Both run actual bitcoin mining operations - data centers full of specialized hardware competing to solve the cryptographic puzzles that earn new bitcoin - which means their economics depend on real operating costs, not just bitcoin's price.

**The treadmill problem is built into how mining works.** MARA's hash rate reached 72.2 exahashes per second by the end of Q1 2026, up 33% year over year - a real expansion of mining capacity. But when competitors like Riot add hash rate of their own, Bitcoin's network difficulty rises to compensate, and MARA earns fewer bitcoin per unit of hash rate deployed than before. A miner has to keep expanding just to hold its share of the network's total block rewards, let alone grow it. Riot, for its part, actually cut its 2025 hash rate target from 46.7 EH/s down to 38.4 EH/s - a real pullback, not just a slower growth rate.

**The actual cost of production is a real, disclosed number, not a rough estimate.** MARA's energy cost alone to mine one bitcoin at its owned sites ran approximately $40,047 in the first quarter of 2026 - before accounting for depreciation, hosting fees, management costs, or interest on $3.65 billion of outstanding debt. That figure is the floor, not the ceiling, of what it actually costs the company to produce one bitcoin - a cost structure MSTR simply doesn't carry, since MSTR buys bitcoin directly rather than manufacturing it.

---

## MARA Is Actively Diversifying Away From Pure Bitcoin Exposure

**This is the detail that most changes how MARA specifically should be understood as a "bitcoin proxy" going into the rest of 2026.** The company sold approximately $1.5 billion of its bitcoin holdings in 2026, including $1.1 billion used to repurchase its own convertible notes near the end of a quarter, and has signaled a strategic pivot toward using its power infrastructure for AI and high-performance computing workloads instead of mining alone - backed by a planned $1.5 billion acquisition of the Long Ridge Energy & Power campus in Ohio. A company actively selling bitcoin to fund a pivot toward AI infrastructure is, by definition, becoming a less pure bitcoin play over time, regardless of how it's still marketed or how it still trades day to day based on bitcoin sentiment.

## What This Means for Choosing a "Proxy"

**None of these three deliver bitcoin exposure the same way, and none of them delivers it cleanly.** MSTR's leverage means it should, in theory, amplify bitcoin's moves in both directions - but the mNAV discount means that relationship has become genuinely unpredictable, since a discounted stock doesn't need to track bitcoin's price move-for-move if the market is separately repricing the sustainability of the accumulation strategy itself. MARA and RIOT's returns depend on mining economics - network difficulty, energy costs, hardware efficiency - layered on top of bitcoin's price, meaning a period of flat or even rising bitcoin prices doesn't guarantee flat or rising mining-company profitability if difficulty rises faster than a miner's own hash rate. And MARA specifically is now also exposed to a new, separate business line (AI/HPC infrastructure) that has nothing to do with bitcoin at all.

## Access and Tax Treatment

**All three trade as standard US common stock** on major exchanges, accessible through any broker offering US market access, with no special account type or crypto-specific onboarding required the way a crypto exchange account would need. **Dividend and capital gains treatment for a non-US investor follows the same standard framework** covered throughout this site's [taxes category](/taxes/) - none of the three currently pays a meaningful dividend, so the practical tax question for most holders centers on capital gains rather than dividend withholding, and non-resident aliens generally owe no US capital gains tax on the sale of US common stock held outside a US trade or business, the same treatment that applies to any other US equity.

## Bottom Line

MSTR, MARA, and RIOT get grouped together as bitcoin proxy stocks, but they're fundamentally different vehicles: a leveraged bitcoin holding company whose entire capital-raising model just lost the premium it depends on, and two mining operators whose profitability runs through real, disclosed production costs and a difficulty treadmill that has nothing to do with a chart pattern - with one of them now actively redirecting resources toward an entirely different business. The 2026 mNAV reversal on MSTR specifically is the single most important, current fact for anyone evaluating it today, and it's a fact a lot of older coverage of this stock simply doesn't reflect yet.
