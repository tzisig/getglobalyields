Deribit, an offshore exchange most casual crypto investors have never heard of, spent years as the dominant venue for bitcoin options trading. That changed faster than almost anyone expected once IBIT - BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust - got its own listed options market on Nasdaq in November 2024. Within about a year, a mainstream, regulated US ETF's options overtook a specialized offshore derivatives exchange as the largest bitcoin options venue in the world.

---

## The Launch and the Takeover

**IBIT options started trading November 19, 2024, and moved $446 million in the first hours alone** - a volume Bloomberg's senior ETF analyst Eric Balchunas called "a ton" for any product's first day, let alone an options market this new. Nearly 98% of the contracts traded that first day were calls, an extremely bullish skew reflecting the pent-up demand from investors who'd wanted regulated, exchange-listed bitcoin options exposure and simply hadn't had a clean way to get it before.

**The growth from there wasn't gradual - it overtook the incumbent outright.** Open interest in IBIT options climbed to nearly $38 billion within roughly a year of launch, passing Deribit's $32 billion. As of early 2026, IBIT accounts for 52% of all bitcoin options open interest globally - a genuine majority of the market - against aggregate bitcoin options open interest of $65 billion and bitcoin futures open interest of $60 billion. Daily trading volume on IBIT options now runs $2-3 billion, within range of Deribit's own $3-4 billion daily average, meaning the two venues now compete closely on live trading activity even though IBIT has already pulled ahead on cumulative open interest.

---

## Why a Regulated US Product Won So Fast

**The underlying reason isn't complicated: IBIT options trade on Nasdaq, through the same brokerage accounts and clearing infrastructure investors already use for every other US-listed option, with standard CFTC and SEC oversight** - a fundamentally easier, more familiar, and more trusted access point than an offshore crypto-native exchange requiring separate account setup, different custody arrangements, and a different regulatory framework entirely. For an institutional investor or a large fund with compliance requirements around counterparty risk and regulated venues, IBIT options simply removed a whole category of friction and due diligence that trading on Deribit required.

**Regulators have responded to that success by loosening the constraints on how large a position any single trader can hold.** Standard position limits for even the largest-cap stocks and ETFs are typically capped around 250,000 contracts. Given IBIT's liquidity, exchanges have proposed raising that limit specifically for IBIT to as much as 1,000,000 contracts per side - a real, filed regulatory proposal reflecting how much larger IBIT's options market has grown relative to the standard limits built for other products.

## The Market Is Mature Enough Now to Have Its Own Volatility Index

**IBIT's implied volatility currently runs around 33% (30-day, as of early August 2026)** - meaningfully higher than QQQ's roughly 25-26% covered elsewhere on this site, but well below TQQQ's typical 55-65% range, and genuinely low relative to IBIT's own historical pattern, where 30-day IV has typically ranged between 35% and 90%. A current reading near the very bottom of that historical range signals a relatively calm period for bitcoin-linked options pricing by the asset's own standards, even though 33% would still register as elevated for a conventional equity index.

**A concrete sign of how institutionalized this market has become: Cboe launched a dedicated volatility index built specifically on IBIT options in March 2026** - the Cboe IBIT Volatility Index, nicknamed BITVX, constructed the same way the VIX is built from S&P 500 options, using weekly IBIT option expirations bracketing a constant 30-day horizon. A derivatives exchange building a standalone benchmark index around a single ETF's options market is a real marker of maturity - it doesn't happen for niche or thinly-traded products, only for ones with enough consistent volume and open interest to support a reliable, tradeable volatility benchmark.

## What This Means Practically

**For an investor wanting to run an options strategy against bitcoin exposure specifically** - covered calls, protective puts, or more complex structures - IBIT's depth and liquidity now make it a genuinely viable, arguably preferable venue compared to trying to access Deribit directly, particularly for anyone already trading through a standard US brokerage rather than maintaining a separate crypto-derivatives account. The same [covered call mechanics covered for QQQ](/options-income/selling-covered-calls-qqq/) elsewhere on this site apply structurally to IBIT - strike selection, theta decay, assignment risk - though bitcoin's own volatility profile, and the fact that IBIT itself is structured as a grantor trust rather than a standard RIC as covered in our [crypto ETF tax treatment guide](/taxes/crypto-etf-tax-treatment-international-investors/), mean the specific numbers and tax characterization differ meaningfully from an equity ETF option.

**For an investor comparing IBIT itself against the mining and holding-company stocks covered in our [MSTR/MARA/RIOT comparison](/crypto/mstr-mara-riot-bitcoin-proxy-stocks-compared/)**, IBIT's now-massive options market is a real, concrete advantage specifically for anyone who wants both clean price exposure to bitcoin and the ability to run an active options strategy against that exposure - a combination none of MSTR, MARA, or RIOT deliver in the same way, since their options markets exist but don't carry anything close to IBIT's depth or the specific bitcoin-price correlation a spot bitcoin trust offers by design.

## Access and Tax Treatment

**IBIT and its options trade through any standard US brokerage offering options approval**, no different in mechanics from any other listed option. The underlying tax treatment of IBIT itself - grantor trust rather than RIC - is covered in detail in our dedicated [crypto ETF tax guide](/taxes/crypto-etf-tax-treatment-international-investors/); options premium and exercise/assignment outcomes on IBIT options follow standard US options taxation mechanics separately from that underlying-fund structure question, and a non-US investor should confirm the specific combined treatment with a tax professional rather than assuming it mirrors either a plain equity option or a standard crypto trade in isolation.

## Bottom Line

IBIT's options market didn't just launch successfully - it took over as the largest bitcoin options venue in the world within about a year, overtaking an offshore exchange that had dominated the space for years before IBIT existed. That's a genuine structural shift in where bitcoin derivatives trading actually happens, driven by the simple advantage of a regulated, familiar, US-brokerage-accessible product beating a specialized offshore venue once given a real chance to compete on the same playing field.
