Before getting into how this works, an honest framing most crypto tax content skips: tax-loss harvesting only matters if you actually owe US capital gains tax on crypto in the first place, and most readers of this site - non-resident aliens - generally don't, the same way they generally don't owe US capital gains tax on stock sales, covered in our [crypto ETF tax treatment guide](/taxes/crypto-etf-tax-treatment-international-investors/). This article is genuinely useful specifically for a US citizen or green card holder living and investing from abroad, or a non-US investor who meets the 183-day substantial presence test in a given year - not a universal strategy for every reader.

---

## The Rule, and Why It Doesn't Cover Crypto

**The wash sale rule, IRC Section 1091, disallows claiming a tax loss if you sell a security at a loss and buy a "substantially identical" one within 30 days before or after the sale** - the rule that stops a stock investor from selling a losing position purely to book the loss, then immediately buying it right back. The rule's text specifically covers "stock or securities." The IRS classified cryptocurrency as property, not a security, in Notice 2014-21 - a classification that has held for over a decade and remains the operative rule in 2026.

**The practical result: you can sell Bitcoin at a loss on Monday and buy it back Tuesday, claiming the full capital loss, with no 30-day waiting period required** - something legally impossible with a stock or a regular equity ETF. This is a genuine, currently-legal tax planning advantage unique to direct crypto holdings, not a gray area or an aggressive interpretation of ambiguous rules.

---

## This Loophole Has Survived Years of Attempts to Close It

**This isn't an obscure gap regulators haven't noticed - it's been targeted repeatedly and remains open anyway.** The 2021 Build Back Better Act included language specifically extending wash-sale rules to digital assets; it didn't pass. The Lummis-Gillibrand Responsible Financial Innovation Act proposed the same closure. New proposals surfaced again in 2024 and 2025 targeting the same gap. As recently as mid-2025, a White House report specifically recommended treating crypto as its own asset class subject to securities-like rules, including wash sales. None of these efforts have become law as of 2026 - the loophole that's been "about to close" for several years running is, as a factual matter, still open.

## The Important Exception: Crypto ETFs Are Different

**This is the detail that connects directly to the crypto ETF content covered elsewhere on this site, and it's genuinely easy to get wrong.** IBIT, ETHA, and other spot crypto ETFs - covered in our guides on [IBIT options](/crypto/ibit-options-largest-bitcoin-derivatives-market/) and [Ethereum ETF staking](/crypto/ethereum-etf-etha-staking-yield-explained/) - are themselves securities, exchange-listed shares, regardless of the fact that the underlying asset they hold is bitcoin or ether. Selling IBIT at a loss and buying it back (or buying a "substantially identical" fund like FBTC) within 30 days is a transaction in securities, and the standard wash sale rule applies to that transaction the normal way it applies to any equity or fund. The loophole covers direct coin ownership - buying and selling actual Bitcoin or Ethereum on an exchange or through a wallet - not the ETF wrapper built around the same underlying asset.

## Who This Actually Matters For

**A US citizen or green card holder living outside the US, investing in crypto directly**, remains a US taxpayer on worldwide income regardless of residence, and can use this loophole exactly the way a US-resident crypto investor would - selling depreciated holdings to realize a loss against other gains, then immediately repurchasing the same position without the 30-day wait a stock investor would face.

**A genuine non-resident alien who happens to meet the substantial presence test in a specific year** - covered in our [visitor insurance and substantial presence guide](/insurance-planning/visitor-medical-insurance-usa-investors/) - or who has crypto gains effectively connected to a US trade or business, becomes a US taxpayer on that income for that year and could find this relevant in that specific, narrower circumstance.

**A typical non-resident alien with no US tax residency trigger and no effectively connected income** generally has no US capital gains liability on crypto sales to harvest against in the first place - for this reader, the far more relevant question is how their own home country treats crypto gains, which varies enormously and is worth confirming separately rather than assuming any US-focused strategy applies.

## How the Strategy Actually Works, for Those It Applies To

**The mechanics are simple precisely because the restriction that complicates it for stocks doesn't exist here.** Sell a losing crypto position, realize the capital loss for tax purposes, and repurchase the same coin immediately if you want to maintain the position - no 30-day window to sit out, no need to buy a "similar but not identical" asset to avoid the wash-sale label the way a stock investor sometimes does. The realized loss can offset capital gains elsewhere in your portfolio, and in the US, up to $3,000 of any excess loss beyond that against ordinary income in a given year, with any remainder carried forward.

---

## Bottom Line

The crypto wash sale loophole is real, current, and has survived multiple serious legislative attempts to close it - you can harvest a crypto loss and immediately rebuy in a way that's flatly illegal for a stock position. The catch specific to this site's audience: it only matters if you actually owe US capital gains tax on crypto, which most non-US investors don't, and it doesn't extend to crypto ETF shares, which are securities subject to the normal rule. Know which category you're actually in before assuming this strategy applies to you.
