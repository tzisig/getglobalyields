The personal Wise account is the one most individual investors actually want - free to open, no minimum balance, and built around holding multiple currencies at once rather than forcing a conversion on arrival. It's a different product from the Wise Business account covered in our [Wise Business guide](/currency-banking/wise-business-account-international-investors/), and for someone simply moving personal savings into a US brokerage account, it's also the right one: nothing about the Business tier's added features - invoicing, team management, accounting-software integration - applies to a solo investor, while its one-time opening fee does.

---

## Opening an Account - What's Actually Required

**A personal Wise account is free to open, with no monthly fee and no minimum balance requirement** - the account-opening fee that applies to Wise Business simply doesn't exist on the personal side. Opening one requires your full legal name, date of birth, residential address, a phone number for two-factor verification, a government-issued photo ID (passport, national ID, or driver's license), and in many cases proof of address such as a recent utility bill or bank statement. Some applicants are also prompted for a selfie-based identity check. Verification for personal accounts typically clears within roughly half a day for most applicants, noticeably faster than the multi-day review Business accounts can take.

**You don't need to be a resident of the country whose currency account you're opening** - Wise's whole model is built around holding balances in currencies you don't live in, which is exactly what makes it useful for funding a US brokerage account from outside the US.

---

## What "Multi-Currency" Actually Means in Practice

**A single Wise account holds balances in 40+ currencies simultaneously**, rather than forcing every incoming transfer to convert immediately into one base currency. For an investor funding a US brokerage account, this means you can receive or hold USD directly, convert only when you choose to, and avoid an unnecessary conversion round-trip if you're also holding, say, EUR or GBP for other purposes in the same account. This account previously operated under the name "Wise Borderless" - a name Wise has since retired in favor of simply "Wise account," though the older name still shows up in some search results and older reviews referencing the same underlying product.

## The Debit Card

**The physical Wise card costs a modest one-time fee to order** - £7 for standard delivery in the UK, or from £10.85 for express shipping, with a digital card available at no cost and a lost-card replacement running £2.50. Fees vary somewhat by market. Wise states there's no markup specifically for using the card abroad, though e-wallet top-ups in some currencies carry a separate 2% fee, worth checking if that's part of your intended usage pattern.

**ATM withdrawals changed meaningfully on May 1, 2026** - Wise raised its free monthly withdrawal allowances and replaced its old flat per-withdrawal fee with a percentage-based fee once you exceed the free amount. As of the new structure: UK £250/month free, then 2.69% on the excess; US $250/month free, then a $1.95 fixed fee plus 1.95%; EU generally €250/month free with similar excess pricing; Australia 400 AUD, Canada 100 CAD, and Singapore 100 SGD free allowances, though Wise notes Canada and Singapore carry additional local regulatory restrictions on card-based withdrawals. Brazil is a notable outlier with only one free withdrawal per month before a flat 20 BRL fee applies. Confirm your specific country's current allowance directly, since this structure only took effect mid-2026 and further adjustments are plausible.

## Transfer Fees - What to Actually Expect

**Wise's published fee model for major currency pairs runs roughly a small fixed fee plus a percentage of the transfer amount** - indicative figures for a typical EUR-to-USD personal transfer cluster around 0.4-0.5% total, broadly consistent with the same 0.33%-2% band that applies to Wise Business transfers, varying by currency pair and funding method (bank debit tends to run cheaper than card or wire funding). Wise publishes a live calculator on its own site, and given how often exact percentages shift, checking the calculator at the time you actually transfer is more reliable than treating any single cited figure as fixed.

## A Feature Worth Knowing About: Interest on Idle Balances

**In several countries, Wise pays variable interest on money sitting in your account balance**, funded through underlying government money-market funds rather than a traditional bank deposit account - available in the UK, Singapore, and a number of EEA countries (including Austria, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Norway, Spain, and Sweden, with Hungary in beta), and previously offered to US customers as well. As of mid-2026, published rates ran up to roughly 2.40% on USD balances, 2.47% on GBP, and 0.68% on EUR, with a separate 3.26% variable rate on a newer UK current-account product launched in March 2026. **This is not a bank deposit and isn't covered by deposit guarantee or insurance schemes** the way a traditional savings account would be - relevant context for our [USD cash holding strategy guide](/currency-banking/usd-cash-holding-strategy-international-investors/), since idle USD sitting in a Wise balance between transfers behaves differently, and carries different protections, than cash sitting in an insured bank account or a brokerage's own cash sweep.

## Reliability - What Reviews Actually Show

**Wise holds a "Great" rating on Trustpilot, 4.3 out of 5 across 280,000+ reviews, with roughly 80% five-star and about 9% one-star.** The one-star complaints cluster mainly around account freezes and slow customer-service response times rather than the core FX/transfer mechanics. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a Wise Business account instead of Personal if I'm actively trading, not just holding savings?**
No - active trading activity doesn't change which Wise tier fits. Business exists for invoicing, team accounts, and accounting-software integration, none of which relate to trading frequency. A personal account covers the funding and currency-holding use case regardless of how often you trade once the money reaches your broker.

**Is "Wise Borderless" a different, older product from today's Wise account?**
No - Borderless was the original name for the same personal multi-currency account. Wise renamed the product, but the underlying account and its multi-currency functionality are the same thing under a new name.

**Does the interest Wise pays on idle balances count as taxable income?**
In most jurisdictions, yes - interest-like returns from a money-market-fund-backed balance are generally treated as taxable income by tax authorities, though the exact characterization depends on your specific country's rules. Confirm with a local tax advisor rather than assuming it's treated identically to bank interest or investment income.

**Can I use my Wise debit card to withdraw cash while traveling, the same way I'd use a regular bank card?**
Yes, within the free monthly ATM allowance for your specific currency and country, described above - beyond that allowance, the percentage-based fee applies, so frequent large withdrawals in one currency are worth checking against your account's specific limit before relying on the card as your primary travel cash source.

The personal Wise account is free, opens quickly, and does everything most individual investors actually need for moving money into a US brokerage account - multi-currency balances, a debit card with no card-specific foreign-spending markup, and in a growing list of countries, real (if not deposit-insured) interest on idle balances.
