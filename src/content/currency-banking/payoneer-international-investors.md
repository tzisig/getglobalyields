Payoneer is built for a different primary use case than [Wise](/currency-banking/wise-business-account-international-investors/) - it's optimized for receiving payments from marketplaces and clients (Amazon, Fiverr, Upwork, and similar platforms), not for the kind of one-off, transparent currency conversion a retail investor wants when moving personal savings into a brokerage account. If you already have USD sitting in Payoneer from freelance or e-commerce income, it can genuinely double as a funding source. If you're starting from scratch purely to fund a brokerage account, it's worth understanding exactly where it's a fit and where it isn't before opening one.

---

## What Payoneer Actually Is

**Payoneer supports account holders in 200+ countries**, with two account tiers: a Freelancer/individual account for unregistered freelancers, contractors, and sole traders, and a Business account, required once you have a legally registered company. Some countries can't complete registration entirely online, so availability isn't perfectly uniform globally.

**Once verified, Payoneer issues real local receiving accounts with genuine local bank details** - confirmed currencies include USD (via US ACH), EUR (SEPA), GBP (via Payoneer's own UK-licensed entity), JPY, AUD, CAD, HKD, SGD, and AED, among others. This is functionally similar to what Wise offers through its own banking partner - genuine local account numbers rather than just an internal balance you have to wire into separately.

---

## What It Actually Costs

**There's no account opening fee.** An **annual inactivity fee of $29.95 applies only if you receive less than $2,000 total in any rolling 12-month period**, and it's waived automatically once you clear that threshold - so an account that's actually being used for real income doesn't pay it.

**Currency conversion costs vary meaningfully by the type of operation**: roughly 0.5% for converting between balances you already hold inside Payoneer (say, EUR to USD internally), rising to as much as 2% when withdrawing to a local bank account in a currency different from your Payoneer balance. Withdrawing to a local bank in the *same* currency as your balance runs a flat fee around $1.50, for withdrawals up to roughly $50,000 a month. There's no minimum balance requirement.

## Funding a US Broker - What's Actually Confirmed, and What Isn't

**This is the part worth being careful about: there is no confirmed official integration between Payoneer and Interactive Brokers, unlike the documented Wise-IBKR partnership.** Several third-party and SEO-oriented sites claim an active partnership with a multi-day verification hold on withdrawals, but none of this is confirmed by either IBKR's or Payoneer's own official materials, and should be treated as unverified rather than fact.

**What is well-documented, and applies here the same way it applies to Wise:** IBKR generally rejects third-party deposits and requires the depositing account name to match the IBKR account holder exactly. Since a Payoneer USD receiving account is typically issued in your own name, a name-matched ACH transfer from it should, mechanically, clear IBKR's own name-match check the same way a Wise transfer does - but no explicit IBKR statement confirms Payoneer specifically as an accepted funding source, so this is a reasonable inference from IBKR's general policy, not a guarantee.

## Payoneer vs Wise for This Specific Purpose

**For someone solely trying to fund a brokerage account - not running a freelance or e-commerce business - Wise is generally the better-documented, cheaper option.** Wise converts at a published rate plus a transparent fee starting around 0.57% for major pairs, disclosed clearly at the point of transfer. Payoneer's markup varies by transaction type and isn't displayed with the same up-front transparency, and its core product is genuinely designed around receiving business/marketplace payments rather than one-off personal currency conversion.

**Where Payoneer earns its place is different: if you already have USD sitting in a Payoneer balance from freelance or marketplace income, moving it directly to your broker avoids first converting to your home currency and back again just to use a different transfer service** - a real, practical reason to use the account you already have rather than opening Wise purely to move money you could send directly.

## A Development Worth Knowing Before You Commit

**Payoneer (Nasdaq: PAYO) agreed to be acquired by Nuvei, a Canadian payments company, in an all-cash deal worth roughly $2.75 billion (\$7.40/share), announced mid-2026 and targeted to close in mid-2027**, pending regulatory and shareholder approval. This doesn't affect your account's function today, but it's a real, disclosed structural change worth factoring into a decision to rely on Payoneer as long-term brokerage-funding infrastructure - product terms, branding, and even the underlying banking partnerships described above could change once the acquisition closes, in a way that a comparably-sized competitor not mid-acquisition wouldn't carry as a near-term risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a registered business to open a Payoneer account?**
No - a Freelancer/individual account covers unregistered freelancers, contractors, and sole traders. The Business account tier is specifically for holders of a legally registered company, and a registered business can't use the Freelancer tier instead.

**Is the $29.95 annual fee something I need to plan around if I'm just funding a brokerage account occasionally?**
Only if your total received amount stays under $2,000 in a rolling 12-month window - an account seeing meaningful real activity, including brokerage-funding transfers, is likely to clear that threshold and avoid the fee entirely.

**Should I open Payoneer specifically to fund my brokerage account if I don't already have one for other income?**
Generally no - if funding a brokerage account is your only goal, Wise's more transparent, typically cheaper conversion pricing makes it the better-suited tool for that specific job. Payoneer makes more sense as a funding source when you already hold USD there from freelance, marketplace, or client income.

**Will the pending Nuvei acquisition affect my existing Payoneer balance or account access?**
Nothing has changed as of this article's publication, and the deal isn't expected to close until mid-2027 at the earliest - but it's a real reason to check Payoneer's own communications periodically rather than assuming today's terms and features are permanent.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Open the account tier that matches your actual status - Freelancer/individual vs Business - since a registered company can't use the Freelancer tier
- [ ] Compare Payoneer's variable 0.5%-2% conversion cost against Wise's more transparent published rate before choosing which to use for a one-off brokerage funding transfer
- [ ] If funding IBKR, confirm the name on your Payoneer account matches your IBKR account holder name exactly, given the same third-party-deposit restriction that applies to other funding sources
- [ ] Use Payoneer specifically when you already hold USD there from freelance or marketplace income - not as your first choice if you're starting from zero purely to fund a brokerage account
- [ ] Keep an eye on Payoneer's own updates regarding the pending Nuvei acquisition if you plan to rely on the account long-term

---

## Bottom Line

Payoneer gives you a genuine US receiving account with real local details, useful specifically if you already have USD income flowing through it from freelance or marketplace work - in which case funding a brokerage account directly from that existing balance is a real, practical use. If you're opening an account purely to fund a broker with no other use case, Wise's more transparent and generally cheaper conversion pricing makes it the better-suited tool for that specific job, and the pending Nuvei acquisition is one more reason not to default to Payoneer without a specific reason to prefer it.
