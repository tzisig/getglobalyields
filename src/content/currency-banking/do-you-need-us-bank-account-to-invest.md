No - a US bank account is not a requirement to buy US stocks from abroad, and the large majority of this site's readers invest in US markets without one. A US brokerage account is a separate thing from a US bank account, and international-friendly brokers are built specifically to serve investors who don't have either a US address or US banking relationship.

---

## Brokerage Accounts vs Bank Accounts - A Genuine Distinction

**A brokerage account holds and trades securities. A bank account holds cash and provides payment/deposit services.** These are structurally different types of accounts, run by different types of institutions, even though a brokerage account also holds a cash balance internally. International-friendly brokers like Interactive Brokers accept account applications directly from non-US residents, fund via international wire transfer or services like Wise, and never require a US bank account as a prerequisite - the account-opening process is covered in more detail in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/).

---

## What You Actually Need to Open a US-Focused Brokerage Account

**A valid form of government ID, proof of address in your home country, and (for most brokers) a completed [W-8BEN form](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/)** to establish your foreign tax status and access reduced treaty withholding rates on dividends. None of these require a US bank account, a US address, or US citizenship/residency.

**Funding the account** happens via international wire transfer, a currency-transfer service like Wise, or in some cases a linked account in your home country's currency - none of which requires a US bank account as an intermediate step.

---

## The Narrow Cases Where a US Bank Account Genuinely Helps

**Frequent, large USD transactions unrelated to your brokerage account** - for instance, an investor who also owns US rental property, runs a US-facing business, or has other recurring USD-denominated obligations - may find a US bank account genuinely useful for reasons entirely separate from stock investing.

**HSBC Expat and similar offshore banking products**, covered in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/), combine banking and investing for high-net-worth investors who want both in one relationship - but this is a convenience choice for investors who already qualify for the balance threshold, not a requirement for investing itself.

**Opening a genuine US bank account as a non-resident** is, in practice, difficult for most people - most US banks require a US address, Social Security Number or ITIN, and often in-person account opening, which puts a true US bank account out of reach for the large majority of international investors regardless of whether they'd want one.

---

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Recognize that a brokerage account and a bank account are separate, structurally different things - you need the former, not the latter, to invest
- [ ] Confirm your chosen broker explicitly supports non-resident applicants funding via international wire or a service like Wise
- [ ] Complete your W-8BEN as part of account opening, regardless of your banking setup, to access reduced treaty withholding
- [ ] Only pursue a genuine US bank account if you have a specific, separate need for one (US property, US business activity) - not as a step toward investing in US stocks

---

## Bottom Line

You do not need a US bank account to invest in US stocks - a brokerage account with an international-friendly broker, funded via wire transfer or a service like Wise, is the entire requirement for the vast majority of international investors. A US bank account only becomes relevant for a narrow set of investors with separate USD-denominated financial needs beyond stock investing itself.
