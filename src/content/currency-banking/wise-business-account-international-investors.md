Wise Business is built for freelancers and small companies invoicing clients in multiple currencies - but a growing number of international investors use it for something the product wasn't originally marketed for: getting a real USD account, with a genuine US routing number, without a US bank account. Whether that's actually worth doing over Wise's personal account, or over just letting your broker handle the conversion itself, comes down to fees most investors never look up directly.

---

## What a Wise Business Account Actually Gives You

**Sole traders and freelancers can open a Wise Business account using the same personal identity documents as a personal account** - Wise treats a sole trader as legally the same entity as the individual, so a separate registered company isn't required to use the business product. Full functionality depends on your country of registration, and Wise maintains a restricted list (Afghanistan, Belarus, Cuba, Iran, Iraq, Libya, North Korea, and Myanmar, as of 2026) where accounts can't be opened at all.

**Once opened and verified, the account unlocks real local account details** - a US ACH account and routing number issued through Wise's US banking partner Evolve Bank & Trust, a UK sort code, a EUR IBAN, an AUD BSB, and equivalents in other currencies depending on your registration country. This is the specific feature that matters for funding a US brokerage account: rather than wiring your home currency internationally every time, you can hold and receive genuine USD through a US account number, which many brokers treat as a domestic-style ACH transfer rather than an international wire.

---

## The Real Cost Structure

**There's no monthly or subscription fee** - Wise Business is pay-as-you-go, a structure multiple independent reviews confirm as of 2026. What isn't free is a **one-time setup fee to unlock your receiving account details**, and this fee is country-dependent rather than a single global figure: **$31** in the US, **£50** in the UK, **54 SGD** in Singapore, based on Wise's own published country pages. Check your specific country's fee before opening the account rather than assuming one of these figures applies to you.

**Currency conversion itself runs roughly 0.33% to 2% above the mid-market rate**, varying by currency pair - this range comes from third-party fee-tracking sites rather than a single official Wise-published table, so treat it as an approximate band to confirm at the time you convert, not a fixed number. There's no minimum account balance requirement in any source reviewed.

## The Official Wise-to-IBKR Integration

**Wise and Interactive Brokers built an official integration** - "Pay with Wise," letting an IBKR account holder transfer directly from a Wise balance - first announced in 2023 and still active as of 2026 per Wise's own IBKR-specific page. In practice, this means the USD sitting in your Wise Business balance can move to your IBKR account without a separate international wire.

**The one detail that causes more real problems than the fees do: IBKR generally rejects third-party deposits, and requires the depositing account name to match the IBKR account holder's name exactly.** A Wise Business account registered under a company name that differs from your personal name on your IBKR account is a genuine mismatch risk - even smaller differences, like a missing middle name or a transliteration variant, have been enough to trigger a rejected deposit according to IBKR's own support documentation. If you're a sole trader using Wise Business specifically to fund a personal IBKR account, confirm the exact name registered on both accounts matches before relying on this path for a real transfer.

## Is It Actually Cheaper Than Just Converting Inside IBKR?

**Not necessarily - and this is the comparison most investors researching Wise Business skip.** IBKR's own built-in currency conversion runs meaningfully cheaper than Wise's 0.33%+ band: roughly **0.03% markup for automatic conversion**, or as low as **0.002% (0.2 basis points), with a $2 minimum, for manual conversion** done directly inside the platform. An investor already comfortable holding a foreign-currency balance and converting manually inside IBKR may find that meaningfully cheaper than paying Wise's spread on the way in.

**Where Wise still earns its keep is the receiving side, not the conversion rate itself** - getting genuine local account details in multiple currencies (useful if you're also invoicing clients, receiving payments, or holding balances in more than just USD and your home currency) is a distinct feature from "cheapest possible FX," and it's the reason freelancers and small business owners adopt Wise Business in the first place, with brokerage funding as a secondary use of an account they already have for other reasons - not necessarily a reason to open one from scratch purely to fund a brokerage account, if minimizing conversion cost is the only goal.

## What Changed at Wise in 2026

**Wise moved its primary stock listing from the London Stock Exchange to the Nasdaq on May 11, 2026** (ticker WSE), retaining a secondary LSE listing - a structural change to where the company itself trades as a public stock, not a change to how customer accounts work.

**More relevant to account holders: the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency denied Wise's application for a national trust bank charter around July 21-24, 2026**, citing AML and compliance deficiencies, and Wise's stock fell as much as 11% on the news. Wise's existing US operations - money transmitter licenses and the Evolve Bank & Trust partnership that issues the routing/account numbers described above - reportedly continue unaffected, though the fuller operational detail behind that wasn't independently confirmable from public sources at the time of writing. Worth checking Wise's own status page if you're opening a new US account and want current confirmation rather than relying on this article's snapshot.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a registered company to open Wise Business, or does freelance/sole-trader status count?**
Sole trader and freelancer status counts - Wise treats you as the same legal entity as your individual identity for this purpose, so you can open a Business account using personal ID documents rather than company registration paperwork.

**Is Wise Business actually better than Wise's personal multi-currency account for funding a brokerage account?**
For most individual investors funding a personal brokerage account, the personal account is the more natural fit - the Business account's real advantage is invoicing and multi-currency receiving for freelance or business income, which is a different use case than simply moving personal savings into a brokerage account.

**Does the routing number Wise provides work with every US broker, not just Interactive Brokers?**
The official integration referenced above is specifically with Interactive Brokers. Other brokers may or may not treat a Wise-issued US ACH account the same way a genuine US bank account is treated - confirm directly with your specific broker's funding documentation before assuming the same mechanics apply.

**If the OCC denied Wise's bank charter application, is my money still safe with Wise?**
The charter denial affects Wise's ability to operate as a chartered US bank itself, not the money transmitter licensing and banking-partner structure (via Evolve Bank & Trust) that Wise currently operates under in the US - but confirm current terms directly with Wise given this is a recent, evolving regulatory situation rather than a settled one.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Confirm your country's specific one-time setup fee before opening an account, since it varies by country rather than being a single global figure
- [ ] Compare Wise's ~0.33%+ conversion spread against your broker's own built-in FX conversion cost before assuming Wise is automatically cheaper
- [ ] If funding IBKR specifically, verify the exact name on your Wise account matches your IBKR account holder name precisely, to avoid a rejected third-party deposit
- [ ] Use the official "Pay with Wise" integration rather than a manual international wire, if you're funding IBKR from an existing Wise balance
- [ ] Treat Wise Business as the right choice specifically if you also need multi-currency receiving for freelance or business income - not solely for the lowest possible FX cost on brokerage funding

## Bottom Line

Wise Business gives non-US residents a genuine US ACH account and routing number without needing a US bank account, through an official integration that connects directly to Interactive Brokers - a real, useful piece of infrastructure for investors who already need multi-currency receiving for other reasons. But it isn't automatically the cheapest way to convert currency into a brokerage account: IBKR's own built-in conversion runs well below Wise's typical spread, and the one detail that causes the most real friction isn't the fee at all - it's making sure the name on your Wise account matches your brokerage account exactly, since a mismatch is what actually gets deposits rejected.
