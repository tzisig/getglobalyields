Money moving from your home country to a US broker doesn't take one single, uniform path - it travels through one of several genuinely different systems, each with different speed, cost, and transparency characteristics. Understanding which rail your funds are actually using explains a lot about why the same "wire transfer" can cost $15 and take one day for one investor, or $50 and take four days for another.

---

## SWIFT: The Traditional Correspondent Banking Network

**SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) is a messaging network**, not a payment system itself - it lets banks instruct each other to move money, with the actual funds typically passing through one or more correspondent (intermediary) banks between the sender and the final destination, particularly when the sending and receiving banks don't have a direct relationship.

**Each intermediary bank in the chain can, in some cases, deduct its own handling fee**, and the exchange rate applied (if a conversion is needed along the way) isn't always transparent to the sender - a mechanic that can contribute to the double-conversion problem covered in our [double conversion fees guide](/currency-banking/avoiding-double-currency-conversion-fees/). SWIFT transfers commonly take one to five business days, and total cost (sending bank fee, intermediary fees, receiving bank fee, and any FX spread) can be difficult to know in advance with full precision.

---

## Modern Fintech Rails: A Genuinely Different Mechanism

**Services like Wise generally don't move your actual currency across borders in the traditional correspondent-banking sense.** Instead, they hold pools of local currency in multiple countries, and a transfer effectively matches your outgoing home-currency payment against an existing local-currency balance on the receiving end - a mechanism that avoids most correspondent-bank fees and allows for meaningfully faster settlement and a transparent, disclosed exchange rate, generally at or very close to the mid-market rate plus a clearly stated fee.

**The practical result:** transfers through these rails are typically same-day or next-day, with a cost you can calculate precisely before sending, rather than an estimate that depends on how many intermediary banks get involved.

---

## Local/Domestic Rails Where Available

**Some countries have direct, low-cost domestic transfer rails** that can connect to certain brokers or services without touching the SWIFT network at all - covered specifically for the US ACH system, which some international investors can access indirectly through certain broker-linked accounts. Where available, these tend to be the cheapest option, though availability depends heavily on your specific country and broker combination.

---

## Choosing a Rail for Broker Funding

**For most international investors funding a US brokerage account, a service like Wise connecting to a broker that supports the same currency you're sending is the combination that avoids the most fees and the most uncertainty** - the specific mechanics of the Wise-to-IBKR path are covered in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/).

**A direct SWIFT wire from your local bank remains a reasonable option** when the amount is large enough that a bank's flat wire fee is a small percentage of the total, or when your bank offers a genuinely competitive FX rate on the conversion itself - the trade-off is worth calculating on your specific numbers rather than assuming either rail is always better.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Understand that a SWIFT wire may pass through intermediary banks, each of which can add fees or delay - request a full-transparency wire ("OUR" fee arrangement) if your bank offers it, to avoid deductions along the route
- [ ] Compare the total, all-in cost of a SWIFT wire (bank fee + intermediary fees + FX spread) against a fintech alternative like Wise before choosing
- [ ] Check whether your broker supports receiving funds via a fintech transfer service directly, rather than only via traditional wire
- [ ] For large transfers where a flat wire fee is a small percentage of the total, a direct bank wire can still be competitive - run the actual numbers rather than assuming

---

## Bottom Line

SWIFT wires and modern fintech transfer services move money internationally through genuinely different mechanisms, with real differences in speed, cost transparency, and total cost. For most international investors funding a US broker, a transparent fintech rail like Wise, connected to a broker supporting the same currency, tends to minimize both cost and uncertainty - but the right choice depends on your specific transfer size, country, and broker combination.
