If you worked in the US at some point, accumulated a Traditional IRA or 401(k) balance, and have since moved abroad and become a non-resident alien, one question eventually becomes unavoidable: does the RMD requirement still apply to you, even from outside the US and even without US citizenship. It does - RMDs follow the account type, not your citizenship or current residency.

---

## What RMDs Are and Which Accounts They Apply To

Required Minimum Distributions are mandatory annual withdrawals the IRS requires from certain tax-advantaged retirement accounts once the account holder reaches a specific age - currently 73, rising to 75 for those born in 1960 or later, under current law. RMD rules apply to Traditional IRAs, SEP IRAs, SIMPLE IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s, and similar defined-contribution plans. **Roth IRAs are the notable exception** - the original account owner does not face lifetime RMDs on a Roth IRA, though this treatment differs for account beneficiaries.

**The amount owed each year** is calculated as the account's prior-year-end balance divided by an IRS life-expectancy factor specific to the account holder's age - the calculation itself does not vary based on citizenship or residency status.

---

## Why This Applies Regardless of Where You Live

RMD rules are a feature of the account type itself, established under US tax law when the account was created and funded - they do not change based on where the account holder currently resides or what citizenship they hold. A non-resident alien who accumulated a Traditional IRA or 401(k) balance while working in the US, and later relocated abroad permanently, is still subject to the same RMD schedule as if they had remained a US resident.

**This connects directly to a point covered in our [401(k) and IRA access guide](/retirement-pension/401k-ira-access-non-us-citizens/):** while a nonresident alien generally cannot make *new* contributions to these accounts without qualifying US-source earned income, an *existing* balance accumulated during a period of US work remains fully subject to ongoing RMD rules once the applicable age is reached, regardless of current residency or citizenship status.

---

## Withholding on RMD Distributions

RMD withdrawals taken by a non-resident alien are generally subject to US withholding - the default rate is 30%, though this can be reduced if a specific provision in your country's tax treaty with the US addresses pension or retirement account distributions. **This is worth checking specifically, rather than assuming it matches the standard dividend withholding rate covered throughout this site's [country-specific tax guides](/taxes/)** - treaty treatment of pension and retirement distributions is a distinct provision from the dividend article in the same treaty, and the rates do not always match.

---

## The Penalty for Missing an RMD

Failing to take a required distribution on time results in a real penalty - historically up to 25% of the amount that should have been withdrawn, though this can potentially be reduced to 10% if corrected within a specified window. This penalty structure applies regardless of citizenship or residency, making it genuinely important for a former US worker living abroad to actually track their RMD obligation rather than assuming distance from the US somehow exempts them.

---

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] If you have a legacy Traditional IRA or 401(k) balance from working in the US and are now living abroad, confirm your current age against the applicable RMD start age (73 or 75, depending on birth year)
- [ ] Set up a reliable system to track and take RMDs annually, since the penalty for missing one is real and applies regardless of your current country of residence
- [ ] Confirm the specific withholding rate that applies to RMD distributions under your country's treaty with the US, since it may differ from the standard dividend withholding rate
- [ ] Confirm with your account custodian whether they can still service the account and process distributions for a non-resident alien - some providers restrict services for account holders outside the US
- [ ] Remember Roth IRAs are not subject to the same lifetime RMD requirement, if you're deciding how to structure any rollover from an existing Traditional account

---

## Bottom Line

RMDs are tied to the account, not to where you live or what passport you hold - a non-resident alien with a legacy Traditional IRA or 401(k) from a period of US employment remains subject to the same distribution schedule and the same penalties for missing it as a US resident would be. If this describes your situation, confirm your custodian can still service the account from abroad and get current guidance on your specific treaty's withholding treatment for retirement distributions.
