Israeli investors researching US stock exposure often run into Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah - a flexible Israeli investment savings product offering a genuine capital gains tax exemption most direct brokerage accounts can't match. Whether it's a better vehicle for US stock exposure than a direct international brokerage account depends on your goals, your fund selection within the product, and critically, whether you are also a US citizen.

---

## What Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah Actually Offers

Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah is a flexible investment savings plan available to Israeli residents, with an annual deposit limit (roughly NIS 80,000, adjusted periodically) and typical management fees in the 0.6%-0.9% range. **The genuine advantage:** funds held inside this structure are exempt from Israeli capital gains tax - a real, meaningful benefit compared to a standard taxable brokerage account, where capital gains are taxed under Israel's ordinary rules. Funds are also liquid and can generally be withdrawn without penalty, unlike a pure pension-locked account.

---

## The US Exposure Question

This is a fund-based product, not a direct brokerage account - you select from a menu of funds the Kupat Gemel provider offers, rather than picking individual US stocks or specific ETFs like TQQQ directly. Some Kupat Gemel funds do include meaningful S&P 500 exposure as part of their broader allocation, but this is indirect, blended exposure within a managed fund - not the direct, specific control over individual holdings that a standalone brokerage account provides.

**What this means practically:** if your goal is broad, diversified equity exposure with a real tax advantage and you're comfortable with a fund manager's specific allocation choices, Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah can serve that goal well. If your goal is holding specific US securities - TQQQ, individual stocks, or a specific ETF selection - a direct brokerage account is the only way to get that level of control, and Kupat Gemel is not a substitute for it.

---

## The Real Trap: PFIC Risk for US Citizens

**This is the single most important point in this comparison for a specific subset of readers.** If you are a US citizen or green card holder living in Israel - a genuinely common situation given the size of the American-Israeli community - Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah's underlying funds are very likely classified as Passive Foreign Investment Companies (PFICs) under US tax law, since they are foreign investment vehicles holding passive assets. As covered in detail in our [PFIC rules guide](/taxes/pfic-rules-international-investors/), PFIC classification triggers some of the harshest tax treatment in the US tax code - punitive effective rates and burdensome Form 8621 reporting requirements, for every year the position is held.

**This is not a minor footnote for a US citizen in Israel.** The general capital gains tax exemption that makes Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah attractive for a non-US-connected Israeli resident can be substantially offset - or worse - by PFIC exposure on the US side for someone who is also a US person. This is exactly the kind of situation where the "best domestic product" and the "best product accounting for your full tax picture" diverge sharply.

---

## The Comparison, Summarized

**For an Israeli resident with no US citizenship or US tax obligations:** Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah's capital gains exemption is a genuine, real advantage worth using, within the limits of the fund menu's actual US exposure.

**For a US citizen or green card holder resident in Israel:** the PFIC exposure this product likely creates needs to be weighed seriously against its Israeli-side tax benefit - for many US persons in this situation, a direct US brokerage account holding US-domiciled ETFs (which are not PFICs, since PFIC status applies to foreign, not US, investment vehicles) avoids the PFIC problem entirely, even though it forgoes the Israeli capital gains exemption.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Confirm whether you are a US citizen, green card holder, or otherwise a US person for tax purposes - this single fact determines whether the rest of this comparison tilts toward Kupat Gemel or a direct brokerage account
- [ ] If not a US person, evaluate Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah's specific fund menu for how much genuine US equity exposure it actually provides
- [ ] If a US person, get dedicated cross-border tax advice on your specific PFIC exposure before contributing meaningfully to this or any similar Israeli fund product
- [ ] If PFIC exposure is a concern, compare the real after-tax outcome of a direct US brokerage account (no PFIC issue, full US withholding per the [US-Israel tax treaty](/taxes/us-israel-tax-treaty-explained-capital-gains-dividends-2026/)) against Kupat Gemel's Israeli-side benefit weighed against its US-side cost

---

## Bottom Line

Kupat Gemel Lehashkaah offers a real, meaningful Israeli tax advantage and reasonable US equity exposure through its fund menu - a genuinely good option for an Israeli resident with no US tax connection. For a US citizen or green card holder in Israel, the same product likely creates real PFIC exposure that can outweigh its domestic tax benefit, making a direct US brokerage account the more carefully-considered choice despite forgoing the Israeli capital gains exemption.
