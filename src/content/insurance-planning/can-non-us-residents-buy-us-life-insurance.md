An investor who's read this site's coverage of how life insurance proceeds generally sit outside US-situs estate tax exposure for a non-resident alien, and goes looking to actually buy a US life insurance policy from abroad, often runs into a wall most standard US insurers simply weren't built for: many require a US residential address, a Social Security Number, and sometimes US citizenship or a specific US visa status just to underwrite a policy. The favorable tax treatment is real. Actually accessing a policy in the first place is a separate, genuinely harder question.

---

## Why Most Standard US Insurers Restrict Foreign Applicants

**Life insurance underwriting depends heavily on being able to verify an applicant's identity, health history, and financial background** - processes most US insurers have built specifically around US-based verification systems (US credit bureaus, US medical records access, US financial institution relationships). **A foreign national applicant, particularly one who has never lived in the US, often doesn't fit cleanly into these verification pipelines**, which is a large part of why many mainstream US carriers simply decline foreign applications by default rather than building out a separate underwriting process for a smaller segment of applicants.

**Insurable interest requirements add a further layer of friction.** US insurers generally require a demonstrable financial or personal connection between the policy and US interests - a US-based business relationship, US-situs assets the policy is meant to protect, or US family ties - rather than issuing a policy purely because a foreign investor wants the favorable estate-tax treatment covered in our [life insurance vs brokerage assets guide](/estate-planning/life-insurance-vs-brokerage-assets-estate-planning/). An applicant with no demonstrable US connection beyond wanting the tax benefit is likely to face a harder underwriting path than one with clear, documented ties.

---

## The "Foreign National" Life Insurance Market

**A specific segment of US insurers and specialized brokers explicitly serve foreign nationals**, generally requiring some documented connection to the US - US real estate ownership, a US business interest, US-based family members, or a history of regular US travel - combined with proof of financial capacity to pay premiums, often documented through non-US financial statements and international reference letters rather than a US credit history. **This market exists specifically because demand from foreign investors and high-net-worth individuals with US ties is real enough that specialized underwriting processes have been built around it** - it isn't a mainstream, walk-in-any-insurer's-website process, but it isn't nonexistent either.

**Working with a broker or agent who specifically specializes in foreign national life insurance** is generally the practical starting point, since these specialists know which carriers currently accept foreign applications, what documentation each requires, and how to structure an application that fits a foreign applicant's actual financial and residency profile - information not readily available by approaching a standard US insurer's general application process directly.

---

## What Documentation Foreign National Applications Typically Require

**Expect to provide more extensive documentation than a US resident applicant would**, commonly including: proof of the specific US connection justifying insurable interest, non-US financial statements or bank references demonstrating capacity to pay ongoing premiums, a passport and any relevant visa documentation, and sometimes a medical exam conducted in your home country by a physician the insurer's underwriting process recognizes. **The exact requirements vary meaningfully by insurer and by the applicant's specific country**, since some countries carry additional underwriting scrutiny due to insurers' own risk assessment of that jurisdiction - this isn't uniform across all foreign applicants.

---

## Alternatives When Direct US Access Isn't Available

**International or offshore life insurance policies**, issued by insurers based outside the US and specifically structured for internationally mobile individuals, are a genuinely different path worth considering when direct US insurer access proves difficult - covered in more depth in our [offshore portfolio bonds and private placement life insurance guide](/insurance-planning/offshore-portfolio-bonds-private-placement-life-insurance/). These policies don't carry the same US-situs treatment analysis as a US-issued policy, and the specific tax treatment (both US-side and home-country-side) needs independent confirmation rather than assuming it mirrors the US insurer scenario.

**A life insurance policy purchased in your home country**, from a domestic insurer you already have an established relationship or at least easier underwriting access with, is often the more practically accessible option - though the specific US-situs estate tax treatment that applies to a US-issued policy on a non-resident alien's life doesn't automatically extend to a foreign-issued policy in the same way, and this needs to be confirmed specifically rather than assumed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does holding a green card or US visa make it easier to get a US life insurance policy?**
Generally yes - a documented US residency or immigration status typically simplifies underwriting significantly compared to an applicant with no US residency status at all, since many of the standard verification processes insurers rely on become more directly applicable.

**Is it worth pursuing a US life insurance policy purely for the favorable estate tax treatment, without another underlying reason?**
This is worth discussing carefully with a cross-border estate planning professional rather than assuming it's automatically worthwhile - insurable interest requirements alone can make a policy difficult to obtain without a genuine underlying reason beyond the tax treatment, and the underwriting friction involved may not be justified if the projected estate tax exposure is modest.

**Do foreign national life insurance policies cost more than a policy for a US resident with similar health and age profile?**
Premiums can be higher for foreign national applicants in some cases, reflecting the additional underwriting complexity and risk assessment insurers apply, though this varies by insurer, country, and individual circumstances rather than following a single universal markup.

**Can a foreign national life insurance policy be used for the same estate-liquidity purpose covered in the brokerage-assets guide?**
Yes, in principle - a properly issued US life insurance policy on a foreign national's life generally follows the same US-situs treatment discussed in that guide, though confirming the specific policy's treatment with a professional remains essential given how much ownership structure and specific policy details can matter.

## Turning Can Non-US Residents Buy US Life Insurance? Access and Alternatives Into Actions

- [ ] Understand that most mainstream US insurers restrict or decline applications from foreign nationals with no US residency status
- [ ] Identify a genuine, documentable US connection (property, business, family ties) if pursuing a US-issued policy, since insurable interest requirements are real
- [ ] Work with a broker or agent specializing specifically in foreign national life insurance rather than approaching mainstream insurers directly
- [ ] Prepare more extensive documentation than a US resident applicant would need, including non-US financial statements and proof of premium-paying capacity
- [ ] Consider offshore or home-country policy alternatives if direct US access proves difficult, while separately confirming their tax treatment
- [ ] Confirm the specific estate tax treatment applies to your specific policy and structure before relying on it as a planning tool

---

## What Can Non-US Residents Buy US Life Insurance? Access and Alternatives Comes Down To

US life insurance access for foreign nationals is real but meaningfully harder than for US residents - most mainstream insurers restrict foreign applications, and accessing a policy generally requires a documented US connection, specialized broker guidance, and more extensive underwriting documentation. Understanding this access gap in advance, and identifying the specific path (foreign national specialist market, offshore policy, or home-country policy) that fits your actual situation, matters before assuming the favorable estate tax treatment is simply available for the asking.
