Our [needs-analysis guide](/insurance-planning/how-much-life-insurance-do-you-need-cross-border-investors/) covers how much coverage a cross-border investor actually needs, and our [term vs whole life comparison](/insurance-planning/term-vs-whole-life-insurance-international-investors/) covers which product type fits. This article answers a different, more practical question: once you know what you need, who will actually sell it to you as an expat, and what does it really cost? The answer is dominated by a small cluster of insurers most people have never heard of before they start shopping.

## The Providers That Actually Dominate This Market

**A handful of Isle of Man-domiciled international life offices write most expat life insurance globally**: RL360, Friends Provident International (FPI), Zurich International Life, Canada Life International, and Utmost International, which absorbed Lombard International Assurance in a completed December 2024 acquisition and now operates roughly £107 billion in assets under administration across some 200,000 customers as of mid-2025. These aren't household names in most home countries - they exist specifically to serve the internationally mobile.

**Concrete product data worth knowing:** RL360's LifePlan carries a minimum premium around $200/month, offers coverage up to $7.5 million, pays out in four currencies, and is sold in over 100 countries. A separate broker-underwritten option, William Russell, caps coverage at $2 million (or £1.6M/€1.8M) and up to 20 times annual salary - sample published rates include a 30-year-old non-smoker in Indonesia paying about $17/month for $250,000 of coverage, and a 45-year-old in France paying roughly $185/month for $800,000; applicants under 50 requesting $750,000 or less are often approved within 48 hours without a medical exam. **Expat Financial**, a brokerage rather than a direct insurer, quotes $250,000-$5 million in coverage across 1-30 year terms, available to residents of roughly 170 countries in EUR, GBP, or USD - with an excluded-country list running to 20-plus names, so availability isn't universal even through a broker with this much reach.

**One broker-sourced claim worth flagging rather than stating as settled fact:** Zurich and FPI reportedly scaled back from a broader footprint between 2013 and 2015 and now sell term products in only two to four expat-hub markets - this comes from a single review source, not confirmed independently, so treat it as a reason to check current market availability directly with the insurer rather than an established fact.

## Offshore vs a Home-Country Policy

**The Isle of Man is the default jurisdiction for this industry**, regulated by its own Financial Services Authority, with a Policyholder Compensation Scheme that covers roughly 90% of a claim - notably less than the 100% coverage the UK's domestic FSCS provides for comparable UK-based products, a real difference in protection worth understanding rather than assuming offshore means equivalently protected. Guernsey shows up more often for insurance-linked investment wrappers than pure term life, and Bermuda-domiciled structures are marketed specifically toward highly mobile clients anticipating eventual repatriation.

**A separate, US-based path exists for foreign nationals with US ties**: Guardian Life runs a "Global Citizens" program, but critically, the application, underwriting, medical exam, and policy delivery are all required to happen physically inside the US - a meaningful practical constraint if you're shopping from abroad and don't have an upcoming US trip to work around it.

## Can a US Citizen Abroad Keep or Buy a US-Domiciled Policy?

**Findings here are genuinely mixed, and worth treating cautiously rather than as settled.** Multiple sources describe death benefits as generally payable worldwide regardless of where the insured was living at death, but note that cash-value access, premium payment logistics, and the ability to make policy changes can all be complicated by relocation, in ways that vary by insurer. MetLife's Worldwide Benefits program reportedly requires the applicant to be on a US payroll and pre-approved through their employer - not a path open to every expat. Specific country-exclusion policies for major US insurers weren't confirmable from public sources in this research; they appear to be handled case-by-case rather than published, which means **directly asking your specific insurer about your specific country of residence is the only reliable way to know**, not inferring an answer from general guidance.

## What Expat Life Insurance Costs in Practice

**International/offshore policies commonly run 25-75% more expensive than a comparable domestic policy** - broker-sourced figures, not independently audited actuarial data, but consistent across multiple sources reviewed - reflecting broader underwriting standards, genuine cross-border mortality risk, multi-jurisdiction claims exposure, and simply thinner competition among the small number of insurers actually writing this business. One broker's own ranking of Isle of Man term products from cheapest to most expensive placed FPI Protector below Zurich International's term product, below RL360's LifePlan, below Zurich Futura specifically - treat this ordering as one broker's opinion rather than an independently verified ranking, since pricing on any specific policy depends heavily on your individual age, health, and coverage amount.

## The Claims Process Detail Most Guides Skip

**USD, GBP, and EUR are the standard currencies across the major expat insurers** - straightforward on the way in. The part that actually causes real friction is what happens if a claim needs to be filed after a death abroad: it generally requires a certified, translated death certificate, and for a US citizen specifically, a Consular Report of Death Abroad issued by the local US embassy. Insurers typically conduct their own "foreign death investigation" as part of processing, and the whole process commonly takes several weeks to a few months, not the faster timeline a domestic claim might see. **The most commonly cited reasons a claim gets denied or delayed aren't fraud - they're administrative**: undisclosed foreign travel or high-risk activity that wasn't flagged at application, a lapsed premium the family didn't realize had stopped being paid, or documentation that hasn't been properly translated and certified. This is worth knowing while you're still healthy and shopping, not after a claim is already in progress.

## Worth Knowing, Historically: Why Offshore Life Wrappers Draw Extra Scrutiny for US Persons

**This is genuinely historical context, not a 2026 development, and worth stating that plainly rather than dressing it up as current news:** Zurich International Life reached a resolution with the US Department of Justice in 2019 over offshore insurance-wrapper policies used to conceal US taxpayer assets, and its parent Swiss Life paid $77.3 million in a related 2021 agreement covering roughly 1,600 policies issued between 2005 and 2014. **The relevance today isn't that this is ongoing news - it's that it explains why offshore life insurance products, particularly investment-linked "wrapper" policies rather than straightforward term coverage, draw real FATCA and IRS scrutiny when a US person is involved**, and why a US-person applicant should expect more disclosure requirements from an offshore insurer than a non-US applicant would face for the same product.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a broker like Expat Financial better than going directly to an insurer like RL360?**
A broker can compare multiple insurers' terms for your specific country and situation in one place, which is genuinely useful given how much availability varies country to country - the tradeoff is that a broker's own excluded-country list or panel of insurers may not include every option a direct search might surface, so it's reasonable to check both paths rather than relying on just one.

**Does being a US citizen change which offshore insurers will cover me?**
Yes, meaningfully - US persons face additional FATCA-related disclosure requirements with offshore insurers precisely because of the industry history described above, and some offshore insurers restrict or decline US-person applicants entirely rather than take on that compliance burden. Confirm this directly and early, since it can rule out otherwise-suitable options before you invest time comparing quotes.

**If I move to a new country after buying an expat policy, do I need to notify my insurer?**
Generally yes - a change in country of residence, especially to a higher-risk-classified country, is exactly the kind of undisclosed change that shows up as a common claims-denial trigger later. Notify your insurer proactively rather than assuming a policy purchased under one country's circumstances stays valid unchanged after a move.

**Is term or whole life more commonly offered through these expat-specific insurers?**
Both exist, but the products and sample pricing referenced throughout this article are predominantly term structures - for the broader mechanical comparison between term and whole life itself, independent of which insurer you use, see our [term vs whole life guide](/insurance-planning/term-vs-whole-life-insurance-international-investors/).

## A Working List for Expat Life Insurance

- [ ] Start with the Isle of Man-domiciled insurers (RL360, FPI, Zurich International Life, Canada Life International, Utmost) and a specialist broker like Expat Financial, rather than assuming your home-country or US insurer will cover you abroad
- [ ] Confirm directly whether your specific country of residence is on any insurer's or broker's excluded list before spending time on a full quote
- [ ] Ask explicitly about US-person-specific restrictions if that applies to you, given the FATCA-related scrutiny offshore insurers apply
- [ ] Compare the Policyholder Compensation Scheme protection level (commonly ~90% on the Isle of Man) against what a domestic policy in your home country would offer, rather than assuming offshore means equally protected
- [ ] Notify your insurer proactively after any change in country of residence, since this is a common real-world claims-denial trigger
- [ ] Keep your documentation (proof of premium payment, any foreign travel disclosures) organized in a way your family could access and act on quickly, given the added translation/certification burden on a death-abroad claim

## Reading Expat Life Insurance Overall

Expat life insurance is a genuinely real, purchasable product - but it's written by a small cluster of specialist insurers most people have never heard of, at a real 25-75% premium over a comparable domestic policy, with meaningfully different consumer-protection guarantees than a purely domestic product carries. The coverage and underwriting side is generally the easier part; the part worth preparing for while you're still healthy is the claims process itself, since certified translation requirements and country-of-residence disclosure are the practical details that actually delay or complicate a claim, far more often than any dramatic underwriting dispute.
