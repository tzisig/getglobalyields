An investor who's already structured a life insurance policy to offset the projected US estate tax liability on a brokerage account, covered in our [life insurance vs brokerage assets guide](/estate-planning/life-insurance-vs-brokerage-assets-estate-planning/), sometimes hears from an advisor that the policy itself should be owned by a trust rather than directly by the insured. This isn't a change to the underlying US-situs tax treatment already covered - it's an additional, separate layer of structure addressing a different set of questions: who controls the policy, how proceeds get distributed, and how ownership itself is treated.

---

## What an ILIT Actually Changes

**An irrevocable life insurance trust is a trust specifically created to own a life insurance policy, rather than the insured individual owning it directly.** Once established and funded, the insured generally can't reclaim ownership, change beneficiaries directly, or otherwise treat the policy as their own asset - control passes to the trust and whoever administers it (the trustee), governed by the terms set out when the trust was created.

**The core reason this structure exists, in the standard US domestic context, is to remove the policy from the insured's own estate for tax purposes** - relevant for US citizens and residents where policy ownership by the insured can itself create estate tax exposure on the death benefit. For a non-resident alien, this specific tax-driven reason is less directly applicable in the same way, since life insurance proceeds already generally sit outside US-situs estate tax exposure for an NRA regardless of ownership structure, as covered in our base [life insurance vs brokerage assets guide](/estate-planning/life-insurance-vs-brokerage-assets-estate-planning/) - meaning an ILIT's value for a foreign investor comes from different benefits than the ones that make it standard practice for US citizens.

---

## Why a Foreign Investor Might Still Use One

**Control and distribution planning is the more relevant benefit for a non-resident alien.** A trust structure lets the policy owner specify exactly how and when proceeds get distributed to beneficiaries - staggered distributions over time, conditions tied to a beneficiary reaching a certain age, or protection from a beneficiary's own creditors or a future divorce - rather than the death benefit paying out as a single lump sum directly to a named individual beneficiary, which is what happens with direct ownership.

**Probate avoidance in multiple jurisdictions is a second, genuinely relevant benefit for internationally mobile investors.** A trust-owned policy can be structured to distribute proceeds without going through probate in either the US or the insured's home country, which matters more for an investor with meaningful assets and beneficiaries spread across multiple countries - each with its own probate process, timeline, and cost - than it does for a simpler, single-jurisdiction estate.

**Coordinating the policy with a broader cross-border estate structure** - alongside the LLC, trust, or direct-holding decisions covered in our [LLC vs trust vs direct holding guide](/estate-planning/llc-trust-vs-direct-holding-us-stocks/) - is a further reason a foreign investor with substantial, complex holdings across multiple asset types might want the life insurance component integrated into the same overall trust framework rather than sitting separately as a directly owned policy.

---

## The Real Complexity and Cost Trade-Off

**An ILIT is not a simple or low-cost structure to establish or maintain.** Setting one up generally requires specialized legal drafting, ongoing trustee administration (often an independent trustee, since the insured typically can't serve as trustee while also being the insured without undermining the structure's own purpose), and periodic compliance work - all of which carries real, ongoing cost that a directly owned policy simply doesn't.

**The three-year rule and funding timing matter in the standard domestic context**, and cross-border variants of these timing rules should be confirmed specifically with a professional rather than assumed to mirror the domestic US rules exactly - transferring an existing policy into a newly created ILIT, versus having the trust apply for and own a new policy from the start, can carry different treatment depending on jurisdiction and circumstances.

**For a foreign investor with a relatively modest, single-purpose policy** - sized specifically to cover a projected estate tax liability in the tens of thousands of dollars, the scale of example used in our base life insurance guide - the added cost and complexity of an ILIT structure often isn't justified relative to simply owning the policy directly with clearly designated beneficiaries. **The trust structure earns its complexity mainly for larger estates, more complicated beneficiary situations (minor children, special needs beneficiaries, multiple jurisdictions), or where the control and distribution-timing benefits genuinely matter** to the specific family situation.

---

## Setting One Up: What the Process Actually Involves

**Establishing an ILIT typically requires drafting the trust document itself** (specifying trustee powers, distribution terms, and beneficiary provisions), **selecting and appointing a trustee** (often an independent professional trustee rather than a family member, particularly for cross-border situations where ongoing administration across jurisdictions adds complexity), **funding the trust** so it has resources to pay ongoing premiums (commonly through gifts to the trust structured to use available gift tax exclusions, though the specific gift tax mechanics for a non-resident alien differ from the domestic US citizen case and need separate confirmation), **and having the trust itself apply for and own the policy**, or receiving an existing policy via transfer, depending on the specific structure chosen.

**This is not a do-it-yourself structure** - the legal, tax, and administrative complexity involved means an ILIT should be set up with a cross-border estate planning attorney experienced specifically with non-resident alien clients, not a generic domestic estate planning template applied without modification to a cross-border situation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is an ILIT required to get the favorable US-situs tax treatment on life insurance proceeds for a non-resident alien?**
No - the base US-situs treatment covered in our [life insurance vs brokerage assets guide](/estate-planning/life-insurance-vs-brokerage-assets-estate-planning/) generally applies to a directly owned policy without needing a trust structure at all. An ILIT adds control and distribution benefits on top of that base treatment, not the treatment itself.

**Does my home country recognize a US-style irrevocable trust the same way the US does?**
Not necessarily - trust recognition and tax treatment vary significantly by country, and some jurisdictions don't have an equivalent legal concept or tax it very differently than the US does. This is a critical point to confirm with an advisor familiar with both the US trust framework and your home country's specific treatment before establishing one.

**Can an ILIT hold other assets besides the life insurance policy?**
An ILIT is typically structured specifically to hold a life insurance policy and the funds needed to pay its premiums, rather than serving as a general-purpose holding vehicle for other investments - broader asset-holding structures are a separate question covered in our [LLC vs trust vs direct holding guide](/estate-planning/llc-trust-vs-direct-holding-us-stocks/).

**Who should serve as trustee for a foreign investor's ILIT?**
This is highly situation-specific, but an independent professional trustee with cross-border experience is commonly used specifically because self-trusteeship by the insured can undermine the structure's purpose, and a trustee unfamiliar with the specific cross-border tax and legal issues involved can create real administrative problems down the line.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Understand that an ILIT's main value for a non-resident alien is control and distribution planning, not the base US-situs tax treatment, which already applies without a trust
- [ ] Weigh the real ongoing cost and administrative complexity of an ILIT against the specific benefit it would provide for your situation
- [ ] Consider an ILIT more seriously for larger estates, complex beneficiary situations, or genuine multi-jurisdiction probate concerns
- [ ] Confirm how your home country recognizes and taxes a US-style irrevocable trust before establishing one
- [ ] Work with a cross-border estate planning attorney experienced with non-resident alien clients specifically, not a generic domestic template
- [ ] Select a trustee - typically an independent professional for cross-border situations - with genuine cross-border administrative experience

---

## Bottom Line

An ILIT adds a real layer of control and multi-jurisdiction distribution planning on top of the base favorable US-situs treatment life insurance already receives for a non-resident alien - but it's a genuinely complex, costly structure that mainly earns its place for larger estates or more complicated beneficiary situations, not for every foreign investor holding a modest, targeted policy. Understanding what an ILIT actually adds - and doesn't add - matters before taking on its added complexity.
