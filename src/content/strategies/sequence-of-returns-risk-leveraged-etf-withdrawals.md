Sequence of returns risk is one of the least intuitive concepts in retirement and withdrawal planning: two portfolios with the identical average annual return over 20 years can produce wildly different outcomes depending purely on the order those returns occurred in - specifically, whether the bad years happened early or late relative to when withdrawals began. For a portfolio holding leveraged ETFs during a withdrawal phase, this risk is meaningfully amplified.

---

## Why Order Matters More Than the Average

During the accumulation phase - contributing regularly, no withdrawals - the order of good and bad years matters relatively little to the final outcome; what matters is the average compounded return over the full period. **Once withdrawals begin, order matters enormously.** A severe market decline in the first few years of withdrawal forces selling a larger proportion of a smaller portfolio to generate the same dollar amount of income, permanently reducing the capital base available to benefit from the eventual recovery. The exact same decline occurring late in the withdrawal period, after years of growth, affects a much larger base and leaves substantially more capital intact.

**A simplified illustration:** two investors both average a 7% annual return over 20 years of withdrawals. Investor A experiences a -30% year in year one, followed by recovery and growth. Investor B experiences the identical -30% year in year 19, after 18 years of growth. Investor A, forced to withdraw from an already-diminished portfolio during and after the crash, can run out of money entirely before year 20 - despite having the exact same average return as Investor B, who finishes with a substantial portfolio intact.

---

## Why Leveraged ETFs Amplify This Risk Specifically

**Higher volatility means larger, more frequent sequence-risk events.** TQQQ's 3x leverage produces meaningfully larger drawdowns than the underlying Nasdaq-100 during down periods - covered throughout this site's [risk management](/strategies/risk-management-leveraged-etf-investors-2026/) and [long-term outlook](/strategies/tqqq-long-term-outlook/) content. A -30% Nasdaq-100 year can translate to a substantially larger TQQQ decline in the same period, which means the sequence-risk scenario described above is both more likely to occur and more severe when it does, for a portfolio holding TQQQ during withdrawals.

**Volatility decay compounds with sequence risk rather than being a separate, unrelated issue.** A choppy, volatile period - even one that isn't a clear directional crash - already erodes TQQQ's value relative to a simple 3x multiple of the index, due to the daily-rebalancing decay mechanics covered in our [rebalancing and decay guide](/strategies/leveraged-etf-rebalancing-reduce-decay/). During a withdrawal phase, this decay reduces the capital base at the same time withdrawals are also reducing it, a genuinely compounding double drag that doesn't apply to the accumulation phase in the same way.

---

## Practical Approaches to Managing This Risk

**De-risking before the withdrawal phase begins**, reducing or eliminating leveraged positions in the years immediately preceding planned withdrawals, is the most direct way to limit exposure to sequence risk specifically during the vulnerable early-withdrawal years - a deliberate, planned shift rather than a reactive one.

**A cash buffer or bond ballast** - similar to the conservative leg in the [barbell portfolio structure](/strategies/barbell-portfolio-tqqq-core-bond-ballast/) - covering several years of planned withdrawals in genuinely stable assets allows withdrawals to be funded from that buffer during a market decline, rather than forcing sales of a leveraged position at depressed prices.

**Flexible withdrawal rates**, reducing withdrawal amounts during confirmed down periods rather than maintaining a fixed dollar or fixed percentage withdrawal regardless of portfolio performance, reduces the forced-selling dynamic that makes sequence risk so damaging in the first place.

---

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] If you are approaching a planned withdrawal phase, consider deliberately reducing leveraged ETF exposure in the years immediately before withdrawals begin, rather than maintaining full leverage into the vulnerable period
- [ ] Build a cash or bond buffer covering several years of planned withdrawals, specifically to avoid forced selling of leveraged positions during a downturn
- [ ] Build flexibility into your withdrawal plan rather than committing to a fixed amount regardless of market conditions
- [ ] Understand that TQQQ's structural volatility decay compounds with sequence risk during withdrawals, not just during accumulation - this is a genuinely different risk profile from holding the same position while still contributing

---

## Bottom Line

Sequence of returns risk is a real, well-documented phenomenon in withdrawal planning generally, and leveraged ETFs amplify it specifically because their volatility - and the resulting decay - is structurally higher than the underlying index during exactly the kind of choppy or declining periods that do the most damage early in a withdrawal phase. This argues for deliberately de-risking leveraged positions before withdrawals begin, rather than carrying full TQQQ exposure through a phase where the order of returns, not just their average, determines the outcome.
