Some investors choose to build their own concentrated position in a handful of mega-cap tech names - the largest Nasdaq-100 constituents - rather than holding TQQQ, on the reasoning that picking the specific winners themselves gives more control than a leveraged index wrapper. This comparison is worth making carefully, because the intuition about "more control" doesn't hold up as cleanly as it first appears.

---

## What Each Approach Actually Concentrates Risk In

**TQQQ concentrates risk in the Nasdaq-100 as a whole, amplified 3x.** The index itself is already meaningfully concentrated in a handful of mega-cap technology names by weighting, but TQQQ's exposure spans all 100 constituents, weighted according to the index methodology - genuine (if imperfect) breadth across the full index, leveraged.

**A hand-picked portfolio of 5-10 mega-cap tech names concentrates risk in whichever specific companies the investor selected**, with no leverage multiplier, but also with none of the breadth the full index provides. A single company-specific event - a regulatory action, a product failure, an earnings miss, a key executive departure - hits a hand-picked concentrated portfolio far harder in proportional terms than the same event hits TQQQ, where any single company's weighting within the broader 100-stock index limits its individual impact.

---

## The "More Control" Intuition, Examined

The appeal of stock-picking is the sense of control - choosing exactly which companies to own, rather than accepting whatever the index methodology happens to include. **This intuition is real but doesn't translate into lower risk.** Choosing five specific companies instead of accepting an index's 100 does not reduce single-company risk - it concentrates it further, since any one of those five companies now represents a much larger share of the total position than it would within the full index. "Control" over which companies you own is not the same as "reduced risk" from any individual company's specific problems.

**A genuine, measurable difference:** a hand-picked portfolio has no leverage-driven volatility decay (covered throughout this site's [rebalancing and decay content](/strategies/leveraged-etf-rebalancing-reduce-decay/)), since it isn't using daily-rebalanced leverage at all. This is a real structural advantage of the stock-picking approach specifically relative to TQQQ - though it comes paired with the concentration risk described above, and does not include TQQQ's amplified upside in a genuine broad-index rally.

---

## A Worked Comparison

**TQQQ:** exposure to all 100 Nasdaq-100 constituents, weighted by the index methodology, amplified 3x daily. A severe drawdown in any single constituent affects the position only to the extent of that company's index weighting, times three.

**A hand-picked 5-stock tech portfolio:** exposure to exactly five companies, unleveraged. A severe drawdown in any one of the five affects roughly 20% of the position directly (assuming equal weighting), with no offsetting exposure to the other 95 Nasdaq-100 constituents that TQQQ would still hold.

**Neither is straightforwardly "safer."** TQQQ trades single-company concentration risk for amplified index-wide volatility and structural decay. The hand-picked portfolio trades leverage and decay risk for genuine single-company concentration risk that a broader index, even a leveraged one, does not carry to the same degree.

---

## Practical Considerations

- **Confirm your actual reason for preferring stock-picking** - if it's genuinely about avoiding leverage and decay, a hand-picked unleveraged portfolio does achieve that. If it's about "picking winners" for higher returns than the index, be honest that this requires being right about specific company selection in a way that index exposure does not.
- **Check actual position-level concentration** in a hand-picked portfolio against the index weighting of the same companies within the Nasdaq-100 - the difference is often larger than intuition suggests.
- **Consider a blend** - some investors hold a core TQQQ or QQQ position for broad index exposure alongside smaller, deliberate positions in specific companies they have genuine conviction in, rather than choosing exclusively between the two approaches.

---

## Bottom Line

TQQQ and a hand-picked concentrated tech portfolio both carry real, but different, forms of concentration risk - TQQQ in the Nasdaq-100 as a whole, amplified by leverage; a stock-picked portfolio in whichever specific companies were chosen, without leverage but with genuine single-company exposure the broader index does not carry to the same degree. Neither is a straightforwardly safer choice than the other; they are different risk trades, not a spectrum from "risky" to "safe."
