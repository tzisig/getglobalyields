Search "best robo-advisor" and you will get the same handful of names every time: Betterment, Wealthfront, Schwab Intelligent Portfolios. Nearly all of that content is written for US residents, and for good reason - these platforms are, in practice, essentially closed to non-US residents due to FATCA-related compliance restrictions that most US-focused robo-advisors have adopted. If you are researching this topic from outside the US, most of what you find will not actually apply to you, and it's worth understanding specifically why before spending time trying to open an account anyway.

---

## Why the Big Names Don't Work for You

Betterment, Wealthfront, and Schwab Intelligent Portfolios are built around US retail investors and generally require a US residential address and US tax identification to open an account. This is a compliance and business-model choice rather than a technical limitation - automated portfolio management involves ongoing regulatory obligations that these platforms have chosen to manage only for US-resident clients, given the added complexity and cost of building out compliance infrastructure for international account holders that many of these firms simply haven't prioritized.

**Why this differs from a standard international brokerage**, covered throughout our [best-brokers category](/best-brokers/): a standard brokerage account is a comparatively simpler regulatory product (you direct your own trades), while a robo-advisor is providing ongoing discretionary investment management - a more heavily regulated activity in most jurisdictions, requiring additional licensing and compliance infrastructure the mainstream US robo-advisors haven't built out for a non-US client base. Confirm current eligibility directly if you want to test this for your specific situation, but treat rejection as the default expectation rather than something worth spending significant time pursuing.

## What Actually Serves International Investors: Interactive Advisors

**Interactive Advisors**, the automated portfolio platform affiliated with Interactive Brokers, is the genuine option in this space for non-US residents. Because it operates on IBKR's existing international account infrastructure - the same infrastructure that accepts clients from 200+ countries for standard brokerage accounts, covered throughout our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/) - it extends meaningfully broader international access than the mainstream US robo-advisor names, leveraging compliance infrastructure IBKR had already built for its core brokerage business.

**What it offers:**
- A choice between traditional automated Asset Allocation portfolios (automatically rebalanced) and a large selection of thematic and manager-driven portfolios you can follow, giving more flexibility than the more standardized model-portfolio approach most mainstream robo-advisors use
- A management fee around 0.20% - competitive with, or lower than, most mainstream robo-advisors, on top of whatever the underlying funds within the selected portfolio charge
- Automated rebalancing, with tax-loss harvesting features available depending on account type - though the practical value of tax-loss harvesting depends heavily on your specific home country's tax treatment of investment losses, covered throughout this site's [taxes category](/taxes/), and doesn't automatically translate into the same benefit a US taxpayer would see
- A relatively low minimum (commonly cited around $100 for many portfolios), making it accessible to investors just starting out rather than requiring a large initial deposit

**What to confirm before committing:** country eligibility for Interactive Advisors specifically, separate from standard IBKR brokerage account eligibility - confirm this directly, since automated advisory services sometimes carry additional country-specific restrictions beyond the base brokerage account, given the additional regulatory considerations discretionary management can trigger in certain jurisdictions.

## The Tax-Loss Harvesting Caveat Worth Understanding

**A commonly cited robo-advisor feature - automated tax-loss harvesting - is specifically designed around US tax rules**, including the wash-sale rule and US capital gains/loss offsetting mechanics. For a non-resident alien, whose US capital gains are generally untaxed in the first place (the standard rule covered throughout this site's [main dividend withholding guide](/taxes/taxes-us-dividends-foreign-investors/)), the US-side tax benefit of automated tax-loss harvesting may simply not apply the way it would for a US taxpayer - meaning this specific, often-marketed feature may be less valuable to many of this site's international readers than the marketing materials for any robo-advisor platform suggest. Whether it has value for your specific home country's tax treatment is a separate question worth confirming rather than assuming based on how the feature is generally described.

## The Alternative: Building Your Own "Robo" Portfolio Manually

For many international investors, the practical alternative to a true robo-advisor is simpler than it sounds: a small number of broad ETFs (covered in our [strategies](/strategies/) and [index-funds](/index-funds/) categories), bought on a regular schedule, rebalanced once or twice a year. This does not offer automatic tax-loss harvesting or algorithmic rebalancing, but it achieves most of what a robo-advisor's core value proposition actually is - diversified, low-cost, hands-off investing - without needing a platform that may not accept your country of residence in the first place, and without paying an ongoing management fee on top of the underlying funds' own expense ratios.

**A simple structure many investors adopt as a manual alternative:** the [3-fund portfolio](/index-funds/building-3-fund-portfolio-international-investor/) covered elsewhere on this site - a small number of broad, low-cost funds, purchased on a regular schedule, with an annual rebalancing check-in - captures most of a robo-advisor's practical benefit for a fraction of the ongoing cost and without any country-eligibility risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are there other robo-advisor platforms beyond Interactive Advisors that serve international clients?**
Some other international-facing brokers occasionally offer automated portfolio or model-portfolio features, though coverage and country eligibility vary considerably and change over time - worth checking directly with any specific broker you're already considering for other reasons, rather than assuming broad availability across the industry.

**Does a robo-advisor handle currency conversion for international investors automatically?**
This depends on the specific platform - Interactive Advisors, built on IBKR's infrastructure, generally follows the same multi-currency funding mechanics covered in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/), rather than a separate, dedicated currency-conversion process specific to the advisory product.

**Is a robo-advisor's underlying fund selection different from what I'd choose building a portfolio manually?**
Not necessarily - many robo-advisors, including Interactive Advisors' Asset Allocation portfolios, are built from broad, low-cost ETFs similar to what an informed investor would select manually; the main value-add of the robo-advisor is the automated rebalancing and portfolio construction convenience, not access to fundamentally different underlying investments.

**Does using a robo-advisor change my W-8BEN or dividend withholding treatment?**
No - the standard NRA dividend withholding framework covered throughout this site's [taxes category](/taxes/) applies to holdings within a robo-advisor account the same way it applies to a standard self-directed brokerage account, since the underlying tax treatment depends on your own tax status and the securities held, not on whether a human or an algorithm selected them.
