"Zero commission" is one of the most misleading phrases in retail investing. A broker can genuinely charge $0 per trade and still cost you meaningfully more than a broker with a small explicit commission, once FX spreads, thresholds, and other embedded costs are accounted for. This guide covers the real cost structure behind the main zero-commission options available to international investors, not just the headline number that dominates each platform's marketing.

---

## XTB - Zero Commission Up to a Monthly Threshold

[XTB](/best-brokers/xtb-review-2026/) offers 0% commission on real stocks and ETFs up to €100,000 in monthly trading volume, with automatic interest paid on uninvested cash balances (rates vary by currency). Above the threshold, standard commission rates apply, a detail easy to overlook if you're only reading the headline pricing.

**The real cost to know about:** a 0.5% FX conversion fee on cross-currency trades. For an investor regularly converting a non-USD, non-EUR currency to buy US stocks, this fee is the actual cost center, not the headline "zero commission" claim - a $2,000 conversion at 0.5% costs $10, a real, recurring cost on every single funding transaction even though the trade itself carries no explicit commission.

---

## eToro - Zero Commission Funded by FX Spread

eToro offers commission-free stock and ETF trading, available across 100+ countries. The trade-off: eToro's zero-commission model is funded partly through FX conversion spreads on non-USD deposits (commonly cited around 150 basis points) and through spreads on CFD products for investors using those instruments.

**The real cost to know about:** for investors depositing in a non-USD currency, the conversion spread on deposit and withdrawal is where eToro actually earns its margin - factor this into any cost comparison, particularly for larger or more frequent transactions. A 150-basis-point spread on a $5,000 deposit costs $75 - a genuinely larger real cost than XTB's 0.5% fee on the same amount ($25), despite both platforms marketing themselves as commission-free.

---

## Interactive Brokers (IBKR Lite) - Zero Commission Where Available

IBKR Lite offers $0 commission on US-listed stocks and ETFs, but availability for non-US clients is more limited than IBKR's standard Pro tier - confirm current eligibility for your country before assuming Lite pricing applies to you. Where Lite is not available, IBKR Pro's tiered commission structure ($0.0005/share, typically with a small minimum) still delivers among the lowest all-in costs available to international investors once IBKR's near-interbank FX conversion rates are factored in.

**The real cost to know about:** for many international investors, IBKR Pro's small explicit commission, combined with genuinely excellent FX rates (0.03 basis points, covered in more detail in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/)), ends up cheaper overall than a "zero commission" competitor with a wider embedded FX spread - the headline number is not the full picture, and comparing only the stated commission figure across brokers systematically misleads in IBKR's disfavor despite it often being the actual lowest-cost option for regular currency converters.

## How to Actually Compare These for Your Situation

**If you trade infrequently and in small amounts:** the headline zero-commission brokers (XTB below the threshold, eToro) are genuinely simple and cost-effective, since the FX spread on a small, infrequent transaction is a small absolute dollar amount - the added simplicity of a straightforward zero-commission platform can outweigh a modest cost gap for an investor who isn't converting or trading frequently.

**If you invest regularly and convert currency often:** run the actual math on FX costs specifically, using your own typical contribution size and frequency - a broker with excellent FX rates and a small explicit commission (IBKR) can be cheaper overall than a "free" competitor once conversion spreads are included, and the gap compounds meaningfully the more frequently you're converting currency over a long investing career.

**If you trade above XTB's €100,000 monthly threshold:** the zero-commission benefit disappears entirely above that volume, worth knowing if you are a more active trader rather than a buy-and-hold, periodic-contribution investor.

## Why This Matters More the Longer You Invest

**A one-time cost comparison understates the actual stakes for a long-term investor.** An investor making monthly contributions over a 20-30 year investing career will convert currency dozens or hundreds of times over that period - a seemingly small per-transaction spread difference, multiplied across that many transactions, compounds into a genuinely significant total cost gap between the cheapest and most expensive options in this comparison. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does "zero commission" ever apply to options trading as well as stock trading?**
Options trading generally carries its own separate fee structure at most brokers, distinct from the stock/ETF commission structure covered in this guide - confirm options-specific pricing separately if options income strategies, covered throughout our [options-income category](/options-income/), are part of your plan.

**Do these brokers charge anything for holding cash, beyond the FX conversion spread?**
This varies - some brokers pay interest on uninvested cash balances (mentioned for XTB above), while IBKR's cash yield structure is covered in more detail in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/); confirm current rates directly, since they change with broader interest rate conditions.

**Is there a "best" zero-commission broker, or does it genuinely depend on my situation?**
It genuinely depends on your specific contribution size, frequency, and home currency - the worked comparison in this guide is designed to help you run the numbers for your own situation rather than pointing to a single universal winner, since the ranking can change based on how much and how often you're converting currency.

**Do withdrawal fees factor into this comparison as well as deposit/conversion fees?**
Yes, in principle - some platforms apply their FX spread symmetrically to both deposits and withdrawals, meaning the total round-trip cost (funding the account, and eventually withdrawing or repatriating funds, covered in our [repatriating gains guide](/currency-banking/repatriating-us-investment-gains-home-currency/)) can be higher than a deposit-only comparison suggests - worth checking both directions before finalizing your comparison.

## Bottom Line

Every broker in this comparison genuinely offers $0 explicit commission on at least some trades - the honest differentiator is where each one recovers its margin instead, most commonly through FX conversion spreads. For infrequent, smaller international investors, the headline zero-commission brokers are a reasonable, simple choice. For anyone converting currency regularly or trading in larger size, running the real all-in cost - FX included - against IBKR's structure is worth the extra few minutes of comparison, particularly given how much a small per-transaction difference compounds over a long investing career.
