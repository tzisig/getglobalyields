"Zero commission" is one of the most misleading phrases in retail investing. A broker can genuinely charge $0 per trade and still cost you meaningfully more than a broker with a small explicit commission, once FX spreads, thresholds, and other embedded costs are accounted for. This guide covers the real cost structure behind the main zero-commission options available to international investors, not just the headline number.

---

## XTB - Zero Commission Up to a Monthly Threshold

[XTB](/best-brokers/xtb-review-2026/) offers 0% commission on real stocks and ETFs up to €100,000 in monthly trading volume, with automatic interest paid on uninvested cash balances (rates vary by currency). Above the threshold, standard commission rates apply.

**The real cost to know about:** a 0.5% FX conversion fee on cross-currency trades. For an investor regularly converting a non-USD, non-EUR currency to buy US stocks, this fee is the actual cost center, not the headline "zero commission" claim.

---

## eToro - Zero Commission Funded by FX Spread

eToro offers commission-free stock and ETF trading, available across 100+ countries. The trade-off: eToro's zero-commission model is funded partly through FX conversion spreads on non-USD deposits (commonly cited around 150 basis points) and through spreads on CFD products for investors using those instruments.

**The real cost to know about:** for investors depositing in a non-USD currency, the conversion spread on deposit and withdrawal is where eToro actually earns its margin - factor this into any cost comparison, particularly for larger or more frequent transactions.

---

## Interactive Brokers (IBKR Lite) - Zero Commission Where Available

IBKR Lite offers $0 commission on US-listed stocks and ETFs, but availability for non-US clients is more limited than IBKR's standard Pro tier - confirm current eligibility for your country before assuming Lite pricing applies to you. Where Lite is not available, IBKR Pro's tiered commission structure ($0.0005/share, typically with a small minimum) still delivers among the lowest all-in costs available to international investors once IBKR's near-interbank FX conversion rates are factored in.

**The real cost to know about:** for many international investors, IBKR Pro's small explicit commission, combined with genuinely excellent FX rates, ends up cheaper overall than a "zero commission" competitor with a wider embedded FX spread - the headline number is not the full picture.

## The Actual Comparison: All-In Cost, Not Headline Commission

| Broker | Headline Commission | Where the Real Cost Is |
|---|---|---|
| XTB | $0 up to €100k/month | 0.5% FX conversion fee |
| eToro | $0 | ~150 bps FX spread on deposit/withdrawal, CFD spreads |
| IBKR Lite (where available) | $0 | Near-interbank FX - genuinely low all-in cost |
| IBKR Pro | Small per-share fee | Offset by near-interbank FX - often cheapest all-in for active or larger accounts |

---

## How to Actually Compare These for Your Situation

- **If you trade infrequently and in small amounts:** the headline zero-commission brokers (XTB below the threshold, eToro) are genuinely simple and cost-effective, since the FX spread on a small, infrequent transaction is a small absolute dollar amount.
- **If you invest regularly and convert currency often:** run the actual math on FX costs specifically - a broker with excellent FX rates and a small explicit commission (IBKR) can be cheaper overall than a "free" competitor once conversion spreads are included.
- **If you trade above XTB's €100,000 monthly threshold:** the zero-commission benefit disappears entirely above that volume, worth knowing if you are a more active trader.

---

## Bottom Line

Every broker in this comparison genuinely offers $0 explicit commission on at least some trades - the honest differentiator is where each one recovers its margin instead, most commonly through FX conversion spreads. For infrequent, smaller international investors, the headline zero-commission brokers are a reasonable, simple choice. For anyone converting currency regularly or trading in larger size, running the real all-in cost - FX included - against IBKR's structure is worth the extra five minutes of comparison.
