Choosing a broker as a European investor comes with one constraint that does not apply almost anywhere else covered on this site: the PRIIPs regulation blocks direct access to US-domiciled ETFs like VOO and QQQ, regardless of which broker you use. The real question for a European investor is not "which broker gives me the widest access" - it is which broker offers the best UCITS ETF selection, the lowest costs, and correctly handles treaty documentation for the individual US stocks you can still buy directly.

---

## What Every European Investor Needs to Know First

Regardless of broker, the PRIIPs regulation means you will be buying Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs (like CSPX for the S&P 500 or VWCE for global equity) instead of their US-domiciled equivalents - covered in full mechanical detail in our [UCITS vs US-domiciled comparison](/index-funds/ucits-vs-us-domiciled-etfs-tax-comparison/), including the genuine dividend withholding and estate tax advantages this restriction incidentally provides on top of being a forced compliance requirement. This is also covered in our country-specific guides for [Germany](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-germany/), [Netherlands](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-netherlands/), [Spain](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-spain/), [Italy](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-italy/), and [Poland](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-poland/). Individual US stocks remain fully accessible - the restriction is specifically on packaged products.

### 4. Saxo Bank - Best for Institutional-Grade Tools

### 5. eToro - Best for a Simple, App-First Entry Point

[eToro](/best-brokers/etoro-review-2026/) is regulated across multiple European jurisdictions and offers a simpler, more visual interface than the platforms above, with copy-trading features that some investors find genuinely useful as a starting point. **Best for:** first-time investors who want the simplest possible onboarding.

## What to Check Before Committing

- **W-8BEN handling:** confirm the broker processes W-8BEN digitally and applies your country's treaty rate automatically to individual US stock dividends - this is standard at all five brokers above but worth confirming during onboarding, since an incorrectly processed W-8BEN can result in the full 30% default withholding rather than your treaty rate, covered in more detail in our [W-8BEN guide](/taxes/w8ben-form-non-us-investors/).
- **UCITS ETF selection:** confirm the specific funds you want (CSPX, VWCE, or others) are actually tradable on the platform - selection varies slightly by broker, and a platform's overall UCITS support doesn't guarantee every specific fund you want is available on it.
- **FX conversion costs:** this is often a larger real-world cost than the headline commission rate, particularly for brokers that build in a spread rather than charging a transparent conversion fee - the same distinction covered throughout our [zero-commission brokers comparison](/best-brokers/best-zero-commission-brokers-international-investors/) and [currency-banking category](/currency-banking/) generally.
- **Accumulating vs distributing share class availability:** for the specific UCITS funds you're targeting, confirm which share classes are available on your chosen platform, since this choice has real tax-timing implications covered in our [accumulating vs distributing guide](/index-funds/accumulating-vs-distributing-etfs/) and interacts differently with each European country's own tax rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the PRIIPs restriction apply to individual US stocks the same way it applies to ETFs?**
No - PRIIPs specifically targets packaged retail investment products (funds, structured products), not individual company shares; a European investor can generally buy individual US stocks like Apple or Microsoft directly through any of the brokers above, with the restriction applying specifically to fund products like US-domiciled ETFs.

**Is there any advantage to choosing a broker headquartered in my specific home country over a pan-European one?**
This is largely a matter of personal comfort and, in some cases, local customer support quality or language availability, rather than a fundamental access or cost difference - all five brokers covered in this guide operate under EU/European regulatory frameworks that provide broadly comparable investor protections, though specifics vary and are worth confirming for your situation.

**Do any of these brokers offer better US estate tax outcomes than others?**
No - broker choice doesn't affect US estate tax situs, which depends on the underlying asset's domicile (US vs non-US fund, covered in our [estate-planning category](/estate-planning/)), not which broker holds it; this is a fund-selection decision, not a broker-selection decision.

**Should I prioritize the broker with the lowest fees, or the one with the best UCITS fund selection?**
This depends on your specific target funds and investing style - an investor with a clear, narrow target fund list (a handful of core UCITS ETFs) should prioritize confirming those specific funds are available before optimizing purely for the lowest fee structure, since a marginally cheaper broker that doesn't offer your target fund isn't actually the better choice for your situation.
