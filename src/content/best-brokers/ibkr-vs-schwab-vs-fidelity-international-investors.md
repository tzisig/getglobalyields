Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab, and Fidelity are the three names that come up most often when researching US brokers - they are the giants of the US retail brokerage industry. Naturally, international investors researching where to open a US-facing account run into all three. The honest answer: they are not equally accessible, and Fidelity in particular is far more closed to genuine non-US residents than its size and reputation would suggest.

---

## Interactive Brokers - Built for International Access From the Ground Up

[Interactive Brokers](/best-brokers/interactive-brokers-review-2026-the-best-broker-for-international-investors/) accepts account applications from residents of 200+ countries and territories, with W-8BEN and treaty documentation handled digitally as part of onboarding. This is the broker's core business model, not an accommodation - IBKR was built to serve a genuinely global client base from the start.

**For a non-US resident:** this is the most straightforward of the three, with the broadest country eligibility and the most mature international onboarding process.

---

## Charles Schwab International - Built Mainly for US Expats

Charles Schwab does offer international account access through Schwab International, but its primary audience is **US citizens and green card holders living abroad**, not foreign nationals with no US connection. Schwab International does accept some non-US-citizen international clients depending on country of residence, but its onboarding and ongoing service model is optimized around the US-expat use case - accounts linked to a US Social Security Number, US tax reporting already in place, and so on.

**For a genuine foreign national with no US citizenship or prior US tax presence:** Schwab is workable in some countries but is not the natural first choice the way it is for an American living abroad.

---

## Fidelity - The Most Restrictive of the Three by a Wide Margin

This is the comparison most guides get wrong. Fidelity's main US operation **does not open new accounts for customers residing outside the United States.** The commonly cited workaround - Fidelity International - is a genuinely separate entity with its own, much narrower country eligibility list, historically centered on a small number of markets including the UK for self-directed accounts, with a somewhat longer list for managed accounts specifically.

**What this means practically:** if you are a non-US resident without US citizenship researching "Fidelity for international investors," the honest answer for most countries is that it is simply not an option, full stop - not a matter of finding the right account type, but a genuine country-eligibility wall. Confirm your specific country against Fidelity International's current eligibility list directly before assuming it is a viable choice.

## Practical Guidance

**If you are a foreign national with no US citizenship or residency history:** Interactive Brokers is almost always the right starting point among these three. Schwab is worth checking only if your specific country is on its non-expat international eligibility list. Fidelity is very unlikely to be an option at all - confirm this before spending time on an application.

**If you are a US citizen or green card holder living abroad:** Schwab International is specifically built for your situation and is worth serious consideration alongside IBKR.

---

## Bottom Line

These three brokers get grouped together constantly in general "best US broker" content, but they serve meaningfully different audiences. For the typical international investor this site is written for - a non-US citizen with no prior US tax presence - Interactive Brokers is the broker actually built for that situation, Schwab is a secondary option worth checking against your specific country, and Fidelity is, for most readers, simply not accessible.
