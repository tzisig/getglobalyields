Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab, and Fidelity are the three names that come up most often when researching US brokers - they are the giants of the US retail brokerage industry. Naturally, international investors researching where to open a US-facing account run into all three. The honest answer: they are not equally accessible, and Fidelity in particular is far more closed to genuine non-US residents than its size and reputation would suggest, which is exactly the kind of mismatch this site exists to correct.

---

## Interactive Brokers - Built for International Access From the Ground Up

[Interactive Brokers](/best-brokers/interactive-brokers-review-2026-the-best-broker-for-international-investors/) accepts account applications from residents of 200+ countries and territories, with W-8BEN and treaty documentation handled digitally as part of onboarding, covered in more detail in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/). This is the broker's core business model, not an accommodation - IBKR was built to serve a genuinely global client base from the start, and its entire product, compliance, and support infrastructure reflects that founding orientation rather than treating international access as a secondary feature bolted onto a primarily domestic business.

**For a non-US resident:** this is the most straightforward of the three, with the broadest country eligibility and the most mature international onboarding process, built around exactly the kind of investor this site is written for.

---

## Charles Schwab International - Built Mainly for US Expats

Charles Schwab does offer international account access through Schwab International, but its primary audience is **US citizens and green card holders living abroad**, not foreign nationals with no US connection. Schwab International does accept some non-US-citizen international clients depending on country of residence, but its onboarding and ongoing service model is optimized around the US-expat use case - accounts linked to a US Social Security Number, US tax reporting already in place, and so on.

**Why this distinction matters practically:** a US citizen living in London already has a US Social Security Number, is already filing US tax returns regardless of where they live (a US citizen's worldwide income tax obligation, a genuinely different situation from the non-resident alien framework covered throughout the rest of this site), and simply needs a brokerage account that continues serving them from abroad. A foreign national with no US citizenship is a fundamentally different applicant profile - no SSN, no prior US tax filing history, needing W-8BEN treaty documentation rather than the standard US-citizen tax reporting Schwab's core systems are built around.

**For a genuine foreign national with no US citizenship or prior US tax presence:** Schwab is workable in some countries but is not the natural first choice the way it is for an American living abroad.

---

## Fidelity - The Most Restrictive of the Three by a Wide Margin

This is the comparison most guides get wrong. Fidelity's main US operation **does not open new accounts for customers residing outside the United States.** The commonly cited workaround - Fidelity International - is a genuinely separate entity with its own, much narrower country eligibility list, historically centered on a small number of markets including the UK for self-directed accounts, with a somewhat longer list for managed accounts specifically.

**What this means practically:** if you are a non-US resident without US citizenship researching "Fidelity for international investors," the honest answer for most countries is that it is simply not an option, full stop - not a matter of finding the right account type, but a genuine country-eligibility wall. Confirm your specific country against Fidelity International's current eligibility list directly before assuming it is a viable choice, rather than assuming Fidelity's overall market size and reputation translates into broad international access the way it does for IBKR.

**Why this gap between reputation and access exists:** Fidelity's core business has historically been built almost entirely around the US domestic retail and institutional market, without the same deliberate international expansion strategy IBKR pursued. This isn't a flaw in Fidelity as a broker for its intended audience - it's simply a mismatch between Fidelity's actual target market and what a genuine international investor researching this site's content is looking for.

## Practical Guidance

**If you are a foreign national with no US citizenship or residency history:** Interactive Brokers is almost always the right starting point among these three. Schwab is worth checking only if your specific country is on its non-expat international eligibility list. Fidelity is very unlikely to be an option at all - confirm this before spending time on an application, since the account-opening process itself can take meaningful time and effort you'd rather not spend on an application likely to be rejected outright.

**If you are a US citizen or green card holder living abroad:** Schwab International is specifically built for your situation and is worth serious consideration alongside IBKR - though note that as a US person, the entire framework of non-resident alien withholding, W-8BEN documentation, and treaty rates covered throughout most of this site's [taxes category](/taxes/) doesn't apply to you in the same way; you're subject to standard US citizen worldwide taxation regardless of where you live.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does IBKR's broad international access come with any trade-off compared to Schwab or Fidelity's domestic offering?**
IBKR's platform (particularly Trader Workstation) has a real learning curve that some investors find more complex than Schwab's or Fidelity's more streamlined domestic-focused interfaces, covered generally in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/) - though IBKR's GlobalTrader mobile app offers a more accessible entry point for investors who don't need the full platform's depth.

**If my country is on Fidelity International's limited eligibility list, is it worth choosing over IBKR?**
This depends on your specific priorities - Fidelity's fund research and specific product offerings may appeal to some investors, but IBKR's broader international infrastructure, more competitive FX handling, and more mature non-resident tax documentation process make it the more commonly recommended default even where Fidelity International is technically available.

**Can a non-US resident use Schwab's regular US-domestic brokerage account instead of Schwab International specifically?**
Generally no - Schwab's standard domestic account offering typically requires a US address and other domestic-specific documentation, meaning a genuine non-US resident would need to go through Schwab International's specific (and more limited) international application process rather than the standard domestic one.

**Does this comparison change if I have a green card but don't currently live in the US?**
A green card holder is generally treated similarly to a US citizen for US tax purposes (worldwide income taxation) regardless of current physical location, putting this situation closer to the "US citizen/green card holder abroad" category this guide discusses for Schwab International, rather than the non-resident alien framework covered throughout most of this site.

## Bottom Line

These three brokers get grouped together constantly in general "best US broker" content, but they serve meaningfully different audiences. For the typical international investor this site is written for - a non-US citizen with no prior US tax presence - Interactive Brokers is the broker actually built for that situation, Schwab is a secondary option worth checking against your specific country (and the natural choice for a US expat abroad), and Fidelity is, for most readers, simply not accessible.
