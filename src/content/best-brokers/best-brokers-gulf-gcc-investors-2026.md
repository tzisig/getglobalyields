Gulf and GCC-based investors - across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and the wider region - have one genuine advantage most investors on this site don't: no EU-style PRIIPs restriction blocking direct access to US-domiciled ETFs like VOO and QQQ. The question is not which workaround to use, but which broker offers the best combination of cost, access, and local regulatory comfort - a genuinely simpler starting position than the fund-domicile decisions covered elsewhere on this site for European investors.

---

## The Top Brokers Serving the Gulf Region

### 1. Interactive Brokers - Best for Larger Portfolios and Full Access

[Interactive Brokers](/best-brokers/interactive-brokers-review-2026-the-best-broker-for-international-investors/) is consistently rated the strongest option for Gulf-based investors investing seriously - low commissions, direct access to US-listed stocks and ETFs, and confirmed acceptance of UAE and Saudi Arabia residents for account opening. This is the natural default for anyone with a portfolio large enough that IBKR's cost advantages meaningfully compound, following the same fee-structure logic covered in our [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/).

### 2. eToro - Best for a Lower Barrier to Entry

eToro is regulated in the UAE through the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) and offers zero-commission ETF trading with a commonly cited $100 minimum deposit - a genuinely lower starting point than IBKR for a first account. Its simpler, app-first interface suits investors who want to start small and don't yet need the full depth of a more complex platform like IBKR's Trader Workstation.

### 3. Saxo Bank - Best for Institutional-Grade Research and Tools

Saxo Bank is available to both UAE and broader GCC residents and offers a significantly broader product range and stronger research tools than the app-first platforms, at a correspondingly higher cost structure - covered in more detail in our broader [best brokers guide](/best-brokers/best-brokers-international-investors-2026/). A reasonable choice for investors who want more than execution, and who value platform depth and research quality enough to accept a higher fee structure in exchange.

### 4. XTB - Best for Zero-Commission Trading

XTB also serves the region with zero-commission trading on stocks and ETFs, giving Gulf investors another genuinely low-cost option alongside eToro, subject to the same monthly volume threshold and FX conversion fee structure covered in our [zero-commission brokers comparison](/best-brokers/best-zero-commission-brokers-international-investors/).

## The One Genuine Advantage: No PRIIPs Restriction

Unlike investors in the EU, UK, or other jurisdictions covered on this site with a UCITS ETF workaround, Gulf-based investors can buy VOO, VTI, QQQ, and any other US-domiciled ETF directly - the same products available to a US-based investor, on the same terms. This simplifies broker selection considerably: the question is purely cost, platform quality, and account-opening ease, not product availability, removing an entire layer of decision-making (UCITS vs US-domiciled, covered in our [UCITS comparison guide](/index-funds/ucits-vs-us-domiciled-etfs-tax-comparison/)) that dominates broker selection for European investors specifically.

## What Doesn't Change: The Withholding Reality

Broker choice does not affect the underlying tax picture. UAE has no domestic income tax but also no US tax treaty, meaning the full 30% US dividend withholding applies with no reduction - covered in detail in our [UAE investing guide](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-uae/). Saudi Arabia follows a similar pattern - covered in our [Saudi Arabia guide](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-saudi-arabia/). None of the brokers above change this; it is a function of US treaty policy, not broker selection, and no amount of broker shopping will improve the withholding rate itself.

**Where broker choice does matter for the tax picture: dividend reporting accuracy and correct W-8BEN processing.** Even without a treaty rate to optimize toward, ensuring your broker correctly applies the standard 30% rate (rather than an erroneous, higher backup-withholding rate triggered by missing documentation) still matters - covered in our [backup withholding guide](/taxes/backup-withholding-explained/). A broker with a smooth, reliable documentation process avoids this specific, avoidable error even when there's no treaty benefit to chase.

## US Estate Tax Exposure - A Genuinely Relevant Consideration for This Region

**Given direct access to US-domiciled funds without a PRIIPs-driven push toward UCITS alternatives, Gulf investors face a genuine choice European investors don't: whether to voluntarily choose non-US-domiciled fund equivalents anyway, specifically for US estate tax mitigation purposes**, covered in detail throughout our [estate-planning category](/estate-planning/). Since neither the UAE nor Saudi Arabia appears on the [US estate tax treaty countries list](/estate-planning/us-estate-tax-treaty-countries/), the standard $60,000 non-resident alien exemption applies in full, without treaty relief - making the fund-domicile choice covered in our [avoiding US estate tax guide](/estate-planning/avoiding-us-estate-tax-brokerage-account/) a genuinely relevant consideration for Gulf investors building a substantial US-domiciled position, even though broker access itself isn't restricted the way it is in Europe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do any of these brokers offer Sharia-compliant account structures or investment screening?**
Some brokers and specific fund providers offer Sharia-compliant investment products or screening tools, though this varies by platform and isn't a standard feature across all four brokers covered in this guide - confirm directly with your chosen broker if this is a requirement for your investing approach.

**Is account opening documentation different for GCC residents compared to other international investors?**
The core documentation (government ID, proof of address, W-8BEN) generally follows the same standard process covered throughout this site's [multi-currency accounts guide](/us-investing/multi-currency-investment-accounts/), though specific requirements can vary by broker and by individual GCC country - confirm current requirements directly rather than assuming uniformity across the region.

**Does living in a GCC country with no income tax affect anything else covered on this site?**
Yes, indirectly - the [state tax exposure guide](/taxes/state-tax-exposure-non-resident-investors/) and other US-side tax considerations apply the same way regardless of your home country's own tax structure, though the absence of a foreign tax credit mechanism (relevant when a home country also taxes the same income) simplifies the tax picture somewhat for GCC residents specifically, since there's no home-country tax to reconcile against the US withholding.

**Should I choose a broker registered locally in the GCC, or is an internationally regulated broker just as good?**
This depends on your own comfort level and priorities - a locally registered broker (like eToro's ADGM registration) may offer additional local regulatory recourse or familiarity, while an internationally regulated broker like IBKR operates under different regulatory oversight; neither is inherently unsafe, but the choice is worth making deliberately rather than defaulting to one without considering the distinction.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements directly with your chosen broker, since eligibility and documentation requirements can change by country within the GCC
- [ ] File Form W-8BEN regardless of broker - it certifies your status and avoids backup withholding even without a treaty rate benefit
- [ ] Compare minimum deposit requirements if starting with a smaller position - eToro's lower minimum may suit a first account better than IBKR
- [ ] Confirm whether your specific broker holds local regulatory registration (like eToro's ADGM registration) versus operating under a passporting arrangement, if local regulatory standing matters to you
- [ ] Consider whether non-US-domiciled fund equivalents make sense for US estate tax mitigation, even though direct US-domiciled access isn't restricted the way it is in Europe
