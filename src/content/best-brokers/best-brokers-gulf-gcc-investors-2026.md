Gulf and GCC-based investors - across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and the wider region - have one genuine advantage most investors on this site don't: no EU-style PRIIPs restriction blocking direct access to US-domiciled ETFs like VOO and QQQ. The question is not which workaround to use, but which broker offers the best combination of cost, access, and local regulatory comfort.

---

## The Top Brokers Serving the Gulf Region

### 1. Interactive Brokers - Best for Larger Portfolios and Full Access

[Interactive Brokers](/best-brokers/interactive-brokers-review-2026-the-best-broker-for-international-investors/) is consistently rated the strongest option for Gulf-based investors investing seriously - low commissions, direct access to US-listed stocks and ETFs, and confirmed acceptance of UAE and Saudi Arabia residents for account opening. This is the natural default for anyone with a portfolio large enough that IBKR's cost advantages meaningfully compound.

### 2. eToro - Best for a Lower Barrier to Entry

eToro is regulated in the UAE through the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) and offers zero-commission ETF trading with a commonly cited $100 minimum deposit - a genuinely lower starting point than IBKR for a first account. Its simpler, app-first interface suits investors who want to start small.

### 3. Saxo Bank - Best for Institutional-Grade Research and Tools

Saxo Bank is available to both UAE and broader GCC residents and offers a significantly broader product range and stronger research tools than the app-first platforms, at a correspondingly higher cost structure. A reasonable choice for investors who want more than execution.

### 4. XTB - Best for Zero-Commission Trading

XTB also serves the region with zero-commission trading on stocks and ETFs, giving Gulf investors another genuinely low-cost option alongside eToro.

---

## The One Genuine Advantage: No PRIIPs Restriction

Unlike investors in the EU, UK, or other jurisdictions covered on this site with a UCITS ETF workaround, Gulf-based investors can buy VOO, VTI, QQQ, and any other US-domiciled ETF directly - the same products available to a US-based investor, on the same terms. This simplifies broker selection considerably: the question is purely cost, platform quality, and account-opening ease, not product availability.

---

## What Doesn't Change: The Withholding Reality

Broker choice does not affect the underlying tax picture. UAE has no domestic income tax but also no US tax treaty, meaning the full 30% US dividend withholding applies with no reduction - covered in detail in our [UAE investing guide](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-uae/). Saudi Arabia follows a similar pattern - covered in our [Saudi Arabia guide](/us-investing/how-to-invest-in-us-stocks-from-saudi-arabia/). None of the brokers above change this; it is a function of US treaty policy, not broker selection.

## Practical Checklist

- [ ] Confirm current account-opening requirements directly with your chosen broker, since eligibility and documentation requirements can change by country within the GCC
- [ ] File Form W-8BEN regardless of broker - it certifies your status and avoids backup withholding even without a treaty rate benefit
- [ ] Compare minimum deposit requirements if starting with a smaller position - eToro's lower minimum may suit a first account better than IBKR
- [ ] Confirm whether your specific broker holds local regulatory registration (like eToro's ADGM registration) versus operating under a passporting arrangement, if local regulatory standing matters to you

---

## Bottom Line

Interactive Brokers, eToro, Saxo Bank, and XTB all give Gulf-region investors genuine, direct access to US markets - the real decision is cost and platform preference, not product availability, which is a simpler starting point than most investors covered on this site have. IBKR suits larger portfolios and serious investors; eToro suits a lower-cost first account; Saxo suits investors who want deeper research tools.
