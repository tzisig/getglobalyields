# Fact-Check and Final Review

## Fact-check pass (Stage 7)

Go through the final draft claim by claim. For each factual statement:

1. Is this actually a factual claim (vs. opinion, interpretation, or
   recommendation)? Keep those categories visibly distinct in the text.
2. Does it have a real source from the research done in Stage 3?
3. Is that source reliable (see source priority in constitution.md)?
4. Does the wording in the article accurately represent what the source said —
   no rounding a "some studies suggest" into "research proves," no dropping a
   caveat the source included?

Specifically re-verify: dates, numbers/rates/fees, names, statistics, anything
about regulation or tax treatment, and any quote.

If a statement fails any of these: remove it, or rewrite it as an explicitly
marked uncertainty. Do not smooth it into vaguer language that sounds safe while
still implying the same unverified claim.

## Final review (Stage 8)

Before calling the article done, check all of these:

- **Truth** — nothing stated as fact that isn't Verified or Supported
  (constitution.md).
- **Evidence** — every load-bearing claim has a traceable source.
- **Originality** — this isn't a template restatement of existing top-ranking
  content; it has its own angle.
- **Voice** — matches writing-dna.md, not generic AI-neutral prose.
- **Humanization** — the Stage 6 checklist has actually been applied, not just
  skipped because the draft "seemed fine."
- **SEO** — natural keyword usage, no stuffing.
- **Readability** — a reader unfamiliar with the topic can follow it; a reader
  who knows the topic doesn't find anything oversimplified or wrong.
- **Formatting** — plain hyphens only, no decorative excess, lists used only
  where they're genuinely a list.
- **Frontmatter** — the full required frontmatter block (see SKILL.md) is
  present and every field is actually filled in, including `seo.metaTitle` /
  `seo.metaDescription`, not left blank or templated.

Closing test: would someone who actually knows this topic be willing to publish
this under their name? If the honest answer is no, say specifically what's still
wrong — don't just say "looks good" and hand it over. Fix the specific problem,
then re-check.
