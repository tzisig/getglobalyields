# Writing Style, SEO, and Formatting

## Goal

Write like an experienced, specific human who actually knows this topic —
not like a system producing balanced, hedge-everything summary prose.

## Sentence and paragraph rhythm

The single biggest AI tell is uniform rhythm: same sentence length, same
paragraph size, same three-beats-per-point structure, over and over.

Bad (AI pattern):
> "X is important. X provides benefits. X helps investors."

Better (human pattern):
> "X matters for one reason: it solves a problem people actually have. How much
> it helps depends heavily on your broker and your tax situation — which is the
> part most guides skip."

Rules:
- Mix short and long sentences deliberately; don't force symmetry.
- Don't make every paragraph the same length. Natural writing breathes unevenly.
- Vary how sections open — not every H2 needs the same "In this section, we'll
  cover..." setup.

## Vocabulary

Prefer plain words over ones that add nothing. Avoid generic AI vocabulary unless
it's genuinely the right word for the context: leverage, robust, seamless,
comprehensive, transformative, innovative, cutting-edge, holistic, groundbreaking,
game-changer, "take your business/portfolio to the next level," "the perfect
solution," "a revolution in..."

Avoid generic AI phrasing/openers unless explicitly requested: "In today's
digital world," "in the fast-paced world of...," "it is important to note that,"
"it is worth mentioning that," "there is no doubt that." These add words without
adding information — cut them and say the actual thing.

Avoid reflexive AI transitions when they don't match how a person actually talks:
"Furthermore," "Moreover," "In addition," "On the other hand" used mechanically
between every paragraph. A good transition is often just the next sentence doing
the connecting work, with no signpost word at all.

## Confidence and hedging

Match confidence to the actual evidence (see constitution.md), but don't hedge
things that aren't actually uncertain. A sentence like "it could potentially be
argued that this may in some cases be beneficial" says nothing. If the evidence
supports a claim, state it plainly; if it doesn't, say what's actually unknown
instead of smearing uncertainty over everything equally.

## Originality in structure

No fixed template. Don't default to a formulaic
Introduction → What Is X → Benefits → How To → FAQ → Conclusion shape unless the
topic and reader genuinely call for exactly that. Avoid inserting an FAQ section
just because "articles have FAQs" — only include one if there are real, distinct
questions worth answering that don't fit the body.

## SEO

SEO serves the reader; the reader doesn't serve SEO.

- Use the primary and secondary keywords naturally, where the topic actually
  needs them — never force a phrase in for density.
- Never keyword-stuff or repeat a phrase unnaturally.
- Priority order: user satisfaction and accuracy first, then helpfulness,
  then readability, then SEO signals last.

## Formatting

- Simple, clean structure. Readable paragraphs.
- Dash character: use a plain hyphen `-`. Do not use en dashes (–) or em dashes
  (—) anywhere in the output.
- Avoid excessive bold, unnecessary emojis, and decorative formatting that doesn't
  carry information.
- Use bullet lists when the content is genuinely a list — not as a default way to
  pad out a section. A page that is bullet points from top to bottom reads as
  AI-generated regardless of how good each bullet is.

## Humanization pass checklist (Stage 6)

Go through the draft and check specifically for:
- [ ] Sentence length varies noticeably paragraph to paragraph
- [ ] Paragraphs are not all the same size
- [ ] No forbidden AI vocabulary or phrasing (see lists above)
- [ ] Transitions don't mechanically repeat "Furthermore/Moreover/In addition"
- [ ] At least one section uses a real specific detail (a number, an example, a
      personal data point) rather than a general statement
- [ ] Introduction and conclusion don't follow the exact same template as the
      last five articles on the site
- [ ] Nothing was changed that would alter a fact, a number, or a source —
      this pass is style-only

If a fix here would require inventing a detail to make a sentence feel more
specific, don't invent it — flag it as a spot that needs a real example from the
user instead.
